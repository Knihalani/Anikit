# EVERY FRAME: flagship launch film for Anikit Video

72 s · 1920×1080 · 60 fps · one continuous camera move · H.264 + AAC from a single spec

## 1. Logline

Six people ask for six completely different films in plain words. One machine turns each request into
thousands of glass frames, laid across a bright studio floor like film track. Then we run alongside the
tracks and every film comes alive: a chrome product launch, an editorial fashion piece, a data explainer,
a lyric video, a raymarched sculpture, a sports broadcast open. You come away understanding that a video
is just frames, that Anikit Video makes them from words, and that it can make *anything*, in any style,
at the top end.

## 2. Concept

**Video is a strip of glass frames, and we run alongside it.**

The whole film is one unbroken camera journey through a single bright, physical place: an infinite white
photo studio (a seamless cyclorama). Every frame of a video is a real object here, a thin pane of glass with
a picture printed on it. That one idea carries the whole story and needs no prior knowledge.

- **Beginning (the requests).** One glass pane hangs in the white studio with a request in plain words:
  *"A launch film for Sundial. Chrome and glass, golden hour."* The camera pulls back: five more panes hang
  behind it, each a wildly different request (an editorial fashion film, a data explainer, a lyric video, an
  abstract sculpture, a sports open). They turn over together: on each back is the plan an agent wrote, a
  short spec. One after another they drop, like bread into a toaster, into the six slots of a long
  aluminium machine on the floor.
- **Middle (the frames).** The machine's lights blink and it rolls forward, leaving six panes standing where
  the plans went in: frame 1 of six films, side by side, six art directions. Then it accelerates away towards
  the horizon, laying six parallel strips of frames behind it like track: a highway of films, a torrent of
  glass. (Speed is shown, never quoted.) The camera dives to the first strip and accelerates alongside it
  until it passes exactly one pane per frame of video. At that speed the panes lock still on screen and the
  film **plays**, like film running through a projector gate. Then, without slowing, the camera hops the
  strip and drops in beside the next one, and the next: six films, six worlds, one run. The frames *are*
  the videos, and anything you can describe can be one.
- **End (the files).** The camera brakes. All six strips fold up like accordions, rushing in from the
  horizon, and crush down into six small glass tiles, the MP4s, etched with their names. The tiles rise and
  turn to face us, each playing its film, a floating row of six screens over the white floor. The camera
  pulls through them to chrome **Anikit Video** standing on the floor: *Words in. Film out.*

No numbers, no benchmarks, no slides. Every caption is a physical object or etched type that lives in the
studio and gets passed by the camera. The film never cuts; transitions are camera moves.

## 3. Look

### The studio ("daylight lab")

Bright, clean, physical: the opposite of every dark-purple glow film before it. The studio is neutral so
that the six films carry all the colour.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Set** | Infinite warm-white cyclorama: the floor curves up into the wall with no horizon line. A faint 1 m floor grid etched at 6% contrast, visible only at grazing angles. Gentle exponential depth haze to warm white, so the strips dissolve into the distance. |
| **Palette** | Studio white `#EEEBE5` · shadow taupe `#B9B2A7` · ink `#15171A` (type, machine details) · **signal orange `#FF5A1F`** (the machine's six LEDs and the floor rail of each strip, the only studio accent) · glass-edge green `#9FD8C8` (the natural tint of thick glass edges). |
| **Materials** | Frames: 8 mm low-iron glass with rounded corners, the picture printed on the back face (refraction shows through the thickness), polished green-tinted edges catching the key light. Machine: a long, low, bead-blasted aluminium gantry with chamfered edges, six black glass slots and six orange LEDs. Final MP4 tiles: thick optical glass with the frames visible as faint strata inside. Logo: polished chrome on the floor, reflecting the cyc. |
| **Light** | One huge soft key from top-left (a 6 m softbox), a warm bounce from the floor, a thin rim from behind for the glass edges. Soft contact shadows under every pane; bright edges where glass meets light. A real studio reflection map in chrome and glass. |
| **Camera** | Real lens behaviour: 35 mm for travelling, 60–85 mm for the macro moments, shallow DOF (focus pulls are part of the storytelling), motion blur on fast moves, and a shutter that closes as the camera locks to the strip. | **References** | Apple product films in white studios (weight, clean light); Teenage Engineering and Braun product photography (the machine); Muybridge and the zoetrope (frames becoming motion); Stripe Sessions stage design. |

each film is built to peak inside its window. Its first frame is also a designed hero still, because all six
first frames stand side by side in shot 3.

## 5. Sound

Synthesised score (numpy additive + FM, mixed to AAC), 120 BPM, A minor → C major. Every move lands on a beat.

| Time | Bar | Music | Sync points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 1 | Room tone, a soft felt-piano A, a high glassy shimmer pad. | Focus rack on beat 3; a soft glass tone as each of the six panes appears in the pull-back. |
| 0:07 | 4.3 | A second piano voice; a low sub. | The six flips are a rising ripple of glass "tinks"; plan lines tick on 8ths. |
| 0:12 | 7 | Pulse bass (8ths). | Six slot clunks, one per beat (0:12.5–0:15.0); the LEDs click; the machine rolls on 0:16 with a bright chord. |
| 0:18 | 10 | Kick on 1 and 3; ticks per frame accelerate into a pitched whir rising two octaves. | The whir peaks as the machine vanishes over the horizon at 0:25.5, then a sub drop. |

## 6. Claims

None. The film makes no numeric or comparative claims: no render times, no benchmarks, no counters. Speed is
shown only visually (the torrent of frames); quality and range are shown by the six films themselves.

## 7. What the engine has to provide (for 3D, SPEC, RENDER, SHADER)

1. `scene-3d` with instanced thin glass slabs (6 lanes × 1,800 = 10,800), each textured with its lane's
   composition at a per-instance time offset (frame *j*), with LOD for distant panes.
2. A camera along keys, with explicit velocities (the run is exactly linear in X at 102 m/s), per-key lens,
   focus and aperture, DOF, and motion blur with a per-key shutter (near 0 during the lock).
3. Glass (transmission, refraction through thickness, tinted edges), aluminium, chrome, emissive; a cyc
   environment, soft contact shadows, exponential haze, and floor reflections for shot 7.
4. Extruded 3D type standing in the world; text and UI on planes (requests, plans, labels).
5. A glTF or primitive phone for Sundial; a GLSL layer for **Fold** (SHADER).
6. Post: a light bloom on the LEDs and rails, subtle grain, no heavy glow.
