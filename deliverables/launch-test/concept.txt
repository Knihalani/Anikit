Tern: launch film concept (20 s, 16:9)

Noun: birdsong, the spectrogram (a picture of sound), the migration map.  Verb: listens, names, maps.
Before -> after: an unseen singer in the dark -> a named bird -> one dot among thousands moving north.
Motif: the song trace. One event list drives BOTH the synthesised birdsong and the GLSL spectrogram,
  so every syllable you hear is the syllable you see. The trace is named on the phone, becomes a dot on
  the map, and at the end a tern's two-note call ("kee-arr", an up-slur into a down-slur) draws a ^ on the
  spectrogram: the wingbeat that becomes the logo mark.
Structure: sound before light. 0-2.5 s full-frame live spectrogram, no context (a wood thrush sings);
  pull back: it is Tern's listening screen; four species named as they sing (thrush, robin, cardinal,
  sparrow); cut to the map: this morning's IDs at one spot, pull out to spring arrivals rolling north,
  flyways across the Gulf; a tern calls, its call draws the tern, the tern is the name.
World and look: pre-dawn ink (#061114) and teal noise floor, the song in warm amber-to-white, Inter Display
  for words, JetBrains Mono for instrument readouts. 2D: UI, shader, cartography. No 3D (nothing physical here).
Trend check: avoided cyc/chrome/tiles/terrain/cream-paper editorial; closest risk is "dark data dashboard",
  countered by an authentic spectrogram and a real map with honest seasonal data shapes.
Music: acoustic, 96 BPM in D (bar = 2.5 s): strings pad, harp, cello, nylon guitar, piano, brushes;
  the birds are the melody. Hits at bar 5 (map) and bar 7 (the tern's call). Intent: steady.
Honesty: no stats, users or partners invented. Map dots are an illustrative demo of the feature;
  ranges follow the wood thrush's real winter range (S Mexico-Panama) and eastern breeding range.
