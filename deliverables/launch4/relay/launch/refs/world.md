# The subject's world: people who live in their inbox

(Reasoned from common knowledge of email culture; no web research used. Sure = widely known convention; guess = plausible but unverified.)

**Who:** founders, managers, sales/ops leads, anyone whose job arrives by email. Ritual: the first thing in the morning is the phone in bed, thumbing the inbox before getting up (sure). The dread of the unread badge (sure). Batch-processing "email hour" (sure).

**Insider nouns and strings (sure):**
- Subject-line archaeology: `Re: Re: Fwd:`; the reply-all storm where 40 people reply "please remove me from this list".
- Openers/closers: "Just circling back", "Per my last email", "Quick question", "Looping in", "Moving you to bcc", "Sent from my iPhone", "Best,", "Thanks!".
- Calendar back-and-forth: "Does Thursday work?", "What about 2:30?", "Can we push to next week?".
- Receipts, newsletters, "Your order has shipped", invoices "INV-…", "Action required".
- Time stamps in inbox lists ("2:14 AM") — the tell of work happening at night; the time-zone colleague who sends at 3 a.m. (sure).
- Unread count badge; "Inbox Zero" as an aspiration (sure).
- Drafts folder; the "Send" button; "Undo send" (sure).

**Moments only insiders know:** the 11:58 p.m. "small thing before tomorrow" email from the boss; the overnight reply-all storm you wake up to; the email you've "been meaning to reply to" for a week; writing "Sounds good!" ten times a morning (sure/guess mix).

**What the product's material looks like:** text. Inbox rows (sender, subject, snippet, time), a brief (sections, bullets), a draft reply under each thread, an Approve/Send button. That's the whole material world: rows, cards, time stamps, a send button.

**Places:** a bed at night, a phone at dawn — which we will NOT simulate. Night is carried by the clock and the light of the ground only.
