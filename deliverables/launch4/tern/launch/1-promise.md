# 1 · Promise, truth, moment

- **Unownable truth:** most recorders make you choose before the sound happens: press record in time, set the gain right. Tern removes both choices. It is already rolling when you lift it, and its 32-bit float files keep a sound that would have clipped. So the classic failures that fill a recordist's notes, *missed the start* and *clipped*, stop happening.
- **Promise (≤ 15 words, no name):** Every take is the keeper, even the one you weren't ready for.
- **Shareable moment (≤ 15 words):** On a sound report full of NG takes, the pen starts circling every one.
- **Target emotion:** amusement turning to awe. Every recordist recognises "FS – missed it", and the escalation (it circles *everything*, up to a thunderclap) lands the awe.
- **Send test:** a production sound mixer DMs it to their boom op or a sound-design friend: "this is literally our sound report 😂". The sender looks like an insider, because the joke is in the jargon (circle takes, FS, NG).
- **Hostile retell:** "A spreadsheet with handwriting on it for a mic." It's unflattering but not damning. It's a design film, not a fake. Guard: the form must look like a real, beautifully printed object and move with rhythm, never like a slide.
- **Not in this film:** "pocket-sized" (only implied by the copy, not a beat) and "for musicians and sound designers" (carried by the takes' subjects, not stated). The four built-in mics appear as the form's four track columns, which are part of the world, not a feature beat. 30-hour battery gets one row only, as the last escalation ("roll 1 still rolling"), not a feature card.
