# The recordist's world

Who: production sound mixers and boom ops on set; field recordists (wildlife, ambiences, Foley gatherers); musicians catching ideas, rehearsals and room sound; sound designers building libraries.

**The sound report (high confidence).** On every shoot the mixer fills in a *sound report*, a printed multi-part carbonless form. The top copy is white, then yellow, then pink. One copy travels with the media to post, one goes to production, and the mixer keeps one. The header has fields for Production, Date, Roll / Sound Roll, Mixer, **Recorder** (the machine's make), Sample rate / Bit depth, Timecode / FPS. The body has one row per take: Scene · Take · Track columns (Tr 1 … Tr 4, each labelled with the mic: "Boom", "Lav A") · Notes.
- **Circle takes:** the takes the director wants printed are *circled* on the report. A circled take is a keeper. (high confidence)
- **Abbreviations in the Notes column:** **NG** no good, **FS** false start, **INC** incomplete, **WT** wild track, **RT** room tone, **MOS** without sound, **PU** pickup. (high confidence)
- Recordists also write "clipped", "distorted", "wind", "missed top", "batt" (battery). (confident; common field-note language, though spellings vary)
- Written in ballpoint, often blue, pressed hard enough to go through three copies; the copy beneath shows a softer, slightly offset carbon impression. (high confidence)

**Field recording rituals (confident).** Slating a take by voice ("Roll 3, take 12, rain on tin roof"). The "one that got away": the bird call or thunderclap that happened while you were unzipping the bag. Riding the gain on a loud event. Pre-roll / pre-record buffers exist on pro recorders because missing the onset is the most common loss.

**Nouns and verbs:** roll, take, slate, circle, NG, keeper, the top, the tail, peak, headroom, clip, gain, wild track, room tone, the bag, the windjammer ("dead cat"), "rolling", "speed" (the mixer's call that the recorder is running).

**What Tern changes on that page (from the brief only):** no FS/"missed top", because it starts on the lift; no "clipped", because files are 32-bit float; no "batt", because of 30 h of battery; four tracks from four built-in mics.
