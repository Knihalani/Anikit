# References: the moves, not the look

Frame sheets: `refs/sheets/` (judge.py refsheet, tags product-launch, kinetic-type, brand-idents).

1. **Gretel × Mode:** one named behaviour governs every layout. *Steal:* one behaviour, the **circle**, governs every frame. It is the only gesture that "happens".
2. **Pentagram × TwelveLabs:** diagrams of how the product works, no idle loops. *Steal:* nothing moves unless a sound or the pen causes it.
3. **Collins × Dropbox:** one signature motion applied to every static element. *Steal:* the pen stroke's arc is the transition, the reveal and the ending.
4. **Instrument × Dropbox:** real customers' real files as the hero. *Steal:* the hero is the user's own artefact (their paperwork), shot in macro.
5. **Koto × Mews:** one typeface, extreme scale, low/mid/high intensity tiers. *Steal:* Archivo at three widths and three sizes, going from a form label to full-frame.
6. **Apple "Don't Blink":** type at speed, cut to the beat. *Steal:* the escalation section rattles through rows on the beat.
7. **Outside motion:** NCR carbonless business forms (printed rules in a single spot colour, perforations, copy labels "WHITE – POST / YELLOW – PRODUCTION / PINK – MIXER"); Wolfgang Weingart's and Experimental Jetset's use of forms and rules as typography; field notebooks (Rite in the Rain).

**What the good ones do that ours usually don't:** one gesture with a cause; real artefacts in macro; strict type at extreme scale contrast; restraint in colour.

**The category cliché to avoid:** recorder launch = a waveform, a mic on a sweep, a forest at dawn with headphones, a "VU meter going red". Also our own last film (a landscape as a waveform).

**Closest reference:** Gretel × Mode (one behaviour governs all). It differs in being a document world with a human hand and an insider joke, not an abstract identity system.

**From the frame sheets (`05-halt-and-catch-fire`, `01-vertigo`):** one saturated field colour owns the whole frame, a tactile print or scan texture, and type small and confident inside a big image. *Steal:* fill the frame with the coloured carbon copy itself (pink and yellow stock), macro, with a single ink.
