# 2 · Ideas

1. **Circle takes** (family: a production paperwork craft). A real multi-part sound report fills itself in. Every take row so far carries a recordist's excuse (*FS – missed the top*, *NG – clipped*, *INC – batt*). From one row on, the pen stops writing excuses and circles: the bird before you were ready, the slammed door, the thunderclap, an all-night roll. Ends with the pen filling the header field **Recorder: Tern**.
   - Insider details: circle takes, NG/FS/INC/WT, three-part NCR copies, the "Recorder" field, "speed".
   - Reference move: Gretel × Mode (one behaviour governs everything); Instrument × Dropbox (the user's own artefact as the hero).
   - Literal test (delete "Tern" and its mechanism): "a sound report where every take becomes a keeper" survives. It's about the recordist, not 32-bit maths.
   - Swap test: with Zoom or Tascam on it, the "FS – missed the top" row has no answer, because only lift-to-record removes it. Clipping alone would be swappable; the pairing isn't. **Pass.**
   - Renderable: a printed document in macro plus ink. **Pass.** Fresh: no previous film is a paper form. **Pass.** Proof inside the moment: delete the product, and nothing explains why the excuses vanish. **Pass.**
2. **The one that got away** (family: fishing and the angler's tall tale). Fishing logbooks, "the one that got away" sized with outstretched hands. The sound that escaped grows bigger in the retelling until Tern "lands" it. Literal: survives. Swap: weak. Renderable: illustrations of anglers or fish slide towards simulation. **Rejected.**
3. **Seismograph stations** (family: a scientific instrument). A drum recorder that never stops; an earthquake pushes the stylus off the paper. Literal: it's the 32-bit float mechanism drawn as a picture. **Rejected (literal).**
4. **Birder's tally** (family: birdwatching). A checklist of species ticked by ear. "Tern" is also a bird, so this is the product's name drawn. **Rejected (pun).**
5. **(Out there) Score with no rests** (family: music notation). A conductor's score where every bar of silence (*tacet*) fills with whatever the room played: chairs, coughs, rain. A lovely idea for "always listening", but in a launch it reads as a surveillance joke, and swapping "always on" mics is a privacy turn-off. **Rejected (hostile retell: "the recorder that never stops listening to you").**

## Pick: 1, Circle takes

- **Device:** the pen on the sound report. What used to be written as an excuse is now circled.
- **Shareable moment:** the pen starts circling every take, even the ones that used to be NG.
- **Escalation (5 beats):**
  1. Hook: macro on the pink copy, the pen writing "FS – missed the top" under a bird take. (The problem, in the user's own handwriting.)
  2. The page so far: a column of excuses (clipped, batt, missed it) scrolls past, hand-written and pressed through the carbon.
  3. The turn: "Recorder:" is still blank. A hard stop: silence, the pen lifts. *Lift. It's rolling.*
  4. Circles: the next takes get circled on the beat. Bird at dawn ○, slammed door ○, thunderclap ○ (the circle is drawn so hard that it tears the carbon: the loudest take still only a circle), roll 1 "still rolling, 30 h" ○.
  5. Scale: pull back. The whole page is circles, then three copies fan out (white, yellow, pink).
- **Ending:** the pen writes **Tern** in the "Recorder" field; it becomes the logotype at full scale with the one-line promise.
- **Why it won:** it's the only idea where the joke is an insider truth and the proof is the punchline. It escalates by count and loudness. It has one material world (carbon paper and ballpoint) and a built-in ending (the Recorder field).
