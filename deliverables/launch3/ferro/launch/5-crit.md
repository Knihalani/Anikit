# 5. Crit 1 (judge.py --practice, tags product-launch,cg-3d; refs: wealthsimple-trade, tiffany-holiday, da-vincis-demons)

**Overall 4.12** (ref mean 6.0, delta -1.88), judged professional 0/2, mean rank 3; full (with critic) 4.39.
Ranking: trial 0 A(da-vinci) > D(tiffany) > **C(us)** > B(wealthsimple); trial 1 A > C(tiffany) > **D(us)** > B.

Scores: idea 4.0, composition 5.0, typography 3.0, colour 5.5, light_texture 4.0, motion 5.0, craft 3.0, originality 3.5

## Gaps (verbatim) → kind
- **IDEA** — Make the product truth visible: show the weight (a balance, a flip, a single-finger lift, slow-motion physics) instead of writing it on the floor
- **WORLD** — Invest in materials and food: realistic seasoned cast iron, translucent egg white, believable ingredients, sizzle and steam
- **CRAFT** — Give the type a point of view: a custom or carefully chosen wordmark integrated into the scene's light and perspective, not default bold italic sans
- **IDEA** — Dramatise the one claim, lightness, with a specific visual idea (balance, float, counterweight) instead of generic falling ingredients
- **WORLD** — Give the pan and food real material: seasoned-iron sheen, oil, egg translucency, steam. Light them with intent rather than a stock gobo
- **CRAFT** — Replace default bold-sans-with-shadow with a typographic system tied to the brand and integrated into the space

## Tells (verbatim) → kind
- **IDEA** — The idea is literal: pan, ingredients drop in, tagline, logo, 'Back it on Kickstarter'. The claim 'half the weight' is stated in text and never demonstrated visually
- **WORLD** — Simple 3D modelling: the eggs, tomatoes and mushrooms are primitive discs and spheres, and the pan has no cast-iron material detail (no seasoning, grain or specular character)
- **CRAFT** — Default heavy-italic grotesk logo and generic bold sans tagline, laid flat on the floor like a slide, with a drop-shadow CTA
- **WORLD** — A single orange room with a window-grid shadow is the only lighting idea, repeated in every frame; the shadow is pleasant, but the light is otherwise flat and the materials are untextured
- **—** — Square social format and a Kickstarter end card point to a small-budget or self-produced piece
- **WORLD** — An empty orange room with a window-grid gobo shadow is a stock 3D-tutorial set-up. The light is a cliché, not motivated by any story
- **WORLD** — Food elements are low-detail primitives (flat egg discs, sphere tomatoes, untextured mushrooms) with no material credibility, the opposite of what a cookware product needs
- **CRAFT** — Typography is a default heavy grotesk ('FERRO', 'Cast iron, half the weight.') with a generic drop shadow, plus a centred 'Back it on Kickstarter' line set like a slide
- **IDEA** — The idea is literal: ingredients fall into the pan and the product name appears. Nothing visualises the actual claim ('half the weight')
- **(positive)** — Motion does show some intent (toss, fall, settle, pull-back to wide), which lifts it above pure automation

## Critic (motion/sound)
- The 6.0 s shot holds without a clear reason. Motion is busiest at 1–3 s and then sinks to 6–12 per second through the middle, so energy peaks early and goes slack.
- The ingredients fall with almost no motion blur. In the pairs they move like evenly spaced CG props with no squash, settle or weight on landing, and the pancake drops into the pan with no impact.
- Frame 3 is a wide shot with the pan tiny in the corner. It has no subject and reads as dead air.
- 'Cast iron, half the weight.' lies flat on the floor in perspective and is partly hidden by falling food. It is hard to read, and the type does nothing with the claim.
- The food models are plain and plasticky: smooth spheres for tomatoes, flat discs for eggs, and no specular variety. The pan has no cast-iron texture or seasoning sheen, so the product looks like generic black plastic.
- The end card mixes a 3D extruded 'FERRO' with a flat 2D overlay line, and the two sit in different lighting and depth.
- The pan's position and angle change from shot to shot with no match cuts, so the edit reads as separate renders rather than one continuous choreography.
- The soundtrack is a steady loop of evenly spaced transients with almost no change in dynamics. There is no build, no drop that lines up with the brand reveal, and no foley for the sizzle, clank or landings.

## Verdict
- **IDEA gaps exist** ("literal", "the claim 'half the weight' is stated in text and never demonstrated", "generic falling ingredients"). Rule 1: replace the idea from a different family — not polish the toss. The jury itself names the family it wanted to see: *balance / counterweight*.
- **WORLD:** primitive food, untextured-looking iron, the stock orange room with a window gobo. → pass 2 drops the food entirely (it was the least convincing modelling), changes the world to bare concrete + a brass balance + two kinds of iron, and lights it cool.
- **CRAFT:** default bold sans flat on the floor; 2D drop-shadow CTA. → pass 2 stamps all type onto the balance itself (beam face and plinth) in a slab face, no 2D overlay at all.
- **Weakest third cut:** the egg hop, the pancake flip, the wide crane with tiny food (all of the food beats).

# Crit 2 (pass 2: the balance; same tags and refs)

**Overall 3.12** (ref mean 6.23, delta -3.11), judged professional 0/2, mean rank 3; full 3.33.
Ranking: trial 0 D(tiffany) > A(da-vinci) > **C(us)** > B; trial 1 A > C(tiffany) > **D(us)** > B.
Scores: idea 4.0, composition 3.0, typography 2.0, colour 3.0, light_texture 4.0, motion 3.0, craft 3.0, originality 3.0

## Gaps (verbatim)
- **IDEA** — No idea beyond the literal diagram. It needs a memorable metaphor for lightness that only cast iron could carry
- **WORLD** — Material and lighting craft: real iron patina, seasoning sheen and motivated light, not a grey sweep with one gobo
- **CRAFT** — Typography needs a system (a chosen face, hierarchy, integration with the image), not extruded display text and labels on the props
- **IDEA** — It needs an idea beyond the diagram, a way to make lightness felt (a pan that floats, a wrist test, real cooking), rather than a balance scale
- **WORLD** — Real material craft is missing: seasoned iron texture, oil sheen, food and steam, and lighting that sells weight and heat
- **CRAFT** — The type needs a point of view, a chosen or custom face integrated with the image and with scale contrast, not default slab caps glued to the props

## Tells (verbatim)
- The idea is literal and obvious: a balance scale with two pans to say 'half the weight'
- Default-looking Blender scene: a grey sweep backdrop, a single window-gobo light pattern (stock HDRI/cookie), untextured mustard-gold plastic material and a low-poly pan
- Typography is a generic heavy slab/grunge display face extruded as 3D text ('FE' builds to 'FERRO'), with tiny unreadable 'HALF THE WEIGHT' on the beam and 'BACK IT ON KICKSTARTER' slapped onto the base
- The same object is orbited from a few angles. Little happens besides a camera move and a tilt of the scale
- The defocused foreground in the first frame looks like accidental depth of field, not a designed shot
- A literal, obvious idea: two pans on a balance scale to say 'half the weight'
- Typography is a default chunky slab in black, set flat on the beam, the base and as floating extruded letters 'FE'/'FERRO'; there's no hierarchy or craft, and 'BACK IT ON KICKSTARTER' is set like a caption
- Stock 3D setup: a grey cyclorama, a window-gobo shadow on the floor and plain gold and black materials, with no surface detail on the cast iron
- The camera just orbits one prop; the last two frames repeat the same frontal composition
- Muddy, low-contrast palette with flat grey dominating

## Decision
- Pass 1 (the toss) 4.12 vs pass 2 (the balance) 3.12: a 1.0 difference, above the 0.75 tie threshold. **Pass 1 ships** as final.mp4.
- What we learned: the balance answered crit 1's literal ask ("show the weight") and the jury called it *more* literal ("the literal diagram"). Crit 2 points back toward pass 1's family ("a pan that floats, a wrist test, real cooking"). The bigger losses in pass 2 were the grey sweep, the muddy palette and small type on the props, so the world was the main failure. Pass 1's terracotta world held up better.
- No third pass: the next one would need a new idea family and a full rebuild of materials (seasoned iron detail, credible food, steam). Recorded for the next Ferro film: keep the toss, and spend the effort on food and iron at full fidelity rather than on a new device.
- launch/pass1.mp4 is identical to final.mp4 and was removed to save space.
