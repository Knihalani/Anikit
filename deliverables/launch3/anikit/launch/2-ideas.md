# 2 · Four ideas

Previous Anikit launch films used a player-piano roll (light through punched paper) and a darkroom tray (prints developing). Both were paper-on-dark worlds. This film must leave that family: daylight, outdoors, many bodies, no paper as the main material.

1. **The drill chart (marching band).** A one-page drill chart — dots, counts, arrows — is performed by a 144-piece marching band on a sunlit football field. Every player hits every dot on every count. Played on Tuesday afternoon and Saturday night, the show is identical to the step. The band finally drills into the name.
   - Literal test: delete the name and "JSON → MP4": "a page of dots becomes a stadium show, performed identically every night". Survives — it's a world (turf, sun, brass), not a diagram of the product.
   - Swap test: a generative-video brand (Sora, Runway, Veo) can't claim "the same show, to the step, every time"; a generic agent platform doesn't make picture and sound. Fails for competitors → passes.
2. **The greenhouse seed.** One seed packet, the same seed planted twice, two identical trees leaf for leaf. Literal: survives. Swap: weaker — growth reads "AI grows your business", and a plant takes the whole film to say "finished".
3. **The cue sheet (theatre).** A stage manager's cue script; the house runs the show: lights, flies, rain, pit. Literal: survives. Swap: ok. But it is a dark stage with a followspot — the rejected "dark ground with a glow" look, and ray-traced velvet/flies blow the budget.
4. **(Out there) The weather order.** A typed forecast — "rain at 0:04, thunder on the downbeat" — and the sky obeys to the second. Literal: survives. Swap: ok. But skies are a fog/terrain look already rejected, and the device can't escalate beyond "more weather".

## Pick: 1 · The drill chart
- **Device (one sentence):** a page of dots is performed by a whole marching band, and every player hits every mark, every time.
- **Escalation:** (1) dots plot onto a page on the snare → (2) a rank steps onto its marks on the downbeat → (3) the whole block flows into a turning figure → (4) the colour guard flips silks and the field becomes a picture → (5) two nights, one seam: the show in daylight and under stadium lights matches step for step → stop: horns up, silence → (6) payoff: 144 players drill into the name.
- **Ending:** the band spells ANIKIT across the field — the device itself writes the name; the command is painted on the turf beside it.
- **Why it won:** a daylight world with one key (the sun) and long shadows, a sound world that *is* the subject (snare line, brass, bass drums), and the product's one unownable truth — determinism — shown as a picture (the seam), not a caption.
- Rejects kept for later films: greenhouse seed, weather order.

# 2 (revisited after research 1b and play 3b)

Crit 2 named an IDEA gap: "the idea serves a logo reveal". The research gave the answer from inside the subject. A field show **is** an animation drawn with people: the drill writer's sets are keyframes, the counts are frames, and every performer reads only their own line of the **dot book** (insider detail, `refs/world.md`). The new idea comes from a different family: a structural device (the performers become a moving picture) rather than a pattern that leads to a name.

1. **The dot book becomes a film (picked).** The agent writes the page. The band performs it, and what they perform is *a moving picture*: 144 players draw a gull, and its wings beat on the drum as it flies the length of the field. The same gull flies again on another night, identical across the seam. Then the line halts, and the band folds into the name.
   - Insider detail: dot books, "8 to 5", spray dots on the practice field, horns up, company front, the silence after the hit.
   - Reference move: Muybridge's *Horse in Motion* (many devices, each doing one exact thing, make motion); mass-games card stunts (individual exact actions make one moving image); Nike Air Max (one metaphor, very different vignettes, sound from the subject).
   - Literal test: delete the name and "JSON → MP4": "a page of dots is performed by a band, and what they perform is a bird in flight, identical on another night". Survives.
   - Swap test: Sora/Runway can't claim "identical on another night"; a generic agent platform doesn't make moving pictures. Fails for competitors → passes.
2. **Zero ticks** (judging). The press-box judge's tick sheet stays blank all show. Insider: visual judges mark "ticks"; ref move: Channel 4 (idea first, logo last). Rejected: it is about *absence* (no marks), which is hard to see, and a judge is a gatekeeper rather than a maker.
3. **(kept from round 1) Greenhouse seed / weather order**: still rejected for the reasons above; they stay rejects for a later film.
4. **(Out there) The flip folder.** Every player's dot-book card is a frame of a flipbook. Flip all 144 cards and you get the show. Insider: flip folders on lyres. Ref move: Muybridge's grid. Rejected: it takes us back to paper on a desk, the last two films' world.

**Device (one sentence):** a written page, performed by many, becomes a moving picture, and it is the same picture every time.
**Escalation:** dots sprayed (the page) → the block marches it → the block becomes a bird → the bird flies, its wings beating on the drum → the same flight on another night, matching across the seam → the company front, the hit, silence → the band folds into the name, set in the same slab as the painted word VIDEO.
