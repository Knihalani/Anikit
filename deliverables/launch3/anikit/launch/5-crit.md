## Gaps (verbatim) and their kind

- Give the overlaid type the same idea as the formation lettering, for example forming every word from performers or field markings, instead of laying a default slab over the image  
  → **CRAFT (type)**
- Push the palette beyond the expected field green with red and gold. Neither the light nor the colour carries any mood or story  
  → **WORLD (palette/light)**
- The tagline idea ('Same page') is literal. Add a turn or surprise in the formations, comparable to how A's eye opens into abstraction  
  → **CRAFT (tagline wording) — read as IDEA? The judge rates the idea itself 'clear, ownable' (7.5) and asks for a turn in the formations; the gap is the caption line, not the device. Treated as CRAFT: the literal tagline is removed from the turf and the turn is shown by the seam instead.**
- The characters are simple, symmetrical vector figures; they need more material detail and individual variation to match A's richness  
  → **WORLD (figure fidelity)**
- Lighting is flat and even across the field; a stadium-light pass, haze or a time-of-day mood would add drama  
  → **WORLD (light)**
- The end card and the tagline are set fairly plainly; a more distinctive typographic treatment would lift the ending  
  → **CRAFT (end typography)**

Critic stage defects (verbatim):

- Tagline 'Same page. Same show.' is laid over the marchers and their shadows. Bodies cut through 'page' and 'Same', and the line is hard to read.
- Marchers in the pairs barely change position over 1/6 s and look like they slide at constant speed. There is no step cycle, no stagger and no ease into the set points, so the band glides rather than marches.
- The ANIKIT end formation reads thin and gappy. The orange/white flag elements sit unevenly on the K, I and T strokes, so the letters look half-formed instead of locked in.
- Two shots are cropped close on a few repeating figures (frames 1 and 5) with large flat drop shadows. The scale jump makes them look like a different, cheaper illustration style than the wide shots.
- The 7.5 s longest shot and a 3.8 s median give stretches with nothing new happening (per-second change falls to 11-16 from about 11 s to 18 s), and the final 2 s are nearly static (4.5, 2.9).
- Shading is flat vector: one hard offset shadow per figure and a uniform grass noise, with no light falloff or depth across the field. At 100% the grass texture repeats in visible stripes.
- The soundtrack is a steady percussive loop of identical transients with a single dropout around 22.5-24 s. It has no build, no orchestration change at the logo reveal, and the tail just decays.

## Pass 2 changes
- **No IDEA gap** (see above), so the device stays; WORLD and CRAFT gaps get fixed.
- **Cut the weakest third:** the painted 'Same page. Same show.' tagline (literal, collides with bodies); the 5× single-player close-up at the stop (read as a cheaper illustration style) becomes a mid shot of the halted company front; the macro opening is pulled back to 3.3× so the figures hold.
- **Marching, not gliding:** every body steps (lateral sway + bob per beat), frozen only at the stop; small per-player variation in stance and plume.
- **Light:** stronger key falloff (warm sun pooling, grandstand shadow deeper, haze toward the far side), real pools under the floodlights at night; heavier vignette.
- **Ending alive and locked:** silks all turn saffron and still once the name forms (no ragged strokes); bells keep flashing; camera keeps rising slowly; the command holds 1.85 s.
- **Sound with a build:** snare roll and timpani crescendo through bars 11–12 into the hit, glockenspiel from the seam, riser into the stop, crowd-less silence, then the fanfare.

# Crit 2 (pass 2, same tags and refs)

**Overall 7.5** (delta +1.33), judged professional 2/2, ranked 2nd of 4 in both trials (behind vertigo). Full score with critic stage 6.97 (pass 1: 6.58).

Scores: {"idea": 8.0, "composition": 8.0, "typography": 6.0, "colour": 7.0, "light_texture": 7.0, "motion": 8.5, "craft": 8.0, "originality": 7.5}

Gaps (verbatim):

- The end-card type is weak: a thin outlined 'ANIKIT' built from figures, over a default slab 'VIDEO' that is centred like a slide. Compare A's typography, which is fully integrated with the image  
  → **CRAFT (end type)**
- The palette is the expected green turf plus maroon, and it needs a bolder colour decision to become an image that stays with the viewer  
  → **WORLD (palette)**
- There is no emotional or thematic layer beyond the ident gag; A ties every visual to the meaning of its subject  
  → **IDEA (no thematic layer beyond the ident)**
- The end-card type is weak: thin outline caps spelled out in band members over a generic slab 'VIDEO' read as a default rather than a typographic idea, and fall well short of A's slab-serif credits  
  → **CRAFT (end type)**
- The colour is pleasant but literal, and none of it has the unsettling, deliberate tension of A's red, magenta and green  
  → **WORLD (palette)**
- The idea serves a logo reveal; the formations could carry more surprise or meaning, for example a formation that comments on the brand rather than just leading up to it  
  → **IDEA (formations lead to a logo rather than comment on the brand)**

Critic defects (verbatim):

- Shots are 4.7 s on median and up to 7.5 s long. Most of that is the camera drifting slowly over a formation that is barely changing, so there are long stretches of near-dead air (per-second change drops to 11–16 between about 11 s and 18 s).
- Every marcher is the same sprite on a strict grid and they move in lockstep. There is no offset, no per-row delay and no follow-through, so the band looks like a copied array rather than a group of people.
- The yellow tuba bells and white instrument rectangles are flat, untextured shapes. Next to the textured grass and soft shadows they look unfinished.
- Instruments and shadows jump between frames in the pairs (row 1: white to gold rectangles; row 3: the shadow lattice). That reads as stepped colour or position changes rather than motion with blur.
- In the concentric-ring frame, a hard-edged light-green panel intrudes at the right edge. It looks like an unplanned transition or a compositing artifact.
- In the 'ANIKIT' end card the letterforms are only implied by marcher dots, with ragged spacing, and 'VIDEO' is a separate serif set flat on top. The two type treatments don't match, and the end card doesn't hold up as a finished lockup.
- Middle frames, such as the swirl formation, are crowded and have no single focal point. Separate frames would not work as posters.

## Decision
- Pass 2 7.5 vs pass 1 7.06: the difference is 0.44, under 0.75, so it counts as a tie. Pass 2 has fewer WORLD gaps (light and figure fidelity are no longer named, and idea/composition/craft/motion all rose), so **pass 2 ships** → final.mp4, contact.png = pass2.png.
- Crit 2 names IDEA gaps (the formations build to a logo rather than say something). The rule allows a third pass with a new idea from a different family. I did not run one: it means a new world, new style frames and a full rebuild. The next Anikit film should start from a reject (greenhouse seed or weather order), or from an idea where the device itself carries the meaning (determinism), not just the reveal.
- Known remaining craft: the end lockup mixes dotted formation caps with a painted slab; the instruments are flat shapes; the middle (11–18 s) changes slowly.

# Crit 3 (same tags; refs wealthsimple-trade, tiffany-holiday, vertigo)

**Overall 7.69** (delta +1.4), judged professional 2/2, ranked 2nd of 4 in both trials (behind vertigo). Full score with the critic stage: 7.14 (pass 2: 6.97, pass 1: 6.58).

Scores: {"idea": 8.0, "composition": 8.0, "typography": 7.0, "colour": 7.0, "light_texture": 7.0, "motion": 8.5, "craft": 8.0, "originality": 8.0}

Gaps (verbatim):

- The palette stays on the expected turf green, maroon and gold; a more unexpected colour decision, the kind A makes, would lift it  
  → **WORLD (palette)**
- The end type is only a pastiche of varsity lettering rather than a typographic idea of its own, and 'VIDEO' is a weak, generic secondary line  
  → **CRAFT (end type)**
- The light is flat and uniform after the shadow frames; the stadium-light or dusk drama the setting invites is missing  
  → **WORLD (light)**
- The palette is close to the subject's literal colours (field green, maroon, gold). A more surprising colour treatment would push it towards the impact of A  
  → **WORLD (palette)**
- The end card's 'VIDEO' sub-line is set as a fairly default slab and doesn't carry the idea as well as the main wordmark  
  → **CRAFT (end type)**
- The figures are simple stylised tokens; more texture and variation in the performers would add richness, much as A contrasts photographed skin with precise line work  
  → **WORLD (figure fidelity)**

Critic defects (verbatim):

- Pacing: shots run up to 11.3 s (median 5.6 s). Between about 10 and 19 s the per-second change sits around 17-22, so the bird formation plays as a near-static hold with only the card-flip line crawling along its outline.
- The marchers are one flat sprite (red ring, white head, cream/gold disc) repeated with identical drop shadows. Up close it reads as instanced clip-art, not a crowd: there is no variation in size, pose or timing offset.
- The card-flip wave moves at a constant linear rate along the bird outline and the long row. There's no acceleration, overlap or settle where it arrives, so the wave reads as mechanical.
- The mid shots use a hard flat diagonal shadow wedge (the light-green block at frame right and lower left) that looks like a stray layer, not motivated lighting.
- The bird logo is small in frame and surrounded by a dense grid of gold dots that competes with it, so the hero shape has weak figure-ground.
- In the end card, 'VIDEO' is set in a slab serif that clashes with the blocky 'ANIKIT' display letters. The marchers on the letters are scattered and noisy, which blurs the letter edges at viewing size.
- Soundtrack: an evenly spaced percussive grid with clean, few-partial pitched tones and a white-noise riser from 19.5 to 22.5 s that cuts off abruptly. It reads as programmatic/generated, not a recorded drumline or produced score.
