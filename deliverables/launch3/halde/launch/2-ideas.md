# 2 · Four ideas

### A. The light carries the taste (physical metaphor)
A glass of the brewed Guji stands on a counter in raking morning sun. A washed Ethiopian brews clear, almost tea-coloured, so light passes *through* it and throws a bright amber caustic into the glass's long shadow. As the liquid settles, the caustic draws a peach, then a bergamot, then a black-tea leaf. The light that passes through this coffee carries its taste.
- Literal test: delete "Halde", "Guji", "washed" → "sunlight through a glass of coffee draws fruit on the table". Survives; nothing is a picture of a name or a mechanism.
- Swap test: a dark-roast competitor fails it outright — dark coffee is opaque and throws no amber light. Only a light, clear, fruit-noted coffee earns this image; the three shapes are this lot's notes.
- Escalation: a flicker of light → it moves when the glass is set down → peach → bergamot → tea leaf → cloud: the light goes out (hard stop) → sun returns and the light writes the name onto the bag.

### B. Steam draws the notes
Steam off a cupping bowl curls into peach, bergamot, a tea leaf.
- Literal test passes, swap test **fails**: "steam forms the flavour" is a stock coffee-ad trope that any roaster could run. Rejected.

### C. The spoon breaks the crust (structural: one continuous move)
A cupping spoon breaks the crust; one continuous top-down push into the cup, through the surface, and the liquid's interior is an orchard at dawn.
- Passes literal; swap test is weak (any fruity coffee); fidelity risk high (an orchard inside a cup built procedurally would read as placeholder). Rejected.

### D. Out there: the bag is a window
The roaster's paper bag is die-cut; whatever the bag is set down on (a bowl of peaches, a tea tin, a bergamot tree) shows through the window as the taste.
- Literal-ish (a packaging gimmick drawn as the idea), and invents packaging the brand hasn't got. Rejected — and it breaks the honesty rule.

## Pick: A — the light carries the taste
**Device in one sentence:** sunlight through the clear brew throws amber light that draws the coffee's tasting notes on the counter.
**Escalation (6 beats):** stray glint (hook) → the glass lands and its light wobbles → the light becomes a peach → becomes bergamot (bigger, closer) → becomes a tea leaf, spreading → a cloud kills the sun (hard stop) → the sun returns and the light, falling across the bag, lights the name.
**Ending:** the same caustic falls on the bag's label: the name is literally lit by the coffee.
**Why it beat the others:** it is the only idea where the *promise* (light, clear, fruit and tea) is a physical consequence the viewer can see, and a dark or muddy coffee could not make the picture. B is a trope, C a fidelity risk, D dishonest.

# After crit 1: IDEA gap → new idea from a different family

Crit 1 (4.56 vs refs 6.58) named the fruit silhouettes "the literal 'flavour = fruit icon' move" — an IDEA gap. Per the rule, A is retired, not polished.

### E. "It's coffee." (structural reversal / misdirection) — **picked for pass 2**
Everything says tea: a bone-china cup and saucer with a blue floral transfer and gold rim, a silver teaspoon, white linen, morning sun through the shop window, and the window's sign-painted lettering falls across the cloth as shadow type: *bergamot* … *peach* … *black tea* — Earl Grey, surely. The cloud passes; the sun returns on the roaster's black bag and the window says **It's coffee.**
- Literal test: delete the name and the mechanism → "a film that looks like tea time and turns out to be coffee". Survives; no product drawn, no feature diagrammed, no fruit icons.
- Swap test: a dark-roast or chocolatey coffee can't run it — only a coffee that genuinely tastes of bergamot and black tea, brewed light enough to sit in a teacup and look like tea, earns the twist.
- Device in one sentence: the tea ritual is a set-up the coffee knocks over.
- Escalation: one word (a hint) → two → three (the case for tea is complete) → the light goes out (stop) → the turn ("It's coffee.") → the name and the day.
- Different family from A: device is a reversal (not a physical metaphor), world is porcelain and linen under window light (not glass and stone with caustics), type is cast shadow lettering (not light inside a shadow).
