# 2 — Four ideas

### A. Three days of light on one dial
The camera never leaves one engine-turned dial. A window's sunlight rakes across the guilloché — evening amber, blue moonlight, dawn — and each pass is a day; the days pass faster and faster while the blued seconds hand keeps sweeping at the same calm rate. After the third night, darkness and only the tick; then Monday's dawn rakes across and reveals the dial printing: the name.
- Literal test (delete "Calibre 9" and "72-hour"): "a watch keeps running while three days of light pass over it" — still an image with a story. PASS.
- Swap test: needs a hand-engraved guilloché dial lit by daylight in a workshop, plus a 72 h reserve; a smartwatch or a 40 h quartz/auto brand can't say it. PASS (soft — any 3-day watch could, but the guilloché light is ours).

### B. The graver
A rose-engine cutter traces the guilloché line by line, the pattern grows, the name is the last line cut.
- Literal test: it's "how the dial is made" — a feature demo. FAIL (mechanism drawn).

### C. The empty weekend (out there)
An apartment over a weekend, unoccupied: mail piling, plants drooping, sunlight crossing the floor — and on a dresser, a watch still ticking. Monday, the owner's hand picks it up.
- Literal test: PASS. Swap test: PASS. But a 12 s film can't build an apartment at full fidelity; illustrated it would lose the material. Too costly for the duration.

### D. Mountain sundial
An alpine valley's shadows sweep like a sundial; the peaks become the hour markers; the watch dial matches the valley.
- Literal test: PASS. Swap test: FAIL — any Swiss watch brand (the alpine cliché).

## Pick: A
- **Device:** the light of three days and nights rakes across one guilloché dial while the seconds hand never changes pace.
- **Escalation:** (1) evening light crawls across the engraving, macro → (2) night falls, moonlight, the hand still sweeps → (3) days begin to pass in seconds → (4) days strobe, the light whirls round the dial → (5) hard stop: dark, only the escapement ticking → (6) Monday dawn rakes in → (7) the name is revealed in the dial printing by that light.
- **Ending:** the dawn light's raking pass is what makes the dial printing "Atelier Vauclair · Calibre 9" legible; it is the device resolving into the name.
- **Why it beat the others:** it's built from the dial's own material (guilloché only exists to play with raking light), it carries the one promise without a caption, it escalates, and it can be rendered at full fidelity as a procedural engraved surface in a shader. B is a mechanism demo, C is unaffordable in 12 s, D is the generic Swiss alpine film.

Rejects kept for next time: C, D.
