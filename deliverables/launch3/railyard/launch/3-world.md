# 3 · One world

- **Material:** a physics-classroom bench of acoustic instruments: satin steel tuning forks (worn, smudged, `imperfections`) on oak resonator boxes with an open sound-hole end, standing on a pale plaster table/floor that sweeps into the wall. Nothing else enters the world.
- **Key light:** one north window, low and from camera-left, raking through venetian blinds (`rig: window`, blinds gobo) — hard-edged stripes with falloff across the plaster. Arc: cool morning (warmth −0.15) at the first strike → warmer late light (+0.35) on the payoff. The top-down shot sees the window itself as a cast pane of light.
- **Palette family:** plaster grey-white #C3C6C0, steel #C9CED3, oak honey/umber, shadow slate. **Accent reserved for the device:** vermilion felt #C7402A — the mallet head that strikes, and the maker's mark that answers (the name).
- **Type voice:** Archivo, heavy and wide (wdth 118) — the voice of stamped instrument-maker's marks and lab equipment, not a SaaS sans. It exists only once, painted/stamped on the front of the answering box (a decal on the oak, lit by the same window, crossed by the blind shadows). No overlay type anywhere.
- **Sound world:** the forks *are* the score: pure sine A (440 / 880) with a faint inharmonic overtone, struck on beat 2 of each bar; a felt thump and wooden box knock (tom/rim); a cello drone as the boxes' body resonance; a vibraphone glint as the ring spreads. Hard stop = true silence; payoff = an A major chord of forks ringing out (`end: ring`).
- **Anikit means:** `scene3d` only — ray-traced steel/oak, HDRI fill + window rig, real DOF, rack focus, shutter motion blur turns the 440 Hz tine vibration into a visible ghost.
- **Change per shot:** subject (one fork → a far twin → two boxes across a table → a field of forks → dead-still tines → the answering box), scale (macro → table → top-down plan → hall → macro → product), camera (70 mm push, rack focus, overhead plan, low dolly, locked, pull-back).

## Style frames (`frames/frames.json` → `frames/compare.png`)
Same key moment (the payoff: the answering box, still ringing, the name on its front). Decisions varied: A dark brand burned into oak (warm light); B the mark in the vermilion accent (warm light); C dark brand, cool window light.

**Picked B:** the mark is the only thing besides the mallet in the accent colour, so the answer visibly matches the strike — the device resolves into the name; A and C sink dark-on-oak (illegible at 100%), C's cool light makes the ending feel like the beginning instead of an arc.

# Pass 2 world (new idea after crit 1: "Already settled")

- **Material:** a white-veined marble banking counter (`ambientcg:Marble021`, real PBR), hourglasses of worn brass (`imperfections` 0.5) and clear blown glass, the sand a fine cobalt. Nothing else enters the world; no gobos, no seamless sweep.
- **Key light:** a photographed afternoon sun through a tall loft window (`polyhaven:hdri/photo_studio_loft_hall`), traced as the shadow-casting key; it glints on the glass and rakes the marble. Background is the same room, defocused (olive wall, warm wood) — one light logic everywhere, only rotated per camera.
- **Palette family:** marble white/grey, brass ochre, olive-umber room. **Accent reserved for the device:** cobalt — the sand (the money), and the name inlaid in the marble.
- **Type voice:** Cormorant Garamond bold capitals, tracked: the inscriptional Roman of bank façades and counter inlays. It appears once, inlaid in cobalt into the counter in front of the last glass — made of the world's own surface, crossed by its light.
- **Sound world:** felt-and-wood: piano pizzicato-like stabs and a soft brushed kit knock on each landing (the turn lands on the beat), cello and strings for the counter's weight; silence for the frozen glass; a D-major ring at the end.
- **Anikit means:** `scene3d` only: lathe glass (thin shell), lathe sand, brass cylinders, every turn computed as a rigid rotation about the neck; the sand switches to the lower bulb mid-turn, so every glass *lands already settled*.
- **Change per shot:** one glass macro (60 mm) → a row of five, side truck (40 mm) → a receding diagonal line with a rack focus (domino wave) → a high plan of 77 glasses turning in a radial wave → the frozen glass, horizontal, macro → crane up from the last glass onto the inlay.

## Style frames, pass 2 (`frames/frames2.json` → `frames/compare2.png`)
Payoff moment; one decision varied, the inlay: A cobalt inscriptional serif; B gilt-brass inscriptional serif; C cobalt heavy grotesque.
**Picked A:** the name is the same cobalt as the settled sand, so the money visibly becomes the name; B (gilt) sinks into the marble's warm grey, and C's grotesque reads as a logo stuck on the counter rather than lettering cut into a bank.
