# 2 · Ideas

1. **Dust in the sunbeam.** In a sunlit rehearsal room every sound visibly passes through the air as a ring through the dust motes in the window light — and is gone. The moment the recorder is lifted into the light, the next sound's ring *stops and hangs in the air*, kept, every mote in place.
   - Literal test (delete "Tern", delete "auto-record on lift"): "the moment you reach, the air stops letting sounds go — they hang in the light" — survives; it is a world, not a diagram.
   - Swap test: the cause of the change is the *lift*; a recorder you have to arm and press can't claim "the moment you reached". Passes.
2. **The lost first second.** A split frame: every sound we hear arrives with its first second missing (a bitten-off chord, half a bird) — until the object is in hand. Literal: it's the feature diagrammed as a timeline with a hole. Swap: weak. Rejected.
3. **Reverse film.** A glass falls, shatters — then the film rewinds to the instant the hand reached for the recorder, which is earlier than the fall. Strong structure, but needs convincing physics + a hand in 3D in 20 s; risk of placeholder modelling. Rejected (keep for a longer film).
4. (out there) **The room holds its breath.** Musicians' sounds are birds flying out of an open window; lifting the recorder shuts the window and the room fills with birds. Literal: tern = bird, the name drawn as a picture. Rejected.
5. **Footprints in snow.** Sounds leave footprints that fresh snow fills in; lifting keeps the prints sharp. Pretty, but snow + footsteps is the "fogged terrain" family and close to the last film's landscape. Rejected.

## Pick: 1 — dust in the sunbeam
- **Device (one sentence):** sounds pass through the sunlit dust as rings and vanish — until the recorder is lifted, and then they hang in the light, kept whole.
- **Escalation:** a pluck's ring passes and dissolves (hint) → a bird outside: another ring, gone (the loss) → the recorder rises into the beam (cause) → the next chord's ring freezes in the air (the change, once) → a loud hit throws rings bigger than the frame; the camera pulls back and every ring is still whole (at scale) → silence, the room hangs full of kept sound (stop) → the window light on the wall resolves into the name (end).
- **Ending:** the sun patch on the plaster — the light that showed every sound — becomes the name, cast in light through the window; the kept rings still hang in front of it.
- **Why it won:** it dramatizes the one fact only Tern has (lift = recording), it lives in one material world (plaster, boards, dust, low sun) that a single shader can hold at full fidelity, and it escalates in scale. Different world, palette and light logic from the previous Tern film (a landscape at dusk).
