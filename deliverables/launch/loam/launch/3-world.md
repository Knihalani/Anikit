# 3 · One world

**Material:** a hand-printed illustration of a windowsill, every pot drawn **in cross-section** (cut away along its front). Soil is the hero material: stippled grit and pebbles; dry earth is pale sand, wet earth is dark umber. Pot walls show a clay cut-face under a teal glaze. One hand for everything (`illo`: wobbling, broken outlines, boil at 8 fps, misregistered print, locked grain).

**Key light:** low morning sun through a four-pane window, top-left (angle ≈ 215°), warm (#FFE0A8), throwing the mullion shape onto the wall and hard cast shadows to the right. Shadows hue-shift to plum, never grey. The window light is the same in every shot; only the framing changes.

**Palette family:** earth umbers (wall #3A2A22, shelf #5C3B27), sand → umber soil, leaf greens, teal glaze. **Water blue (#57B6E8) is reserved for the device**: water, the fill line, the sensor's reading, and the words that are about amount.

**Type voice:** Fraunces, black weight, SOFT 100 — a soft, clumpy old-style serif that looks like it was pressed into wet earth. Type lives in the world: words are written *by water* (blue, sitting on the fill line or in the pool) or *by wet soil* (the name). Never a caption bar, never tracked-caps labels.

**Sound world:** marimba and kalimba plucks (they sound like drops), nylon guitar, a soft shaker; foley of pouring, soaking fizz, a clip click for the sensor, a single drop before the payoff. 128 bpm, 8 bars = 15 s.

**Anikit means:** one `illo` component per shot (lit vector shapes, single light, print textures), text layers in Fraunces with `matte` for the soak-reveal, generated acoustic music + `sound: auto` + explicit sfx. No 3D, no shaders (the medium is print).

**Changes shot to shot:** subject (flooded pot / dry pot / sensor macro / phone / pour / shelf of four / one drop / the name), scale (macro root-level ↔ whole windowsill), camera (push-ins, a tilt down through the soil, a lateral track along the shelf).

## Style frames (`frames/frames-sheet.png`)
Same key moment (the pour has stopped exactly at the roots, "this much." on the line), varying the light:
- **A · morning window** (warm amber sun patch with mullion, umber wall)
- **B · overcast daylight** (cool sage wall, pale window)
- **C · night lamp** (ink-blue wall, warm lamp from the right)

**Picked A.** The hard-edged sun patch with the window bars is the one light you can point to; it makes the frame a poster and its warmth makes the blue water read as the only cool thing in the film. B goes grey-green and kills the soil contrast; C needs a radial glow for its lamp — the exact tell we avoid.
Fixes carried forward: dry soil paler and warmer (it read purple), no leaves drooping over the soil cut, type sits on the line rather than over the roots.
