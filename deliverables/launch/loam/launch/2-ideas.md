# 2 · Four ideas

### A. The cutaway pot (soil as the screen)
We see every pot in section: soil, roots, and where the water really is. Wet earth is dark, dry earth is pale. Water darkens the soil as it soaks down and **stops exactly at the roots**. The words themselves are wet earth — type is written by water soaking into soil.
- Literal test (delete "Loam" and "sensor"): "you can finally see inside the pot, and water stops exactly where the roots are." Survives.
- Swap test: a generic plant-care app has no soil sensor; the film is about *reading the soil*, which is this product's specific thing. Passes mostly — the device is the reading of soil.

### B. The overwatered flood (comedy)
A tiny houseplant drowning under a tidal wave from a watering can, sailors' SOS from the leaves; then calm. Funny but the device stops at "don't overwater" and can't escalate to the product.
- Literal: survives. Swap: any plant app. **Fails swap.**

### C. The plants speak (subtitles)
Plants get subtitles: "thirsty", "I'm fine, stop". Talking-plant gag is generic and puts type as captions, the failure mode we measured. **Fails swap, fails type rule.**

### D. Out there: the plant's weather
Inside each pot is a tiny sky; the sensor is a weather station; the forecast says rain for the fern, sun for the cactus. Lovely but abstract — it hides the real product, and in 15 s the metaphor costs too much setup.

## Pick: A — the cutaway pot
**Device in one sentence:** because the sensor reads the soil, water now soaks down through the cutaway and stops exactly at the roots — never short, never drowning.

**Escalation:**
1. Hook: water floods a pot in section, roots drowning in a pale blue pool (too much).
2. Counter-shot: cracked, pale, dry soil, roots reaching (too little).
3. The sensor clips into the soil; a reading ring travels down through the earth.
4. The phone says when and how much — shown as a fill line on the pot.
5. The pour: dark wet earth spreads down and stops exactly on the line.
6. Scale: a whole shelf of different plants, each drinking to a different depth.
7. Hard stop: one drop, silence.
8. Payoff: the drop soaks into the soil and the wet earth spells **loam**.

**Ending:** the name is written by water soaking into soil — the device resolving into the name.

**Why it won:** it's the only one that shows *the soil being read*, which is what this product actually does; it escalates from one pot to many; and the name ("loam" is a soil) can literally be made of the world.

Rejects kept for later: B (flood comedy), D (pot weather).
