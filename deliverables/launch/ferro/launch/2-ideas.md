# 2. Ideas

## A. "Everything moves but the pan" (time-lapse of a century)
One pan sits on a stone hearth. Sunlight from a window races across it — days, then seasons, then decades — and the years are written on the stone by that same light (2026 … 2126). The world around it turns over; the pan doesn't move, it only darkens and shines.
- Literal test (delete "Ferro" and "cast iron/seasoning"): "an object that stays still while a century of daylight wheels around it" — survives; it's about time, not mechanism.
- Swap test: a generic pan brand could borrow "lasts generations", but the ending is this pan's own cast mark, *Sheffield*, and a dated century 2026→2126 tied to this launch year. Weak-ish on swap; passes because the name is cast into the object itself, read only when it is turned over.

## B. Tree rings of seasoning
Zoom into the black surface; its seasoning layers read as growth rings, one per year, 100 rings. Out-there, beautiful, but a macro of a texture for 20 s doesn't escalate and hides the pan. Rejected (keep for a later spot).

## C. Weight: the pan balanced on an egg / lifted by a child's hand
Fits "half the weight", not the chosen promise; hands are hard to render honestly. Rejected.

## D. (out there) The pan as a sundial
The pan's handle casts a shadow like a gnomon; the shadow sweeps the hours, then the hours are years. Lovely, but it's the same device as A without the cooking, and a sundial reads "time passes", not "it lasts". Merged into A: the handle shadow is the clock hand.

## E. Molten pour → pan
Literal (foundry mechanism drawn). Fails the literal test. Rejected.

## Pick: A (with D's handle-shadow clock folded in)
- **Device in one sentence:** a century of sunlight sweeps across the hearth and writes the years on the stone, while the pan stays exactly where it is.
- **Escalation:** one slow day (shadow crawls) → days strobe (light flickers across) → the years appear in light and race up → seasons/decades blur into a pulse of light → stop.
- **Ending:** the light stops on 2126; the pan is turned over, and its cast base reads FERRO · SHEFFIELD — the name lands as part of the object, made in the same iron, lit by the same sun. CTA in that light: "Back it on Kickstarter".
- **Why it won:** it shows the one promise (a century) as something visible changing around an object that doesn't; it uses one material world and one light source throughout, and the name arrives through an action (the pan turned over), not a slide.
