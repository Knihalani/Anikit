**Weakest frames**
- **12:** no key light, a black object on a black ground.
- **19 and 20:** duplicates, with a small hero that ends on a whimper.
- **5:** a repeat of 4.

**Biggest change:** the world is too thin to carry a century. Nothing ages and the kitchen never changes. Let each year change something in the scene while the pan stays constant: a different meal or tableware, seasoning that visibly deepens. Use fewer dates, each one its own poster. End on the cast mark in raking light, with the call to action beside it in the same shot.

**Cut:** frames 2, 5, 7, 9, 13, 19 and 20.

## Changes for pass 2
1. **The world ages around the pan (fixes 4, 7 and the thin world).** Each date gets its own life in the sun pool beside the pan, made of the same stone, ceramic and iron world: two eggs (2031), a stoneware mug (2058), a bowl and a wooden spoon (2084), and different crockery in the race. The pan's seasoning visibly deepens: matte grey-brown in 2026, glossy black by 2126 (roughness and colour keyed per shot). Swap test: the century ends on this foundry's own cast mark, dated 2026, so the dates are the product's birth year counted forward. That is specific to a pan launched this year in Sheffield.
2. **No dark frames (3, 5).** The sun's flicker never drops below 55%.
3. **Every frame is a different layout (4, 15).** The race becomes six cuts with six different cameras (overhead, low side, rim macro, 3/4 left, handle macro, high wide), not one camera alternating sides.
4. **Glyphs (12).** Move "2026" fully into frame in shot 1. Lift the floor of the 2084 shot.
5. **Ending (13, 14).** Cut the separate call-to-action shot, which was the weakest third together with the duplicate race layouts. The cast-mark shot runs bars 7–8 (5 s) in one continuous pull-back. "Back it on Kickstarter" slides onto the stone in the same sunlight beside the base at 17.6 s while the light keeps moving, so the last frames differ.
6. **Not changed:** the idea and the one world (checks 1, 2 and 6 pass).
