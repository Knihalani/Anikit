# 2 · Four ideas

1. **The darkroom.** A red-lit developing tray. A sheet slides under the developer; an agent's sentence comes up on it in the margin, then the picture comes up — and the picture *moves*, with sound. Prints keep arriving, each a different film. Two sheets from the same sentence are laid over each other: not one grain differs. The safelight clicks off; room light; the last sheet develops into the name.
   - Literal test: delete name + mechanism → "words become a living photograph in a darkroom tray, and identical twins come out of it". Survives (it's a world, not a diagram).
   - Swap test: a generative-video brand can't claim identical twin prints from one sentence; the determinism beat and "finished, with sound" make it fail for competitors. Passes.
2. **The foundry.** A sentence is pressed into casting sand; molten bronze is poured; the cast that comes out is a scene. Same mould → identical casts.
   - Literal: survives. Swap: weaker — "casting" reads as manufacturing; and ray-traced molten metal across 30 s blows the render budget. Also close to the "chrome on a studio floor" trend.
3. **The night shift.** One continuous move through a sleeping office: a note left on a desk at night; in the morning the screen plays the finished film. Wait → done.
   - Literal: survives. Swap: fails the swap test — any "AI does your work overnight" tool fits. Discard.
4. **(Out there) The echo chamber.** Speak a sentence into a canyon and the echo comes back as a film, and every echo is identical.
   - Literal: survives. Swap: OK. But "echo" says *copy/repeat*, not *finished*; it's hard to make the picture live inside an echo without it becoming an abstract shader demo.

## Pick: 1 · The darkroom
- **Device (one sentence):** a sentence goes into the developer and a finished, moving, sounding film comes up on the paper.
- **Escalation:** (1) words develop on a blank sheet → (2) a picture comes up and *starts to move*, sound arrives → (3) a print of light and material — a ray-traced glass world → (4) the tray fills: many sheets, each a different living film (type, simulation, shader) → (5) two sheets from one sentence slide together and cancel to black: identical → stop → (6) safelight off, room light on, the print is lifted out in full colour → (7) the last sheet develops the name.
- **Ending:** the name develops on paper in the tray like every print before it — the device performs the name; the command develops under it as the call to action.
- **Why it won:** it holds one world (tray, developer, paper, safelight) for the whole film, puts the type physically on paper, gives a natural colour arc (safelight red → white light) and turns the one thing nobody else can claim — identical output — into an image (twin prints cancelling).
- Rejects kept for later films: foundry, echo chamber.
