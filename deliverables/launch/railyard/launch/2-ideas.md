# 2 · Four ideas

## A. Carbon copy
A pen writes on a white sheet; the same stroke appears, at the same instant, in carbon blue on a yellow copy. Then the copy moves farther away — across the desk, out of sight, onto a hundred sheets — and the mark still lands at the same instant.
- Literal test (delete "Railyard", delete "API/settlement"): *a mark made in one place appears elsewhere at the same instant, however far apart.* Survives — it's a film about simultaneity, not rails.
- Swap test: put Stripe/Wise on it → a slower competitor's name breaks it (their pitch is reach or price, not simultaneity). Partial pass; the specific yellow-copy/carbon-blue world and the "same stroke" framing make it ours.

## B. Thunder before lightning (out there)
A storm where the thunder (the receipt) is heard before the flash — cause and effect reversed, because the money landed before the sending finished.
- Literal: survives. Swap: generic "fast" metaphor. Hard to render with weight; reads as a weather film. Rejected.

## C. Ice cube on a counter
A drink is handed across a counter; the cube hasn't started melting when it's already in the other hand. Timelapse/no-timelapse contrast.
- Literal: survives. Swap: fails — "fast delivery" for anything (couriers, food). Rejected.

## D. The relay that has no handoff
Relay runners where the baton is already in the last runner's hand when the first one starts. 
- Literal: drifts toward "money moving along a track" = rails/yard pun. Fails the literal test in spirit (it's the name's world). Rejected.

## Pick: A, Carbon copy
**Device in one sentence:** whatever is written on the sender's sheet is already on the receiver's sheet, in carbon blue, at the same instant.

**Escalation (4–6 beats):**
1. Macro: a pen writes. (hint)
2. The top sheet lifts: the copy underneath already has the mark. (the change, once)
3. The copy is at the far end of the desk, no contact — the mark still lands on it, stroke for stroke. (distance)
4. Dozens of copies across the whole floor all take the same stroke at once. (scale: one-to-many)
5. Hard stop: everything still, silence.
6. The pen presses once; the top sheet tears away; the impression underneath is the name. (payoff)

**Ending:** the name is the last carbon impression — pressed into the copy by the world's own action (tear-off reveal).

**Why it won:** it's the only idea whose device is the promise itself (send = arrive, one stroke), it escalates by distance and count without changing world, and it is tactile and cheap to make beautiful (paper, ink, a lamp).
