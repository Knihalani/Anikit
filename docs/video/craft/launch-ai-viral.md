# AI and software launch videos that spread (2022–2026), and what an engine that can't film should take from them

Web research, 2026-11-18, text sources only; no video was watched. **(fetched)** = page read; **(search)** = snippet only; **n/s** = no source states it (never filled from memory). **[E]** = evidence; **[O]** = our opinion. Counts are unaudited snapshots; conflicting figures are both given.

**AI and software only.** Brand virality, Sora, Devin, Friend, Cluely and Kalshi are in [`launch-viral.md`](launch-viral.md); craft cases in [`launch-research.md`](launch-research.md); our jury results in [`launch-evidence.md`](launch-evidence.md); tells in [`ai-tells.md`](ai-tells.md).

**Caveats.** Distribution decides most reach: Devin had 15 endorsing posts from ~3M combined followers ([Sally Liu](https://sallysliu.substack.com/p/devins-viral-recipe), fetched). Big labs' numbers are confounded by their audiences. The trigger was often an output, a mistake or a fight, not a film; our engine controls only the frame.

## 2. Which formats spread for AI products, and why

**The best data: the Infinite study [E]** ([fetched](https://hub.infinite.fast/research/launch-videos)). It covers 194 hand-checked startup launches on X, uses medians, and its author calls the results "strong correlations, not guarantees".
- **Clarity does not separate winners:** ~9 in 10 were clear, flops included.
- **Hook strength** (median views): strong 32,119, decent 345, weak 196.
- **Opening shot:** a face 976, a product demo 332, a **text card 164**. Title cards were the worst common opening.
- **Differentiation** was the strongest single predictor: strongly differentiated 9,385 vs generic 146.
- **Pacing:** dynamic 6,762, steady 319, flat 141.
- **Format:** landscape 1,198 vs vertical 107.
- **Length:** clips under 15 s were the worst bucket, and 90 s+ was the best (median 4,396). The top quartile's median length was 56 s.
- **Not separators:** "native-to-the-feed" feel and low polish.
- **Engagement asks** ("reply LAUNCH") had a 714× higher median, confounded by big accounts using them more.
- **[O] Reconciling with kinetic type:** a static title card opened worst. Type that moves *with* the product (a prompt being typed, as in Pixel's teaser) is a product-in-motion opening, not a title card.

**Formats ranked by evidence [O, built on the table].**
1. **Output as the ad** spreads furthest (ChatGPT, Midjourney, NotebookLM, Nano Banana, Veo 3, Sora 2): every share is a new example with social currency.
2. **A live or unedited demo of one magic moment:** GPT-4o's interruptible voice, v0 or Bolt streaming an app, computer use moving a cursor. Liveness is the proof; Gemini's edited version became a scandal.
3. **Founder voice with self-awareness:** Arc's anti-keynote, and "we might be the wrong investment". Without humour or proof (Humane, Rabbit) it reads as hype.
4. **Category claim plus anxiety:** Devin ("first AI software engineer"), Friend, Cluely. Huge reach, then a trust bill.
5. **Brand films** build position; they spread with a fight or a joke (Anthropic vs OpenAI). Critics punish abstraction that never says what the product does (Adweek on Codex).

**What backfired, in one pattern [O].** Every backfire is a **proof failure**: staged demo (Gemini), wrong answers (Humane), wrong chart (GPT-5), unshipped feature (Apple), copied output (Figma), live failure (Meta). Second: AI replacing a valued human moment (Dear Sydney).

## 3. The visual language of expensive software and AI launches

**Evidence [E]:**
- **Linear's principles** ([design refresh](https://linear.app/now/behind-the-latest-design-refresh), fetched): "Don't compete for attention you haven't earned"; "Structure should be felt not seen"; warmer, less saturated greys; fewer separators and icons, no decorative colour.
- **Linear's actual launch video is not a motion film.** Linear Initiatives is a 4:15 full-frame real UI walkthrough, with the head of product in a small webcam bubble (moonb, fetched).
- **Raycast "New Raycast" teaser** (39 s): cropped fragments of the real interface (a glowing search bar, an emoji grid), "lit for drama". An AI action list ticks through real tasks ("Getting active Linear Issues") (moonb, fetched).
- **Pixel 9 Pro teaser** (30 s): opens on a typed prompt, "Write me a breakup letter", over a dark screen. The phone appears only afterwards (moonb, fetched).
- **OpenAI's 2025 refresh film** ([Studio Dumbar](https://studiodumbar.com/work/openai-brand-film), fetched): ample space, "subtle imperfections", elements introduced one at a time, dots and logos "animate naturally", sound drawn from "human interactions", a palette "inspired by natural environments".
- **Vercel Ship 2025:** a hand-animated Blender short on twos, with one custom liquid shader. **Figma Config:** a reusable motion library built on four verbs ([launch-research](launch-research.md)).
- **Founder demos:** FLORA's camera is "zoomed in, closely following the on-screen mouse". The Claude→Blender MCP demo is "massively sped-up", 23 s. Daylight uses "a quiet narrator" and shows what the product *removes* ([speedrun](https://speedrun.substack.com/p/how-to-make-a-viral-launch-video), fetched).
- **The common tooling:** Screen Studio-style recorders zoom in on the cursor just before a click, enlarge it and smooth its path ([Screen Studio help](https://www.screen.studio/help), search).

**What reads as expensive [O]:**
1. **Real product, real data** (Raycast, Claude Code, Linear, v0). A plausible task name does more than any glow.
2. **Macro UI.** Crop one component to 2–4× and light it like an object (Raycast). Don't shrink the whole app into a floating window.
3. **One continuous input→output shot per beat, and cursor choreography** (see rules 4 and 7).
4. **Restraint:** one warm neutral ground plus the brand's accent; hierarchy by contrast, not boxes; big moves rare.
5. **Human-made imperfection:** Dumbar's imperfections, Vercel on twos, ManvsMachine's degraded renders, Arc's webcam.
6. **Sound tied to UI events** rather than a stock bed.

**What reads as artificial or template [O, plus [`ai-tells.md`](ai-tells.md) and [`launch-evidence.md`](launch-evidence.md)]:**
- Robots, glowing brains, neural-net gradients, which even vendors call "generic" ([AutoAE](https://autoae.online/blog/how-to-make-ai-agent-launch-video-without-after-effects), [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/ai-startup-launch-video), search); lorem UI; identical eases; a 4 s static end card.
- The "tasteful-AI kit" (dark ground, one accent, tiny tracked mono label, bold grotesk), in 4 of our 7 jury-reviewed launch films. **The trap:** the "Linear look" *is* that kit when copied without real UI inside it [O].

**Reject vendor "specs":** ShipScenes' "84% follow a 4-beat structure", spring constants and LUFS targets have no stated method ([dev.to](https://dev.to/shipscenes/we-analyzed-720-ai-startup-launch-videos-openai-linear-cursor-raycast-here-is-the-4-beat-43c), fetched).

## 4. What an engine that cannot film reality should do

**Evidence that simulated reality reads as fake [E]:**
- TechRadar's critic called Coca-Cola's 2024 AI holiday ad "a soulless and creepy dystopian nightmare", citing "uncanny humans" ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/coca-cola-faces-backlash-again-for-missing-the-spirit-of-the-holidays-with-another-ai-slop-ad), search).
- Toys "R" Us's Sora film was panned for unnatural movement and inconsistent characters ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/internet/toys-r-us-ai-video-ad-controversy-explained-commercial-rcna159030), search).
- Viewers caught people floating and glitching feet in Sora's Tokyo sample ([Futurism](https://futurism.com/grotesque-womans-feet-amazing-openai-video), search).
- An Animoto survey: ~83% suspect they've watched AI video; robotic gestures are the most-cited tell ([Quartz](https://qz.com/ai-video-animoto-state-of-industry-report-2026), search).
- On the remedy: "Viewers don't mind AI. They mind being fooled." Keep synthetic humans to 1–2 s, or use a non-human mascot ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/stop-trying-to-make-ai-video-look-real), fetched).
- **Internal:** our ray-traced 3D board scored 3.88 vs 4.0 for the 2D board, and better light did not move the jury ([`launch-evidence.md`](launch-evidence.md)). The user called the ray-traced marching band "horrid… so artificial and weird".
- **[O]:** this evidence is about *generative* video; extending it to procedural CG is our inference. The mechanism is shared: viewers know how bodies, crowds, grass and food move, so every error reads.

**Decision table [O]:**

| The brief wants… | Do | Don't |
|---|---|---|
| Show the AI product working | Rebuilt real UI: macro crops, a cursor path, input→output in one shot, real strings | A full window shrunk to 40% with lorem |
| A feeling or brand idea | Kinetic type acting out its meaning; an abstract graphic world with one metaphor (OpenAI's dots, Vercel's liquid) | Stock AI tropes (brains, robots, particle "neural nets") |
| A physical product | 3D of the *object*: studio light, macro materials, exploded views (Quest 3, Dyson in moonb) | Placing it in a simulated living room full of people |
| A real-world scene (a stadium, a city, people, food, nature) | Translate it into a graphic system: flat or illustrated, diagrammatic, toy-scale or typographic (a marching band becomes formation dots on a field diagram that spell the line) | Ray-traced realism of bodies, crowds, grass or faces |
| Proof | Numbers computed from data, a real repo or task, an "unedited" or "sped up 2×" label | Hand-drawn bars; implied real time |
| Humour or a hook | A typed prompt as the cold open (Pixel), self-aware copy (Arc), genre parody in graphics (Know-It-Alls as a type-only trailer) | A synthetic human delivering the joke |

**3D works [O]** for objects, materials, abstract forms, UI planes and consistent stylised worlds; **it looks fake** for simulated real places, living things, familiar physics, faces and gait.

## Implications for an agent skill

Each rule is a yes/no check on the spec or the 6 evenly spaced judge frames. Thresholds are unvalidated heuristics. Rules 2, 4, 6, 7 and 14 apply to demo/proof films; a brand film (abstract or kinetic type, like OpenAI's dots) is exempt from them.

1. **No simulated living reality.** No frame renders photoreal or ray-traced humans, crowds, animals, food, vegetation or real places; real-world subjects become a graphic system (diagram, illustration, toy scale, type). Fail on any realistically shaded humanoid.
2. **Product in motion by 2 s.** The frame at t = 1.5 s shows the product's UI or output *changing*. A static title card, logo or empty frame fails.
3. **The first on-screen sentence is a claim or a problem, not a name.** Not just "Introducing X": it states the outcome or pain in ≤ 10 words.
4. **One magic moment in one continuous shot.** At least one shot shows input and result together with no cut between them, and holds the result readable for ≥ 2 s.
5. **Real UI content.** Every UI string is plausible domain content: no lorem, no "Feature 1", no garbled glyphs. Task names are specific ("Getting active Linear Issues", not "Doing task").
6. **Macro UI.** At least half of the UI shots crop to one component at ≥ 2× its native size. No shot shows the whole app window below 60% of frame width.
7. **Cursor choreography.** A camera move toward the cursor starts ≥ 6 frames before every click; cursor paths are eased curves, never teleports; each click gets a visible UI response within 6 frames.
8. **Honest time and numbers.** Any compressed latency carries an on-screen "sped up" or "2×" label. Every chart's bar lengths are computed from its numbers, so the check can verify their ratios.
9. **A restrained palette from the brand.** One neutral ground (tinted, not #000 or #fff), plus ≤ 1 accent taken from the product's own brand. Fail on rainbow or "neural" gradients, robots, brains or glowing particle nets.
10. **Not the tasteful-AI kit.** No frame combines a tiny tracked mono or caps label with a centred bold-grotesk headline over a dark backdrop. In at least half of the text frames, the type is the UI's own content or sits inside the scene.
11. **3D only for objects and abstract worlds.** A 3D scene contains the product, materials, UI planes or abstract forms. Its look (palette, light logic, grain) is identical across all shots.
12. **Hero ≥ 30 s, 16:9.** The X hero cut is landscape and at least 30 s, with no upper cap (90 s+ was the best bucket, confounded by who makes long films). A cut under 15 s is only a teaser.

15. **The ending is live and actionable.** The final ≤ 3 s shows the product state or a lock-up made from the film's own world, plus the real URL or handle. No static card longer than 2 s. The skill also outputs post copy: one concrete first line and one explicit ask.
