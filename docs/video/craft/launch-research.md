### Cross-case patterns

1. **One physical or behavioural metaphor for the benefit, never a list:** no gravity (wireless), an expanding room (sound), dots (intelligence), mess tamed, degraded renders (tactility), liquid (malleable software).
2. **The product is a cause, not a subject.** The world changes; the product is why.
3. **Material honesty is the look** (practical sets, film stock, one custom shader), often near-monochrome.
4. **A real tension:** ABCD, HyperFrames and vidpros say show the real product early; BUCK, Yonick, Raycast and Bounce withhold or abstract it. Proof films show real UI early; teasers withhold and pair with a separate spec cut (Oura, Dyson).

## 2. Finding the idea

- **One sentence first.** The single-minded proposition is "one sentence… the single most important thing" the work should convey. Guides cap it at about 15 words and call it "the most difficult sentence" on a brief. Extra messages become support ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/open-mic/why-the-single-minded-proposition-so-important), [ANA](https://ana.net/training/course/id/CD-SMPMSP-HD-HI), search). Hollywood's maxim: describe the plot "in a single sentence" ([Campaign Asia](https://www.campaignasia.com/article/opinion-make-the-connection-with-hollywood-style-brevity/yg4np6a5d5pgpqxcpzkt9zg12u), fetched). 
- **Narrow the promise before animating.** "Narrowing the sentence is often the fastest way to improve the video before making a single animation". Write two alternatives before choosing. Test each scene: "If this scene disappears, which belief about the promise becomes harder to justify?" ([open-design](https://open-design.ai/blog/product-launch-video-storytelling/), fetched).
- **Diverge, then pick.** Joey Korenman mind-maps "words, phrases and ideas" until one "sets off a light bulb", then turns it into a visual metaphor. The concept "cut[s] down the nearly endless ways to animate" ([School of Motion](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/5-step-blueprint-motion-designers-use), fetched). BUCK pitched Fuel TV with 6 concepts (4 picked) and routinely adds "one or two really out-there/different concepts/looks" ([Thunderchunky](https://www.thunderchunky.co.uk/articles/commercial-breakfasts-with-buck/), fetched).
- **Concept before visuals.** Jorge Canedo Estrada (Ordinary Folk) says solidify the concept "before rushing into visuals", warns that over-design can "obscure the concept", and tests with fresh eyes (search). The Ordinary Folk pipeline: script → storyboard → style frames. Mock the camera move first for timing, then "block in your big movements first". A finished frame is often "version 30" ([SoM podcast](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/ordinary-folk-podcast), fetched).
- **Style frames as a comparison, not a gallery.** Show "variations of the same scene side by side" so reviewers judge style, not content. Name each option and note how it differs. Lock the direction before animating ([Milanote](https://www.milanote.com/guide/motion-design-style-frames), fetched).
- **The idea from the object:** reMarkable's look came from handling the device; Halide's scene from where the product is plausibly used; Bounce from the word "wireless".
- **Crit with outsiders.** "If someone gets it with no context, then that's far better". Check work against "your own brief and your own strategy" ([Creative Lives / DixonBaxi](https://creativelivesinprogress.com/articles/how-do-you-know-your-work-is-good-enough-dixonbaxi), fetched). open-design: ask an outsider what changed and record "the exact wrong inference".
- **Kill most of it.** BUCK's Nothing styleframes: "most of which didn't make the final cut". "Kill your darlings" has no sourced studio procedure: it is a writing maxim ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/opinion/2015/02/09/kill-your-darlings-self-discipline-copywriting), search).
- **Pipeline:** brief → concept → script → boards → style frames → animatic → motion → sound → review ([roundup](https://www.motionmedia.com/mm-blog/motion-graphics-workflow-from-brief-to-final-render/), search); basement added prerenders "to fine-tune timing and add or trim shots". Styling before the script is locked means paying "double" (vidpros).
- **Unsourced:** "write the idea without the product name" and a logo-swap test have no formal source (nearest: [Kantar](https://www.kantar.com/north-america/inspiration/advertising-media/swipe-right-how-ai-can-help-your-ads-stand-out), search). They are proposed checks below, not studio practice.

## 3. Structure, timing, end card

- **Google ABCD** (Ipsos and Nielsen-backed, correlational; [PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf), fetched):
  - "surprising, unexpected, or delightful images… early on"; "more than two frames in the first five seconds"; "tightly-framed shots of people or product at the beginning".
  - "Introduce your product or brand in the first five seconds"; "integrating the logo into the storyline" raises recall.
  - A "specific" CTA ("sign up"), "audible and visible"
- **Six-beat proof film, 60–75 s** ([vidpros](https://vidpros.com/saas-explainer-video/), fetched, vendor anecdote): pain hook 5–8 s → cost of pain 5–10 s → solution 10–15 s → real-UI "aha" 20–30 s → proof 5–10 s → CTA 5–8 s. "Open with the pain, not the logo." One use case per video. Public hero videos over 90 s lose attention. "'Learn more' simply squanders a close."
- **HyperFrames launch reference** ([launch.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/main/skills/hyperframes/references/types/launch.md), fetched):
  - Arc: hook → tension → reveal → proof → payoff → claim → end card, at 20–45 s.
  - Camera moves take 0.4–0.6 s. Isolate the action at 1.6–2× real size. Text is ≥ 26 px at 1080p.
  - The mark lands in the last 5–10%, centred, at 15–30% of frame width. "Perform the reveal rather than fading it in." The real CTA holds 1.5–3 s.
- **Type at perception speed:** 5–7 frames per short word, one word at a time (Don't Blink).
- **Endings in the cases:** hard cut to near-silence plus one sound (Don't Blink); pull-back placing the product in a real room (Dyson, Halide); product beside its name (Oura).
- Beats per length: [`storytelling.md`](storytelling.md) §2.2. Lognormal, non-uniform shot lengths: [`editing.md`](editing.md).

## 4. Failure modes

- **Feature lists.** Cramming three features into 90 s dilutes the message, and one use case reportedly tripled reply rate in one test (vidpros, anecdotal). HyperFrames bans "a feature list shown as three cards".
- **UI dumps and fake UI.** "Treat the video as a product ad spot—not a quick screen recording"; desktop text is unreadable on phones ([roundup](https://wowto.ai/blog/common-explainer-video-mistakes-saas-companies-make), search). HyperFrames: "never an invented button, dashboard, or mockup". open-design: "A stopwatch overlay does not turn a staged workflow into a measured study."
- **Stock and template.** Stock footage plus text cards "signals a product that is not ready to be seen" (roundup); "Tool-first decisions produce template-shaped video" (vidpros).
- **The AI-default look.** Inter plus an indigo-to-purple gradient plus three rounded cards ([925studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells), [Superdesign](https://superdesign.dev/blog/why-ai-design-looks-generic), search). The cause is models drifting toward "the statistical center" and Tailwind's indigo defaults ([DEV](https://dev.to/alanwest/why-every-ai-built-website-looks-the-same-blame-tailwinds-indigo-500-3h2p), search). The HyperFrames-banned openers are a typed prompt and a title over a dimmed screenshot. See also [`ai-tells.md`](ai-tells.md).
- **Effects instead of results:** about a tenth of the views (HyperFrames, self-reported). **Generic synthwave:** no source found; treated as a default-look failure. **Logo first or docked in a corner:** vidpros, HyperFrames.

## Implications for an agent skill

1. **Write the idea as one sentence of ≤ 15 words that never names the product**, and a second sentence that names the device ("no gravity", "the room grows"). If the first sentence fits a competitor, reject it (proposed logo-swap test).
2. **Generate ≥ 4 concepts, one deliberately "out-there"** (BUCK), each a different metaphor. Pick one with a written reason; record the rejects so the next film differs.
3. **Choose the mode before any visual: proof film or teaser/brand film.** Proof: real product visible by 5 s, with more than two shots in the first 5 s (ABCD). Teaser: product withheld or fragmented, and the brief must say a spec cut exists elsewhere.
4. **The product must cause a visible change in the world** (mess → order, small → large, heavy → weightless). Fail any shot where the product just floats and rotates with nothing around it (HyperFrames tell).
5. **Pick one material world and one look rule derived from the product** (film grain for tactility, dots for an interface motif, one custom shader). Fail mixed unrelated material languages, and indigo/purple gradients or Inter-on-cards unless the brand owns them.
6. **Show only real UI and copy** from the brief or assets. No invented dashboards, numbers or interactions. Isolate UI at 1.6–2× and keep text ≥ 26 px at 1080p.
7. **One use case or promise per film.** If the beat sheet has more than one feature beat, cut or demote it to support. Each scene must pass "which belief gets harder to justify if this is cut?"
8. **Frame 0 must already be the hook:** a surprising image mid-motion, never a logo, a fade from black, or a typed prompt.
9. **Lock in order: sentence → beat sheet → 3 named style-frame variants of the same key shot, compared side by side → animatic with timing and music → full render.** Don't style before the script is locked.
10. **Fit kinetic type to read rate:** one short word holds 5–7 frames in fast passages, and a card of n words holds ≥ 0.3 s × n + 0.5 s. At least one word must act out its meaning.
11. **Build structure on the music:** choose or generate the track first, cut on completed ideas at bar lines, and put one hard stop or silence before the payoff (Don't Blink); end on an instrumental resolve, not a fade mid-phrase (Halide).
12. **Perform the end:** the mark lands in the last 5–10% at 15–30% of frame width, through an action rather than a fade, and the specific CTA ("Download", a command) holds 1.5–3 s on screen. Never use "Learn more".
13. **Keep length to purpose** (from the cases and vidpros): teaser 15–40 s, hero 30–60 s, proof ≤ 75 s; fail > 90 s unless it's an explainer.
14. **Run an outsider check:** a fresh critic who sees only the render states what changed and which moment proved it. Log any wrong inference and fix that beat.
15. **Kill one third:** after the first full render, remove the weakest third of shots or effects and re-check that the sentence still reads.
