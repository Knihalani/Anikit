# Launch films that spread: evidence and rules

Web research, 2026-11-18, text sources only. **(fetched)** = page read; **(search)** = search snippet only, not read in full; **[E]** = evidence; **[O]** = our opinion. View counts are as reported by the source cited, often by the brand or its agency, and are not audited. Complements [`launch-research.md`](launch-research.md) (craft cases, structure), [`launch-evidence.md`](launch-evidence.md) (our jury results) and [`storytelling.md`](storytelling.md) (hooks, beats).

**Read this first.** Most of what made these films spread happened outside the film. That includes:
- paid seeding: Liquid Death put about $5k of Facebook media behind its ad ([Chief Marketer](https://www.chiefmarketer.com/liquid-death-ceo-on-building-a-provocative-brand-small-marketing-budgets-and-super-bowl-ads/), search);
- coordinated endorsers: Devin had 15 endorsing posts on demo day, from accounts with about 3M combined followers, and only 5 showed signs of having tested it ([Sally Liu](https://sallysliu.substack.com/p/devins-viral-recipe), fetched);
- a founder or celebrity persona;
- real stunts, and live events.

Vendors sell "20 to 30 posts" landing within a 2–3 h window ([launchvideo.com](https://www.launchvideo.com/blog/the-science-behind-every-viral-launch-video-on-x), fetched). The cases are also survivors: nobody writes up the similar films that sank. An agent controls only the frame, so the rules cover only that: a film can make sharing *possible*, not make it *happen*.

## 2. Why things get shared

**Arousal beats valence [E].** In 3 months of *New York Times* articles, a 1 SD increase in awe raised the odds of making the most-emailed list by 30%, and anger by 34%. Sadder content was *less* viral. Lab studies confirm that the activation level drives sharing ([Berger & Milkman 2012](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf), fetched). Caveat: these are emailed articles, not video.

**STEPPS [E, framework].** Berger's six drivers are social currency, triggers, emotion, public, practical value and stories ([Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/contagious-jonah-berger-on-why-things-catch-on/), search). In the table: social currency (first to show friends Sora), practical value (Purple), stories (Duolingo), public (Liquid Death's can).

**Patterns [O].**
1. **The moment is an event, not a claim.** Each one survives as a sentence about something that *happened*: "it blended an iPhone", "eggs didn't break", "he did the splits between trucks". None survives as "best-in-class performance".
2. **Proof, built as a show.** Blendtec, Purple, Volvo and Squatty Potty are product demonstrations dressed as spectacle. The spectacle carries the share; the demo carries the sale.
3. **"How did they do that?"** Old Spice's one-take, Volvo's real stunt and Jacquemus's ambiguity drive rewatches and "is it real?" comments.
4. **Founder voice and self-awareness.** Dollar Shave Club and Arc mock their own category. Arc's video mocked keynotes, restated the problem ("browsers don't actually browse") and was ~90% use cases and reactions ([Off Menu](https://offmenu.substack.com/p/three-reasons-arcs-launch-video-went), fetched).
5. **Participation.** Old Spice answered fans by name; Altman turned replies into demos; Veo 3's "Prompt Theory" format was copied within days ([Hybrid Horizons](https://hybridhorizons.substack.com/p/the-prompt-theory-phenomenon), search).
6. **Controversy is cheap reach with a bill attached.** It works (Cluely, Jaguar, Friend), but the moment that spreads is the critic's version.

**X launch posts [O].** "We built this, please check it out" is not a hook; it must answer "why should I care?" ([Devin analysis](https://sallysliu.substack.com/p/devins-viral-recipe), fetched). Show the product working in the first seconds, legible without the caption, with every frame reproducible by a user next week ([Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/viral-x-launch-videos), fetched, vendor).

## 3. Platforms, 2024–2026

- **TikTok [E].** Watching a longer video to the end is a strong signal that outweighs weak ones. Likes, shares, comments and replays feed recommendations. Follower count is *not* a direct factor ([TikTok newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you), fetched). TikTok's ad research ([creative tips PDF](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Auction_Ads_Creative_Tips.pdf), fetched; ad data, not organic):
  - over 63% of the highest-CTR videos show the key message or product within 3 s;
  - 9:16 gives a 25% higher 6-second watch-through;
  - 40% of top-VTR ads use text overlays, 33% break the fourth wall;
  - tracks above 120 BPM "often drive higher" VTR.

  In [TikTok's conversion study](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/creative-that-drives-conversions) (fetched): 21–34 s ads had a 280% conversion lift, on-screen text in the first 7 s a 43% lift, and 9:16 a 91% lift. In a TikTok-commissioned Kantar survey, 88% of users called sound "essential" ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en-US/blog/kantar-report-how-brands-are-making-noise-and-driving-impact-with-sound-on-tiktok), search). That is a survey answer, not a sound-on viewing rate.
- **Instagram Reels [E].** Mosseri: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends… average watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach" ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/), fetched, Jan 2025). He also says sends per reach correlate with reach "more… than anything else" ([Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/unpacking-instagrams-focus-on-sends-per-reach-a-timeline-why-it-matters-and-tips-for-creators-brands), search).
  Instagram warns "Reels over 3 minutes won't be recommended to new audiences" ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length), search); originals rank above reposts ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html), search).
- **YouTube Shorts [E].** Since 31 Mar 2025 a view counts every start or replay, with no minimum. The old count lives on as "engaged views" ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/), search). YouTube publishes no ranking weights; "viewed vs. swiped away" is a reported metric, not a confirmed ranking input.
- **X [E].** The open-sourced ranker predicts:
  - favorite, reply, repost, quote, share, share via DM and share via copy link;
  - dwell, "video quality view" and "video continuation seconds";
  - negatives: not interested, mute, block, report and "not dwelled".

  The score is a weighted sum, with an out-of-network discount ([xai-org/x-algorithm](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm), fetched). No weights were visible, so the widely quoted 2023 numbers (reply 13.5 and so on) are stale. Links are reportedly downranked against native video (secondary blogs, search).
- **Engagement bait [E].** Meta has demoted "comment YES" and "tag a friend" posts since 2017, and since 2019 also bait spoken in a video's audio ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2017/12/18/facebook-is-clamping-down-on-posts-that-shamelessly-beg-for-your-engagement/), search).
- **Sound off [E, dated].** Publishers reported ~85% silent Facebook views in 2016 ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/), search); TikTok is the opposite. Design for both.

**Consensus [O]:** completion, rewatch and *sends* matter most; likes least. Feeds test a new video on a small cold audience first ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length), search), so the first seconds decide whether it reaches a second one.

## 4. Faux out-of-home (FOOH) CGI

**What happened [E].** Jacquemus posted an 8 s clip of bag-cars on Rue Auber in April 2023. Maybelline's lash train and bus followed in July 2023. A trade piece dates the FOOH boom to Maybelline ([fooh.com/Digitaling summary](https://www.digitaling.com/articles/1037567.html), search).

**Why it worked**, in the makers' words:
- Ian Padgham (Jacquemus): "Probably half of the people who see them either think it's real or can't tell and that's what drives engagement" ([PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/jacquemus-purse-cars), fetched).
- Per Marketing Brew: success "toes the line of being out there but just realistic enough", and ideally only about 10% of the video is 3D ([Marketing Brew](https://www.marketingbrew.com/stories/2023/10/26/why-some-brands-are-embracing-fake-out-of-home), fetched).

Recipe: a real, recognisable place shot handheld; one impossible event; the product at architectural scale doing what it does (lashes get brushed).

**Saturation [E, weak].** By May 2025 an industry blog called balloons, banners, helicopters, drones and parachutes clichés ("Just spectacle without context. Pretty, but forgettable."), praising e.l.f.'s pimple patch on Mount Rushmore because the object *is* the product story ([fooh.com](https://www.fooh.com/blog/overused-cgi-elements), fetched). No study measures the decline.

**The honesty line.** Padgham also says deception "always backfires because then people just feel let down". His target is "Okay, this isn't real. But it would be cool if it was." Our reading [O]:
- Ambiguity about *staging* is fine when the event is impossible on its face.
- Ambiguity about *what the product can do* is not.

**For our engine [O].** Image and video layers exist (`src`, [spec-reference](../spec-reference.md)), so with a real plate the recipe works. Without one, a synthetic "real street" loses plausibility: lean on the product at impossible scale doing its own job.

## 5. Viral for the wrong reasons

- **Apple "Crush!"**: Blendtec destroys a coveted object to prove itself; Crush destroyed creative tools to sell a creative tool. *Which side the viewer is on* decides whether the same device reads as delight or grief.
- **Humane AI Pin**: two wrong answers appeared *in the launch video itself*: an eclipse location and almond protein ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/137771/humane-ai-pin-demo-false-results), search). Then came MKBHD's "The Worst Product I've Ever Reviewed… For Now", with 3.6M views in 2 days ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/marques-brownlees-humane-ai-pin-review), search).
- **Rabbit R1**: 10k units in 24 h at CES ([MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2024/01/11/rabbit-r1-sold-10000-24-hours/), search), then its launcher ran on a Pixel ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/rabbit-r1-is-an-android-app-3438805/), search).
- **Devin / 1X NEO**: capability demos later disputed (Upwork task; teleoperation) ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/346648/humanoid-hype-meets-reality-1xs-20k-home-robot-draws-skepticism-memes), search).
- **Peloton "The Gift That Gives Back"** (2019): read as a husband wanting his wife thinner. Market value fell $942M in one day, ~$1.5B over three ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2019/12/04/peloton-stock-falls-after-backlash-gift-that-gives-ad/), search). Some analysts attribute the drop to post-IPO drift.
- **Coca-Cola AI "Holidays Are Coming"** (2024): "soulless", with glitchy, uncanny people ([Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/creativity/how-coca-colas-ai-holiday-ad-went-from-praise-to-rage/), search). Visible AI tells read as cheapness, which matters for an agent.
- **Jaguar, Friend, Cluely**: huge reach, and the retold sentence was the critic's sentence.

**Common thread [O].** The shareable moment gets retold by hostile viewers too. If its cruelest one-sentence version is damning, it will spread in that version.

## 6. Implications for an agent skill

1. **One sentence, one event.** Write the shareable moment in ≤ 15 words saying something visibly *happens* ("a phone falls through a glass tower and lands unscathed"), not an adjective or claim. Test: a stranger shown only those 2 s writes that sentence back. [O, from §1]
2. **Moment timing.** In cuts of 15 s or less, the moment or the product is on screen by 3.0 s. In longer cuts, a first version of the moment lands by 3 s and the full payoff comes by 40% of the runtime. [E: TikTok 63%/3 s; O for the 40%]
3. **Frame 0 is a poster.** No black, fade-in, logo sting or title card: frame 0 shows the product or the anomaly, motion starts by frame 2, and frame 0 at 25% size reads as intriguing. [O; ABCD "surprising… early", see `launch-research.md`]
4. **Proof inside the spectacle.** The moment must demonstrate a true product property: precision, speed, durability, scale, what it produces. Test: delete the product, and the moment no longer makes sense. [O, from Volvo, Blendtec, Purple, fooh.com]
5. **High-arousal register.** Aim for awe (impossible scale, precision, transformation) or amusement. Never sadness, and never contempt for something the audience loves. Check: name the one target emotion in the brief. [E: Berger & Milkman; Crush]
6. **One "how did they do that?" shot.** At least one shot that is plausible in look and impossible in event. Ray-traced material and real-world lighting are what make it plausible. [E, weak: Padgham, Marketing Brew; O]
7. **Length per platform.** TikTok and Reels: 15–34 s (TikTok's 21–34 s data). Shorts: 15–45 s. X: 20–60 s, native upload. Teasers: 6–10 s. Never over 180 s on Reels. [E: TikTok, Instagram; O for Shorts and X]
8. **Loop.** In vertical cuts, the last 0.5 s matches the first frame's composition, motion direction and sound, so a replay looks seamless. The end card is 1.5 s or less, or overlaid on the payoff; the CTA lives in the caption. [E: replays count on Shorts and TikTok; O for the rule]
9. **Mute test.** With audio off, a viewer can state the moment and the product name from picture and on-screen text alone. Key text appears by 2 s, is ≥ 4% of frame height, and stays inside the centre safe area (clear of the bottom 20% and top 10%). [E: TikTok text-in-7 s lift, Digiday; O for the numbers]
10. **Sound earns the unmute.** The moment has a synced sound event: an impact, a drop, a silence-then-hit. Energetic cuts run ≥ 120 BPM. No speech is required to understand the film. [E: TikTok creative tips]
11. **Native per aspect.** Compose 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9 as separate layouts with their own framing, never crops. The moment is legible in each. [E: TikTok 9:16 lifts]
12. **Send test.** Name who a viewer would DM this to, and why it makes the sender look good (first to know, insider, funny). If you can't, it optimises likes, not sends. [E: Mosseri, X's share/DM signals; O for the test]
13. **Remixable unit.** Leave one reusable element: a format line ("Will it ___?"), a sound, or a repeatable visual gag that others can redo with their own subject. [O: Old Spice, Sora replies, Prompt Theory]
14. **Hostile retell.** Write the cruelest one-sentence description a critic could post. If it's damning ("they crushed art", "it's a person in a VR headset", "a car ad with no car"), change the film. Also scan for visible AI tells, such as warped hands or garbled text. [O, from §5]
15. **Honesty floor.** Nothing is shown as the product doing what it can't do today. No invented numbers, quotes, reviews, press logos, or real people or brands. Staged or CGI events must be impossible on their face, or captioned. No "comment YES" or "tag a friend" bait. [E: Humane, Rabbit, Devin, NEO, Meta's bait demotion]

Outside the agent's control (tell the user; don't fake it): seeding, endorsers, posting time, founder presence, product quality.
