# Launch films that look designed, not generated

Research notes (Nov 2026) on why some launch films look expensive and others look synthetic, drawn from studios, designers and critics. Related docs: [`ai-tells.md`](ai-tells.md) (tells by medium), [`launch-research.md`](launch-research.md) (idea, structure, end card) and [`3d-design.md`](3d-design.md). Quotation marks are used only for text read on the cited page. Fields a source does not describe are marked *not stated*.

## 0. The finding

In the best-documented pieces, **the visual system comes from what the product does or is made of**. It is not a borrowed scene. Pentagram's Intrinsic arm is driven by the software's own functions. TwelveLabs moves like its model reads video. Spline's film was built in Spline, ROLI's mark is its five touch gestures, and Dropbox's campaign shows real customers' real folders. Mitch Paone says kinetic identity "is built on behavior rather than appearance" ([Paone, 2025](https://mitchpaone.substack.com/p/time-is-the-material-from-motion)). Our hypothesis for the rejected film: a ray-traced marching band on a football field does the opposite, a metaphor scene rendered as a near-real place. Architectural critics call that the uncanny slope: "improbably perfect grass", "an implausible amount of stillness", and people "who can look oddly stiff and unnatural" ([Maganga, ArchDaily 2021](https://www.archdaily.com/959563/architectural-rendering-and-the-slippery-slope-of-the-uncanny-valley)).

A second finding: **the "anti-AI" look is now a default as well.** Asked to look less generated, models produce "warm cream with a terracotta accent, or near-black with one acid-green highlight", plus "high-contrast serif display" ([Capital & Compute, Sep 2026](https://capitalandcompute.net/blog/fix-ai-slop-design/); see also Wired on AI serifs, `ai-tells.md` [R7]). Two of our rejected looks, cream paper with an editorial serif and a warm dark ground with a glowing serif, are that second wave. Grounding the film in the product escapes it; a new palette does not.

## 1. Studios and pieces: the visual systems

Few sources name easing or sound.

1. **ManvsMachine, Apple MacBook Air M2 (2022)** ([Stash](https://www.stashmedia.tv/manvsmachine-supercharges-the-new-macbook-air-m2-for-apple/)). A 45-s film made "using only the sleek new laptop and a single feather". Stash's verdict is "excitement from restraint", set against the studio's earlier "visual mayhem". Product: the hero object plus one prop. Type and sound: *not stated*.
2. **ManvsMachine, iMac Pro "Algorithmic Architecture" (2018)** ([It's Nice That](https://www.itsnicethat.com/news/manvsmachine-algorithmic-architecture-imac-pro-film-210318); [studio page](https://mvsm.com/project/imac-pro)). The film runs on "a base algorithmic structure that served as a blueprint for each form and movement". The architecture is CG with "high-resolution photographic textures", in symmetrical, radiating framings, and Apple required it to be made on iMac Pros only. The CG is abstract architecture, not a simulated human event.
3. **Ordinary Folk, Spline (2022)** ([Stash](https://www.stashmedia.tv/?p=85806)). Stash notes that "very few" brand explainers "actually use the featured product to create the finished visuals"; this one does. It mixes 2D and 3D animation, with original music and sound design (Ambrose Yu) and voice-over. Palette and type: *not stated*.
4. **Collins, Dropbox identity (2017)** ([case study](https://wearecollins.com/work/dropbox)). A "Motion Signature" that "transforms static brand elements into instantly recognizable kinetic expressions". The logo is built from identical diamonds, with Sharp Type as the type partner and illustration collaborations. The claim: "a consistent motion language... amplifies brand distinctiveness, deepens recall".
5. **Instrument, Dropbox brand campaign** ([case study](https://instrument.com/work/dropbox-brand-campaign)). The product appears through real files. Billboards show customers' actual Dropbox folders, such as Toro y Moi's "new album coming soon". The system is "a simple and scalable design language", aiming at "a hyper-local, undeniably human body of work".
6. **Pentagram (Hudson-Powell), Intrinsic** ([case study](https://www.pentagram.com/work/intrinsic)). A web tool renders a semi-abstract robot arm "generatively animated through inverse kinematics to replicate real-world robotic motion". Its "behavioural parameters represent the key functions" of the software (Simulation, Sensing, Learning, Collaboration). Colour: a primary grey with a saturated secondary palette. Type: Diatype, with default and semi-mono widths for business and engineer audiences. Gradient "echoes" preview future positions.
7. **Pentagram, TwelveLabs** ([case study](https://pentagram.com/work/twelvelabs/story)). The premise: "If TwelveLabs' models perceive time spatially, the brand should too." The symbol is a Muybridge horse, and diagrams show how the models work. The motion rule is "No idle loops. No decorative transitions."
8. **Pentagram, MIND (cybersecurity)** ([case study](https://www.pentagram.com/work/mind)). One motion idea carries the brand: a swirl that tells the story of catching and holding down a breach. Type is Klim's Söhne.
9. **DixonBaxi, ROLI (2024)** ([It's Nice That](https://itsnicethat.com/articles/dixon-baxi-roli-rebrand-graphic-design-project-091024)). For "a technology-led brand", the identity is "ultimately tech-avoidant, instead opting for a more tactile aesthetic". The mark's five lines are the five gestures used on ROLI instruments, and they drive the motion language. The palette is "inspired by the Circle of Fifths".
10. **Gretel, Mode** ([case study](https://gretelny.com/mode)). "Shifting modes is the core behavior of the identity system." The logo uses the variable typeface WT Zaft², the palette is a family of analogous greens, and modular shapes let the brand "switch from exploratory to analytical expressions".
11. **Koto, Mews** ([Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/news/koto-just-proved-that-design-for-enterprise-platforms-doesnt-have-to-be-beige/)). Söhne set in "all-caps, extreme scale and tight leading", in hot pink, black and white. A low/mid/high framework runs from "a quiet product interface to a high-impact outdoor campaign". On the mark: "The mark becomes a grammar, not just a signature."
12. **Wolff Olins, OVO (2023)** ([Design Week](https://www.designweek.co.uk/issues/17-july-21-july-2023/wolff-olins-ovo-identity/)). Three motion principles, with the brand's square carried into the photography. Wolff Olins' Yiting Nan warns of "applying motion where it doesn't serve a purpose or overwhelms the message" and wants "motion behaviour [that] mirrors the brand's strategic thinking" ([interview](https://wolffolins.com/news/inside-wolff-olins-yiting-nan-on-motion-design)).
13. **Tendril × Builders Club, Meta AI glasses** ([Stash](https://www.stashmedia.tv/?p=122589)). Ten standalone CG gags in "a modular visual universe where everyday domestic items meet high fashion", with "soft color gradients and pearlescent textures". The aim is "wit and sophisticated seduction" rather than technical utility. Product: an accessory on pedestals and dressed tables, never a spec diagram.
14. **BUCK, Airbnb "The Grand Adventure" (2025)** ([case study](https://buck.co/work/airbnb-experiences)). 2D and 3D with "shallow depth of field" and environmental micro-motion (wind in grass, pollen, dripping water). No UI is shown. Technique is *not stated*. Our reading of the credits (a 2D animation director plus 3D teams) is a stylised world, not simulated footage.
15. **Vercel Ship 2025** ([Vercel blog](https://vercel.com/blog/designing-and-building-the-vercel-ship-conference-platform); [basement.studio](https://basement.studio/post/behind-the-ship) partners on Ship). It started from ferrofluid and ended at "A near-metallic liquid system. Reflective and grounded." The imagery was generated (15,000+ images and videos with Flux, Veo 2, Runway and Ideogram), held together by a model trained "to output consistent lighting, form, and material". The team pulled back from oil-slick and prism colour because it got "Too far, too shiny, and too loud", and removed spikes after reviewers reported trypophobia. Generated imagery can pass when one material and lighting model is locked.
16. **Apple / TBWA\Media Arts Lab, "The Mighty Mac mini" (2026)** ([DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/apple-mighty-mac-mini-m6-claymation-tbwa)). Director Nicos Livesey, with stop motion via Blinkink and Stoopid Buddy. A 40-s claymation where the clay Mac mini transforms to act out the spec sheet (arms for CPU, a brain for AI). The making-of says no AI tools were used.
17. **Linear (in-house), 2024 UI redesign** ([Linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-redesigned-the-linear-ui)). The team moved from 98 theme variables to three (base, accent, contrast) in LCH. They limited the accent colour to "return to a more neutral and timeless appearance" and use Inter Display for headings with Inter for body. On alignment: it is "something that you'll feel after a few minutes". Its launch demos keep the real UI full screen, with the presenter in a corner bubble ([Moonb roundup](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video)).

## 2. Principles of premium motion

**Derive, don't decorate.** Paone: "treat motion as a system rather than an effect"; "Typography provides structural stability, allowing motion to remain systematic rather than stylistic" ([Paone](https://mitchpaone.substack.com/p/time-is-the-material-from-motion)). Pieces 2, 3, 6, 7, 9 and 10 each map one product truth to one visual rule.

**Restraint.** Apple's HIG: "Add motion purposefully, supporting the experience without overshadowing it. Don't add motion for the sake of adding motion" ([HIG Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)). Vercel's guidelines: animate only "when it clarifies cause & effect or when it adds deliberate delight" ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/design/guidelines)). BUCK's Yker Moreno calls maximalism "decorative chaos masking minimal intent" ([BUCK 2026](https://pencilsdown.buck.co/p/looking-ahead-bucks-2026-predictions)).

**Hierarchy, grid, few typefaces.** Vercel: "Every element aligns with something intentionally." A widely shared breakdown of Apple product videos gives these rules: "A couple typefaces max"; "One shot = one idea"; "All the color lives on the product or the UI"; "Apple's cuts almost disappear" ([@leomeethewoo via YouMind](https://youmind.com/landing/x-viral-articles/apple-product-video-design-secrets); practitioner commentary, not Apple).

**Easing.** Kowalski: for things entering or exiting, "use `ease-out`"; don't animate from `scale(0)`, start at "(0.9+)"; make motion "origin-aware" so it scales "in from the trigger" ([7 tips](https://emilkowal.ski/ui/7-practical-animation-tips)). Vercel: "Easing fits the subject." Comeau: a spring "slows to a stop in a way that CSS transitions can't replicate" ([Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/animation/a-friendly-introduction-to-spring-physics/)). Material 3 separates *standard* and *emphasized* families, each with accelerate and decelerate variants ([Flutter `Easing`](https://api.flutter.dev/flutter/material/Easing-class.html)). One caveat: Kowalski's "under `300ms`" is for UI response to input. Film beats are longer, so carry over the curve and origin rules, not the durations.

**Real UI, cropped, with honest content.** Use "your real app UI", and "Zoom in on the part of the UI where the action happens, then zoom out to restore context" ([Evil Martians, 2025](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/three-smart-ways-to-highlight-features-for-landing-pages-or-launch-weeks)). "Obvious demo content" will "give off an artificial vibe". Keep the window the same size and position across clips, and use smooth trackpad scrolling ([Automattic, 2019](https://automattic.design/2019/11/12/good-ui-demo-videos/)). Rauno Freiberg: "Great interactions are modeled after properties from the real world, like interruptability" ([Rauno](https://rauno.me/craft/interaction-design)).

**Realism, CG and material honesty.** Near-real CG fails on perfection and stillness (Maganga, above). Generative video fails on faces and physics: the Coca-Cola holiday spots drew complaints about uncanny humans and flickering wheels ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/coca-cola-faces-backlash-again-for-missing-the-spirit-of-the-holidays-with-another-ai-slop-ad)), and the Toys"R"Us Sora film about unnatural movement and inconsistent characters ([NBC](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/internet/toys-r-us-ai-video-ad-controversy-explained-commercial-rcna159030)). Studios are answering with visible craft. "As more AI-generated content infiltrates our feeds, the more we yearn for handmade craftiness" ([BUCK, Jun 2026](https://pencilsdown.buck.co/p/the-return-of-stop-motion)). Head of 3D Alex Dingfelder says "low frame rates, painterly boil, and halftones" can now be used on purpose ([BUCK 2026](https://pencilsdown.buck.co/p/looking-ahead-bucks-2026-predictions)). Counterweight: ManvsMachine, Tendril and BUCK all ship CG. Their CG shows **objects, abstract systems or stylised worlds**, not a photoreal simulation of a human event. The evidence that CG of real-world scenes looks cheap is thin (one architecture essay plus the generative-video backlash), but it is consistent.

**Sound.** "The music is premium and locked to the cut"; "SFX almost aren't there. Only the hits that matter" (YouMind breakdown, above).

## 3. Tells of AI slop and template motion (2024–2026)

- **Indigo-to-violet gradients**, which trace back to Tailwind's `bg-indigo-500` default ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website); [925 Studios](https://www.925studios.co/blog/ai-slop-design-tells): "the single loudest AI tell in 2026").
- **Inter everywhere** with no display face ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/built-with-ai-trust-signals-2026)). Anthropic calls this distributional convergence: unguided, a model "will almost always conform to Inter fonts, purple gradients on white backgrounds, and minimal animations" ([Anthropic, Nov 2025](https://claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)).
- **The "Linear effect"**: dark ground, blurred gradients behind product visuals, animated glowing artefacts ([Daryl Ginn](https://rectangle.substack.com/p/the-linear-effect)).
- **Reflexive glass and glow**, "backdrop-blur panels and glow with no reason", and **motion on everything**: "The same fade-and-slide-up on every section, bounce easing, count-up stats" ([Capital & Compute](https://capitalandcompute.net/blog/fix-ai-slop-design/)).
- **Plastic 3D**: smooth, symmetrical renders and blobs with unnatural lighting ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/built-with-ai-trust-signals-2026)). The gradient blob is "soft, orbital, vaguely optimistic" ([Donska, Built In 2026](https://builtin.com/articles/ai-design-slop-era)).
- **No real proof**: no real product screens, numbers or people (Utsubo).
- **Second-wave "tasteful" defaults**: cream + terracotta, near-black + one acid accent, high-contrast serif (Capital & Compute; `ai-tells.md` R7).
- **Generative-video artefacts**: uncanny faces, disjointed bodies, unstable objects (Coca-Cola, Toys"R"Us, above).
- *Our observation, unsourced:* floating and orbiting devices, isometric office scenes, synthwave grids, bokeh fields, and per-letter bounces on every word recur in template packs. Treat them as likely tells, not as citations.

## Implications for an agent skill

Each rule is checkable on the spec or on frames. Numbers in rules 5, 6, 8, 10 and 14 are our heuristics, not sourced values. The rules complement `launch-research.md` §Implications. Regression: the band fails 1–3; cream/serif and dark/glowing serif fail 4; fogged terrain fails 2; chrome type on a cyc fails 2 and 4; tiles/dominoes fail 2–3.

1. **Subject rule.** Every shot's subject is the product (real UI, its output, the object), a diagram of how it works, or type stating the claim. Fail a shot whose subject is a simulated real-world place or event (field, stadium, office, crowd, band, street), unless it is drawn in a clearly non-photoreal illustration style.
2. **CG blacklist.** With 3D or ray tracing, count these: humans, animals, plants, grass, terrain or landscape, fog banks, water, food, metallic or chrome extruded type on a sweep or cyc, and generic physics props used as metaphor (dominoes, tiles, balls, cloth, marbles). The count must be 0 unless the brief supplies footage. 3D is for the product object and for abstract forms driven by a product parameter.
3. **Derivation line.** The spec states one mapping, *product behaviour → visual rule* (Intrinsic, TwelveLabs, ROLI), and every scene cites it. It fails if a competitor's logo could be swapped in.
4. **Banned looks.** Reject indigo→violet gradients, cream + terracotta, near-black + one acid accent, any serif that glows or sits on paper as the hero, chrome type, and glass or glow without a depth reason. Colours come from brand assets or sampled product screenshots.
5. **Type.** At most 2 families (one may be mono) and at most 3 sizes per frame. Every text box snaps to a declared column grid with fixed outer margins of at least 5% of the width.
6. **Colour lives on the product.** Saturated pixels (OKLCH chroma > 0.1) outside the product, the UI or one accent stay under 10% of the frame.
7. **Real UI only.** UI comes from supplied screenshots or components. Invented metrics, names and charts: 0. Demo content is coherent, never placeholders.
8. **Crop and cursor.** Zoom 1.6–2× onto the element that changes, then return to context at most once per beat. At most one cursor, on eased arcs, settling at least 0.25 s before a click, with a UI response within 4 frames.
9. **Easing vocabulary.** At most 3 named curves. Entries use ease-out or a critically damped spring, exits run faster than entries, and moves use ease-in-out. No `linear` except constant scrolls. Scale starts at 0.9 or more, and overshoot stays at or under 5% unless the product causes the event.
10. **Duration tiers.** At most 3 tiers (e.g. 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.6 s, scaled to tempo), and every animation uses one of them.
11. **No idle motion.** Nothing moves without a cause: no float, drift, glow pulse, particle haze or camera wobble. Each shot has 1 primary mover and at most 1 secondary. This agrees with `taste/TASTE.md` ("Idle wobble") and overrides the "Life in density" advice in `finish-and-life.md` for product and UI shots.
12. **One shot, one idea.** Hold each shot at least as long as it takes to read, cut on bar lines, and keep the SFX count at or below the number of product state changes.
13. **Depth by layering** (our observation). Build depth from the overlap, scale and parallax of flat planes. Use a perspective camera only on 3D objects, and never orbit a flat screen.
14. **Deliberate imperfection.** Use at most one texture (grain, halftone, boil), applied to the whole film. CG materials need roughness or albedo variation. A hold longer than 2 s must not be pixel-frozen: grain or a caused change satisfies this, drift does not.
15. **Artificiality check.** Product timing follows `launch-research.md` rule 3. Show a fresh critic 6 stills and ask: "Does any frame look like a simulation, a template or AI?" and "What does the product do?". Ship only on *no* and a correct answer.

Note: `3d-design.md` proposes `sweep`, `field` and `terrain` set builders. Under rules 1–2, these are not launch-film subjects.
