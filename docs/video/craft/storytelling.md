# Storytelling in 10–30 seconds

Scope: hooks, structures, reveals, payoff and sound for 10–30 s pieces (ads, idents, stings, title-like films) rendered from JSON specs.
Confidence tags: **[P]** primary source (the platform's or practitioner's own document). **[S]** secondary report of a primary study. Platform numbers are often vendor data, so treat them as direction, not physics. **[H]** heuristic distilled here, not taken from a source.

## 1. Principles (why short form works the way it does)

- **The decision happens in under 2 seconds.** Facebook's "Capturing Attention in Feed" study (850+ video ads, Nielsen Brand Effects validation) found that people recall mobile-feed content at a statistically significant rate after **0.25 s** of exposure. Average time per item is about **1.7 s on mobile** and 2.5 s on desktop. [S, Social Media Today on the Facebook IQ report] Widely repeated from the same Facebook/Nielsen work: up to **47%** of a video campaign's value is delivered in the first 3 s and up to **74%** in the first 10 s. [S, Smart Insights; the primary PDF was not found, so treat it as approximate] Frame 1 is already the story.
- **Most viewers see it muted first.** In the Facebook study's muted first-10-second pass, only **46%** of ads had an easily identifiable brand link and **23%** a clear brand message. [S] A related summary: only **24%** were understandable without sound. [S, MarketingProfs] Then again, on TikTok **88%** of users say sound is "vital" to the experience, and TikTok/Kantar report that sound-on ads do better. [P, TikTok "Evolution of Sound"; vendor data] Design for both: **picture-locked story, sound as amplifier**.
- **Memory needs brief but real attention, and distinctive assets cut the time needed.** Karen Nelson-Field's earlier threshold for memory encoding was **2.5 s** of active attention. Her later work with Amplified found **1.5 s** is enough *if* the ad has distinctive, fluent brand assets. Lumen/Havas data: under **0.5 s** of attention per ad, aggregation never builds awareness. [S, Advertising Week; The Media Leader] For a 15 s piece the core image or mark must be readable in a single 1.5–2.5 s hold, not in a flicker.
- **The opening shapes the whole.** Google's ABCD framework (Attract, Brand, Connect, Direct) is built on Google/Ipsos/Kantar analysis. Ads that follow it show **+30% short-term sales likelihood** and **+17% long-term brand contribution** (Google/Kantar 2021, n=11,000 ads). [P, Google] Google's guidance is to show the brand within the first 5 s. Facebook's meta-analysis reportedly found **+23% brand recall** when the brand appears in the first 3 s. [S, Brandwatch] Studio counterpoint: Channel 4's 2004 idents and John Lewis delay or hide the brand, so the brand becomes the payoff. Both work: brand-as-frame vs brand-as-reveal (§2.3).
- **Short can carry story, but simplified.** YouTube's 6 s bumpers drove significant ad-recall lift in about 9 of 10 campaigns (605-study meta-analysis, average lift +30–38%). [P, Think with Google] Netflix's 6 s cut of a 30 s trailer gave +65% ad recall on mobile. [P, Think with Google APAC] Unruly's read of Google's six-second SXSW challenge (classic novels retold in six seconds): the best used simplified narrative or no narrative at all. [S, Unruly] Six seconds holds *one* turn, fifteen holds a setup, turn and button, and thirty holds a small arc.
- **Story and people beat abstraction.** Orlando Wood/System1: right-brain features (characters, dialogue, place, implicit glances, story) predict effectiveness. Left-brain features (abstraction, flat claims, words on screen) predict weak scores. Recurring characters or "fluent devices" add emotional impact. [S, System1] Binet & Field: emotional work drives bigger long-term effects. [S, IPA]
- **Story = change.** Kenn Adams's Story Spine (taught at Pixar, popularised as rule #4 of Emma Coats's 22 rules): *Once upon a time… Every day… One day… Because of that… Because of that… Until finally… Ever since then…* [P, NPR interview with Adams] For 15 s, compress it to **Every day → One day → Until finally**: status quo, disruption, new state.

## 2. Techniques and procedures

### 2.1 The first 1–3 seconds (hook) [H, built on the data above]
- **Frame 0 is never empty or a fade from black.** Open on the most interesting image, mid-motion, at full contrast. The 0.25 s recall finding means the first frame is already being judged.
- **Pose a question in ≤1.5 s**: something incomplete, wrong, too big or too small, or a single object in a strange place. Ask "what is that?" or "what happens next?"
- **The first meaningful event lands by 0.5–1.0 s.** The proposition (what this is about) is clear by 3 s, per TikTok's relayed creative guidance. [S, vendor relays of TikTok guidance]
- **Hook types**: (a) impossible image (the C4 4 assembling); (b) a process already in motion (Cog: start mid-chain); (c) a character with a want (the Long Wait boy staring at a clock); (d) a type statement that provokes, set as an image and not as a caption; (e) a sound event before picture (a hit or drop at frame 0). With a muted autoplay, (e) must have a visual twin.

### 2.2 Beat budgets (measurable)
These are heuristics [H], cross-checked against the Facebook value curve and bumper findings:

| Length | Beats | Shape | Hold for key image |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 s | 2 (setup → turn) | one idea, one twist | ≥1.5 s |
| 10 s | 3 | hook → turn → button | ≥1.5–2 s |
| 15 s | 3–5 | hook (0–2 s) · develop (2–9 s) · turn (9–12 s) · payoff/button (12–15 s) | 2–2.5 s on the end card or mark |
| 30 s | 5–8 | hook · world · escalation ×2–3 · turn · payoff · button | 2.5–3 s end |

- **Information per shot**: one new idea per cut. The eye needs about 0.5–1 s to find the focal point after a cut, so cuts shorter than about 0.4 s read as texture, not information. Use them for rhythm, not story. [H]
- **Read time for on-screen type**: allow about 0.3 s per word plus 0.5 s, and keep it to ≤7 words per card. [H, common subtitling practice]
- **Last 20% of the duration = payoff + button.** Never spend the end on logo boilerplate alone.

### 2.3 Brand-as-frame vs brand-as-reveal
- **Frame**: brand or product visible in the first 3–5 s (Google ABCD, Facebook +23%). Suits direct response and new brands.
- **Reveal**: the brand *is* the twist or completion (the C4 4 forms for less than 1 s, then the camera moves on; John Lewis's final line). Suits idents and emotional pieces. Distinctive assets (colour, shape, sound logo) still need to be present throughout, so the 1.5 s encoding finding still applies.

### 2.4 Making 15 s feel complete [H, with examples]
1. **Close the question you opened.** The hook's question must be answered *visibly*.
2. **Rhyme the end with the start.** Repeat the first frame's composition or motion, transformed. This creates a loop or bookend.
3. **Land on the musical downbeat.** The payoff hits on the bar line, and the button is a small after-beat (a tail, a breath, one last gag, a sound).
4. **The button**: a short, unexpected extra beat after the payoff. Examples: the Old Spice ending ("I'm on a horse"); Skittles' deadpan coda.
5. **Resolve motion.** Everything settles, with physics coming to rest and particles dissipating. Unresolved motion reads as "cut off".

### 2.5 Sound's role
- Williams (title design) starts from the song that sets the tone. [P, Frame.io] Honda "Grrr" was pitched by performing its jingle live, and Adweek named it jingle of the decade. [S, Wikipedia; W+K London]
- Use sound for **causality** (a hit for each impact, as in Cog's chain), **time** (the Surfer drumbeat building tension), **POV** (the *Se7en* industrial score for the killer's mind) and **brand** (sonic logo as a distinctive asset).
- Picture should edit *to* the music grid: cut on beats, land big events on downbeats, put silence right before the reveal. A drop works only if something stops first. [H]

## 3. Catalogue of structures (≥15)
| # | Structure | How it works in 10–30 s | Exemplar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Chain reaction / cascade** | Each event causes the next; payoff is the last domino | Honda "Cog" (W+K London 2003) |
| 2 | **The wait / tension then release** | Delay the thing everyone expects; make waiting the virtue | Guinness "Surfer" (AMV BBDO 1999) |
| 3 | **Motive reversal** | The audience assumes a want; the end reveals the opposite | John Lewis "The Long Wait" (2011) |
| 4 | **Story through an interface** | The whole story told in a found medium (searches, texts, forms) | Google "Parisian Love" (2010) |
| 5 | **One-take transformation** | Continuous camera; the set changes around a constant hero; escalating absurdity | Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like" (W+K 2010) |
| 6 | **Deadpan absurd premise** | Accept one impossible rule, play it straight, follow its consequence | Skittles "Touch" (TBWA 2007) |
| 7 | **Hidden-form reveal / anamorphic** | Scene elements align into a mark for one moment | Channel 4 idents (2004) |
| 8 | **System variations** | Same mark, many personalities or materials; each is a micro-story | BBC Two "2"s (Lambie-Nairn 1991) |
| 9 | **Conversion anthem** | Negative to positive in song; the enemy becomes the motive | Honda "Grrr" (2004) |
| 10 | **Seed then reveal (series)** | A 6 s tease sets up a longer story later | YouTube bumper sequencing guidance |
| 11 | **Inside the mind / maker's POV** | Show the process of someone obsessive; the audience infers the character | *Se7en* titles (Cooper 1995) |
| 12 | **Single animated symbol** | One graphic metaphor evolves; no plot needed | *The Man with the Golden Arm* (Bass 1955) |
| 13 | **Fused portrait / place** | Character and world merge (double exposure, landscape-in-face) | *True Detective* titles (Clair) |
| 14 | **Map / world-building** | Camera travels a model of the world; the structure is the geography | *Game of Thrones* titles (Elastic) |
| 15 | **Loop** | The last frame becomes the first; the satisfaction is seamlessness | [H] social-native format |
| 16 | **List / escalation** | 3–5 examples, each bigger, with the last breaking the pattern (rule of three + twist) | [H] |
| 17 | **Problem → solution demo** | Show the pain in 3 s, the fix in 5 s, the result held | [H] DR standard; matches ABCD |
| 18 | **Countdown / clock** | Explicit time pressure gives structure for free | *24* titles concept (Frame.io) |
| 19 | **Scale shift / zoom** | Macro to cosmic, or the reverse; the reveal is context | [H] (*Powers of Ten* lineage) |
| 20 | **Before/after match cut** | One cut does all the storytelling; rhyme of shapes | [H] |

Choose a structure *from the idea*, not at random. Across a set of pieces, vary the structures. Using the same hook → montage → logo every time is the "boring" failure.

## 4. What separates award work from amateur work
- **Amateur**: a logo animates in, with feature text cards and stock music behind them. Award work: a *situation* with a character, place or causal chain, with the brand fused into the event (Cog, C4).
- **Amateur**: even pacing throughout. Award work: rhythm with contrast, i.e. long hold, burst, silence, hit (Surfer's wait vs release).
- **Amateur**: explains. Award work trusts inference, as with *Se7en*'s notebooks and Parisian Love's queries implying the whole relationship.
- **Amateur**: the ending fades out. Award work ends with a payoff plus a button and a rhyme with the opening.
- **Amateur**: the music is wallpaper. Award work picks music that *is* the idea (the Grrr song, the Surfer drums).
- **Amateur**: every piece uses the same template. Award systems have one rule and endless variation (BBC Two 2s, C4 idents).

## 5. Anti-patterns
- Opening on black, on a slow fade, or on a logo. This wastes the 0.25–1.7 s decision window.
- Text explaining what the picture already shows. Words on screen are a left-brain feature (Wood).
- More than one turn in 15 s. The viewer can't hold two reversals.
- A reveal nobody was set up to want. A twist needs a planted expectation (the Long Wait plants "impatient kid").
- Key images held for less than 1.5 s. They won't encode (Nelson-Field).
- Sound-dependent story with no visual twin (the 46%/23% muted findings).
- Ending mid-motion, or with no closure at all.
- Montage of nice-looking unrelated shots: "a reel, not a story".

## 6. How an agent can check its own work
1. **Frame-0 test**: render frame 0 alone. Is it intriguing and on-subject? Is the main subject identifiable at 0.25 s?
2. **Seconds to first event**: should be ≤1.0 s. Proposition clear by ≤3 s.
3. **Muted test**: describe the story from the frames only. Does it still make sense?
4. **Beat count**: list the beats with timestamps. 15 s → 3–5 beats; exactly one turn.
5. **Hold audit**: the key image, mark or payoff is held ≥1.5 s (aim for 2–2.5 s).
6. **Spine test**: fill in *Every day / One day / Until finally*. If you can't, there is no story. A pure loop or system piece is fine if that was chosen on purpose.
7. **Setup–payoff ledger**: every payoff has a plant before it; every plant is paid off.
8. **Ending test**: does the last 20% contain payoff + button + settled motion? Does it rhyme with the opening?
9. **Sync test**: list the major events and check they fall on beat or bar positions of the generated music.
10. **Variety test across a batch**: no two pieces share a structure from §3 consecutively.


## 7. Gaps against Anikit (TASTE)

- **Measurable today, partly unchecked:**
  - seconds to the first meaningful event (critique has `hook` for words only);
  - the hold on the key image or end mark (≥ 1.5–2.5 s);
  - motion settled at the end (`ending` exists);
  - beats and turns per 15 s.
  - A render-time check for first-frame interest (frame 0 non-empty, high contrast) and for end-frame stillness would be cheap.
- **Rhyme of first and last frame:** the similarity of frame 0 to the last frame, judged alongside a transformation, could be offered as a tip ("the ending doesn't echo the opening") but never required: a loop is one structure among 20.
- **Structure variety across a batch** needs memory of earlier pieces. A per-project log, written by the skills (`concept.txt` plus the §3 structure number), would let agents vary structure deliberately.
- **Sound-first:** title designers start from a song. The music engine can render a score before the picture exists (`anikit-video music`), so a "score first, then cut to it" path is already possible; the skills could offer it.
