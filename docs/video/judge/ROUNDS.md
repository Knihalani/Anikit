Top gaps per brief:

- **a-social**: Needs a specific, memorable idea about this coffee (origin, ritual, process) instead of a cup and a Venn diagram · Typography needs a real point of view: scale contrast, a custom or carefully chosen face, and type that interacts with the image instead of sitting above it like a slide · Needs real material and light (liquid, crema, steam, paper) and compositions that differ from frame to frame, with choreography between them · Transitions: each note (bergamot, black tea, peach) is a separate card swap. Top work would morph one disc into the next, for example the citrus wheel rotating into the hatch and the leaf, then the three discs physically pouring into the cup, so the Venn becomes the brew rather than a static diagram.
- **b-launch**: Find an idea beyond the pun on the name; the best entry turns its subject into a visual system rather than illustrating it · Build an image with texture, light and layering, such as real rail material, signal glow and atmosphere, instead of flat vector strokes on a solid field · Make the type part of the motion and give it a stronger face and scale contrast, rather than wordmark plus caption in a repeated end-card layout · Choreography is a series of separate shots (code → yard → perspective → SETTLED → logo) linked by whoosh cuts. Nothing carries through as a match-cut: the train, the signal or the amount never travels across the cuts. Top work would carry the €12,400 car as a continuous object from the code literal to the logo rail.
- **hf-b-launch**: Find a visual idea behind 'instant settlement' (rails, a physical race, collapsing time) instead of type-on-colour slides; compare how B turns its whole subject into a visual language · Design the typography: deliberate face selection, extreme scale contrast, type that moves as an object, instead of one default sans at one weight · Build real frames with layering, material and light, and choreograph the transitions (the blurred orange block looks like a broken wipe), so each frame could stand as a poster · Choreography and pace: it plays as 2 long shots of UI state changes connected by blur dissolves. It needs ~2 s beats with designed match-cuts (track → pill → logo mark) and real hand-offs instead of defocus. The train/rail metaphor is never pushed into a kinetic peak, and energy stays flat before fizzling out on a static logo card.
- **c-event**: Take the power-station idea literally into image and material: real switchgear, sparks, light that the type itself emits. · Choreograph the reveal. Give the type changes of scale and camera moves instead of fading names into one fixed grid. · Replace the single-accent dark palette and stock texture with lit, tactile frames that each work as a poster. · Motion has no weight or variety. The switches snap linearly with no overshoot or settle, the camera never moves, nothing moves in depth, and the eight closures do not speed up. A top studio would compress the sequence, cascade the last switches together and let the system visibly power up: current running along the bus, light spilling outward, the frame shaking.
- **d-illustration**: Commit to a real visual world: hand-made texture, cut paper, real lighting on the burrow interior. The craft and material depth of A would show the step change. · Replace the slide captions with typography that has a point of view: hand-lettered or custom type living inside the scene, with scale and rhythm. · Choreograph a genuine transformation (the child's drawing physically becoming the world) instead of an opacity crossfade and a repeated establishing shot. Every frame should be a distinct poster. · Motion has no weight or choreography: almost everything is a crossfade, slow drift or sine bob, with no anticipation, overshoot or follow-through, and no motivated camera move such as pushing into the drawing or through the porthole to tie the scenes together
- **e-data**: Give the ice real material and geography (coastlines, texture, light on the surface) instead of a glowing disc; A shows how much the subject gains from real light · Break the repeated layout: choreograph camera, scale and composition changes between beats so each frame is a distinct poster · Find a specific idea and a typographic voice (a custom or characterful face, type integrated with the rings) instead of the label + serif headline + accent default · Choreography and timing: the year sweep needs a speed curve (fast through stable years, slowing into the decline years and the 2012 record) and a real held climax. The rings need overlap and follow-through as each one settles, and the closing comparison needs a staged camera push or reframe. Without these it plays as a linear data tick inside one static frame.
- **hf-e-data**: Find a visual idea that makes the loss felt (real ice texture, melt as a physical event, scale against something human) instead of a glowing blob next to a line chart · Break the single repeated layout: vary the scale, framing and camera across beats so the frames are choreographed rather than swapped · Give the type a point of view: a chosen face, expressive scale contrast, and numbers integrated into the map rather than a slide column · Choreography: the data needs to be staged as camera and editorial beats. The scrub could accelerate through the stable years, slam into 2012, hold, then release. Push the camera in on the ice edge, cut between the map, the chart and the number, and give the ice a melt or erosion behaviour with weight instead of a shrinking mask.
- **f-3d**: It needs a specific idea beyond 'macro of dial, then specs'. The hand-cut guilloché could drive the whole film, for example the engraving tool's path writing the type or the timing structure. · The light needs to be real: sapphire reflections, brushed-steel anisotropy, a light source with a story, and a darker, richer environment instead of a grey vignette. D's set lighting is the benchmark. · Drop the tracked-caps-over-serif template. Commission or choose a face with horological character and compose type and dial as one image, with varied framing across shots. · Motion is a slow push-in per shot plus a fade-up card. The one strong device, light carving the guilloché in shot 1, is never escalated or paid off. There is no build to a 60-80% peak, no choreographed hand-off between shots, and shots run up to 3.7 s with little change. A top studio would make the cutting tool's path drive each transition and the reveal.
- **g-titles**: Find an idea beyond the 'Lisbon = tiles' cliché, e.g. make the tile pattern itself demonstrate a design system (tokens, variants, breaking rules) so the subject and the visual agree · Commit to typography with a point of view: a custom or carefully drawn face integrated into the tile grid instead of a boxed lockup over the pattern · Add real ceramic material, glaze, light, imperfection and camera choreography, as in B's physical world-building, rather than a flat vector tessellation · Motion: the 'offset' concept, a tile shifted into a pattern, is good but only played once and literally. Top studios would vary the device (rotation, mirroring, colour inversion, rows sliding into place with mass and settle), stage a clear peak at about 70%, and land the title by having the tiles themselves form or reveal it. Here a card is placed on top.
- **hf-g-titles**: Build a real idea in place of a literal pun: let the azulejo tiling grow into Lisbon's material reality (glazed ceramic, crazing, real light raking across façades) the way B grows its world · Give the typography craft and a point of view: a custom or carefully chosen face that is built from the tile system itself, with scale contrast and integration, instead of default bold sans plus a drop shadow and mono labels · Choreograph change: every frame should be a distinct poster with progression and surprise, not one layout reused with the words and pattern swapped · Choreography peaks too early (the tile flood around 5–6 s, about 40%) and then stops. Top studios would let the tiles assemble the wordmark, push to a peak at 60–80% and leave a shorter, active hold with secondary motion such as tile ripples, a breathing offset shadow or the pattern drifting
- **h-ident**: It needs an idea specific to a kestrel (hovering, the stoop dive, the hunter's eye) rather than a bird in front of the sun; A builds a whole concept from one silhouette · The bird and the K mark should be one choreographed transformation, with real motion and weight, instead of a cut to black and a fade-up · Replace the default centred editorial-serif lockup and plain gradient with typography and texture that have a point of view · Choreography and idea: no transformation ties kestrel to K. A top studio would have the dive itself draw the letter (the wing becomes the K's diagonal, or the sun becomes the counter or glow), so the reveal reads as one continuous gesture rather than hold, drop, cut, card.
- **i-explainer**: There is no visual idea beyond the textbook diagram; it needs a physical or human subject such as a room, a head or a city going quiet · Typography is default and slide-like; it needs scale contrast, a chosen face and type that reacts to the sound itself · Every frame is the same dark dot-grid layout; it needs varied staging, real light and texture, and choreography between beats · Motion: the cancellation is never shown as a physical event. A top studio would choreograph the inverted wave folding in, phase-locking and collapsing to the flat line, with anticipation, overshoot and a real impact moment. Here, waves scroll past behind text cards.
- **j-music**: No typographic idea: the default serif stays at one size and one position. It needs scale contrast and type that lives in the scene the way D's neon sign is the scene · No choreography: the camera, the composition and the subject never change. It needs distinct framed beats with escalating motion · A generic gradient-dusk palette with one accent; it needs an authored colour world and real material or texture in the water and sky · Choreography: the film needs camera life (a slow push or a drift at the tide line) and a sequenced, overlapping die-off of the harbour lights timed to the lyric. As built, it is a static plate with one reveal effect.
- **k-sports**: It needs a specific concept rooted in real clubs or real places (rivalry, history, crowd) instead of generic stadium iconography · It needs a typographic system with character, either custom lettering or deliberate scale and layout contrast integrated with image, as B and D do, rather than centred default grotesk with tracked labels · It needs real material, light and texture, with photographic or 3D depth and choreographed transitions, instead of flat vector cards with an overlay effect · Choreography: shots are joined by dissolves and hard cuts rather than designed match-cuts. The idea that floodlight bulbs become the 19:45 dot-matrix digits is strong but under-used; the bridge arch and the lighthouse beam should hand off into the pitch geometry (the centre circle and halfway line) so the two cities literally meet at the centre spot, giving it real weight and follow-through
- **l-generative**: There's no specific concept or world. Replace 'word + waveform' with an idea only this piece could have, the way D's clockwork map is a model of its world. · There's no material, texture or real light. D has metal, wood, parchment and motivated sunlight, and this needs physical substance. · Make the typography custom and integrated with the image instead of a default bold sans centred in the frame. · Motion: the timing has no shape. It runs as long holds, then an evenly busy noise section, then a fade. There is no accented key pose, no anticipation before the ripple impact, and no overshoot or settle as the interference resolves into type. The mid-section is procedural jitter rather than choreographed motion.
