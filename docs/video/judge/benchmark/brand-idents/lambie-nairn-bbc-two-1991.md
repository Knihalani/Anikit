# BBC 2 Phase one 1991 — Lambie-Nairn (1991)

**Why it's in the set: the all-time classic of the "logo as a character" ident: one numeral, endless materials and behaviours, each with a single witty idea.**

oEmbed: title "BBC 2 Phase one 1991", author "The Martin Lambie-Nairn Collection" (an archive channel named for the designer; treat as the best available, not a broadcaster upload). Alt: "BBC Two 'Shadow' Ident" from the LambieNairnTube channel. Aspect: originally 4:3 broadcast (*est.*), hence `other`.

## The idea
BBC Two's controller Alan Yentob found the old logo "singularly unmemorable"; Lambie-Nairn made a sans-serif "2" in **Gill Sans** the hero, paired with **viridian** (*source*: Wikipedia). Each ident gives the 2 a material or a personality: Paint, Water, Blade, Neon, Copper Sparks, Glass, Silk, Shadow, Flowers, Powder, Optic, Car, Jumping Dog, Firecracker, Diary, Steam (16 in 1991–97, *source*). "Car" drives the 2 like a remote-controlled toy; "Jumping Dog" makes it a mechanical toy (*source*). The set ran 1991–2001 and was revived 2014–2018 (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
Each ident is one idea in one shot or a few (*est.* 5–15 s each); this 47 s upload compiles the first phase. Pace is measured; the joke or material reveal lands, then the "TWO" caption sits.

## Typography
A heavy Gill Sans "2" as a 3D physical object, with "TWO" set small beneath (*source* for Gill Sans; layout *est.* from stills).

## Colour and light
Viridian plus the natural colour of each material; studio-lit tabletop photography (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
Practical, in-camera: a physical 2 made of polystyrene, metal, glass, silk (*source*: Wikipedia lists the materials); paint thrown over it, water poured, powder blown. Real physics, real light.

## Transitions
Mostly none: single tableau, or a simple cut to the end frame (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Bell and harp-like cues in the originals, plus foley of each material (*source* for bell and harp: Wikipedia; foley *est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. One shape, many behaviours: every ident is a single legible idea in under ~10 s.
2. Material truth: paint, water and silk behave physically, so the logo has weight.
3. The system is generative (new materials = new idents) yet always recognisable.
4. Wit: the logo has a personality (dog, toy car), not just a finish.

## What Anikit would need
- `text` (Gill Sans) extruded via `scene3d`, `camera` macro moves, `post` grade.
- GAP: practical/physical simulation of paint, liquid, powder and cloth on a 3D letter (Anikit has `sim`/`fluid` only in limited form).

## Judge's checklist
- Can the idea of each ident be said in one sentence?
- Does the logo object have believable material and weight?
- Is the numeral recognisable in every frame of the end state?
- Is colour limited to one brand colour plus the material's own?
- Is there wit or personality, not only a surface effect?
