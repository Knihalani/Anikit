# MotoGP identity (motion) — Pentagram (2024)

**Why it's in the set: a motion identity by a top identity studio in which the logo, a variable typeface and the motion behaviours all come from one physical fact of the sport (the rider's lean and the blur of bikes passing).**

oEmbed: "AH_MotoGP_03_v2", author "Pentagram", 133 s, uploaded 2024-11-15; it is the long motion piece embedded on Pentagram's MotoGP case study. The same page embeds 13 short motion clips (3–19 s, e.g. vimeo 1029682703 at 19 s). MotoGP's own "Coming soon: the new MotoGP" teaser (oEmbed author "MotoGP", 0:25) is an alt. A separate launch brand film was made by Nomad (*source*: Pentagram), so it is not this piece.

## The idea
The logo takes its rounded form "from the lean of the riders as they go around corners": the lean is in the M, the O's suggest wheels, the t is the rider between them, GP evokes the track (*source*: Pentagram). "The bikes often blur into a mix of colour and sound as they speed past", and that blur, plus the riders' "seemingly impossible angles", is "mirrored in the animated brand elements" (*source*). Strapline: "Faster. Forward. Fearless." (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
A system reel: logo builds, type sequences, colour/pattern moments and applications (*est.*). The 3–19 s clip lengths suggest short beats; median shot *est.* 1–2 s.

## Typography
A custom **variable** MotoGP display typeface plus a customised text face, both made with **F37**. The display face has alternate characters "that inject energy and movement" and works "particularly well in motion" (*source*: Pentagram).

## Colour and light
The masterbrand is **neutral** (black/white), outside the "rainbow" of team, sponsor and track colours. Instead of fixed values, sections of the colour wheel are assigned to business areas and sub-brands (*source*). Expect flat neutrals with bursts of saturated streak colour (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
Speed blur rendered as graphic streaks of colour; letters leaning and stretching as if cornering; variable-weight/width sweeps (*est.* from the documented intent). Each element is "designed with motion and dynamism in mind" rather than as fixed assets (*source*).

## Transitions
Streak wipes and leans that carry the eye across the frame into the next word or lockup (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Not documented in sources read.

## What makes it world-class
1. The logo geometry is a story of the sport (lean, wheels, rider), so the motion has a reason.
2. Motion vocabulary taken from real perception (bikes blurring into colour), not generic swooshes.
3. A neutral masterbrand that sits cleanly beside any team colour.
4. A variable typeface used as a moving brand element, not just for setting text.

## What Anikit would need
- `shape` paths for the logo, `text` with `split`, `mask` streak wipes, `motionBlur`, `mograph` for repeated streaks, hard or `wipe` `transitions`.
- GAP: animating variable-font axes (weight/width/slant); GAP: directional smear of type into colour streaks.

## Judge's checklist
- Does every motion behaviour trace back to one physical idea from the subject?
- Do letters lean and stretch in one consistent direction (no random wobble)?
- Is the base palette neutral, with colour used as an accent that means something?
- Does speed read through blur/streak at 100% crop, without banding or aliasing?
- Could the logo animation run alone as a 3–5 s ident?
