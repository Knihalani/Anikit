# DixonBaxi | CW — DixonBaxi (2024)

**Why it's in the set: a flat, limited-palette brand system whose whole motion language comes from one container ("the Stage") expanding and contracting; the reference for a deadpan, type-led network ident with no 3D crutch.**

Primary: 48 s brand film (oEmbed "DixonBaxi | CW", DixonBaxi, 48 s). Alt: the 5 s "CW Brand Open" ident itself (oEmbed, 5 s).

## The idea
The CW dropped "The" and moved from green to a reddish-orange called **Hot Sauce**, with secondary colours **Pepper** and **Salt** (*source*: It's Nice That). The logo extends into a flexible container, **the Stage**, which "can blow out to encompass whole frames or just hold characters or typography"; the ident is that contract/expand motion (*source*: It's Nice That). Tone: "voice first", "premium and deadpan" (*source*: Fonts In Use, quoting the case study).

## Structure and pacing
Brand open: one move, logo-shape opens to frame and closes back (*est.* 5 s total, oEmbed). Brand film: a montage of Stage framings over talent/show imagery and type lines (*est.* median shot 1–2 s from the 48 s length and system nature).

## Typography
**F37 Bolton** (bold sans) for display (*source*: Fonts In Use; It's Nice That). Deadpan, short lines of copy; heavy weight set large inside the Stage.

## Colour and light
Hot Sauce hero (#FF4500 per It's Nice That's image caption, *source*), plus Pepper (dark) and Salt (white), with a "pure white backdrop" (*source*: Fonts In Use). Flat colour, no gradients or lighting effects (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
A rounded-rectangle/logo-derived container acting as a mask: it scales up to full bleed to reveal show footage, shrinks to hug a word, snaps between sizes (*est.* from the Stage description). Expect crisp ease-in-out with fast settle, no bounce (*est.*, consistent with "deadpan").

## Transitions
The Stage itself is the transition: expanding to fill the frame becomes the cut to the next scene (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Sonic identity by **We Are Audio**, meant to feel "bright" like the colourways (*source*: It's Nice That).

## What makes it world-class
1. A single geometric container does every job: logo, frame, transition, type holder.
2. Strictly limited palette (three named colours) used flat and full bleed.
3. Copy and type carry personality (deadpan voice) instead of effects.
4. Motion is snappy and exact: shapes land on clean edges with no wobble.

## What Anikit would need
- `shape` (rect with radius) as `mask` over `image`/`video`, animated scale; `text` with `split` for type; `transitions` (`wipe`/custom); `audio` sting.
- Mostly existing features; a reusable "stage" `component` or `defs` would help.

## Judge's checklist
- Is there one container shape that drives every transition and framing?
- Is the palette limited to ~3 colours used flat, with no gradient backdrop?
- Does type sit large and confident inside the shape, with lines that have a voice?
- Do moves settle crisply (no overshoot wobble, no soft fades as filler)?
- Could a 5 s cut-down of the render work as a network ident on its own?
