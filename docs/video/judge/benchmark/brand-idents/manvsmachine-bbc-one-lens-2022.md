# BBC ONE ~ Idents ("Lens") — ManvsMachine (2022)

**Why it's in the set: one simple device (a rotating circular lens that opens onto the same place at another time) carries an entire channel identity across ~24 live-action idents.**

The Vimeo upload is ManvsMachine's 340 s compilation (oEmbed duration, uploaded 2022-05-13). On air, each ident runs as a short single scene (*est.* 10–20 s).

## The idea
"British life through multiple lenses" (*source*: Wikipedia, quoting ManvsMachine). Each ident shows a large shared community space (warehouse, hall, farmland, market, bench, lido, café) slowly rotating, while a circular lens reveals the same space at another time or in another configuration. Each location has three idents and the lens shows the other two (*source*: Wikipedia). The circle echoes BBC One's historic rotating globe (*source*: Wikipedia). It's Nice That frames it as "places we share", "a literal cross-section of culture", with 28 moods for seasons and times of day (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
Per ident: one continuous rotating shot; the lens grows/drifts across frame and reveals the alternate scene; the "O"/BBC logo resolves at the end (*est.* from the documented device). No cuts within an ident (*est.*). The compilation strings idents back to back.

## Typography
Minimal: the BBC block logo (2021 BBC identity) and "ONE" sign-off. In-scene type is diegetic only (e.g. a red "Emma's Cafe" sign, *source*: It's Nice That captions).

## Colour and light
Naturalistic, slightly graded "cinematic filters" (*source*: It's Nice That): warm interiors, red chairs, yellow hi-vis, a dark blue curved shape (*source*: captions). Day/evening/night variants of the same location (Sky set: Daytime, Evening, Night, *source*: Wikipedia).

## Motion techniques
Shot live action on a "gigantic" motion-control rig, with less than 24 hours per location, in mid-March (*source*: It's Nice That). Motion control lets three passes of the same move (three activities/times) be registered exactly, so the lens can composite one pass inside another without drift. The rotation is slow and constant (*est.*); the lens edge is a crisp circle, in one shot described as a "circular, distorted lens effect" (*source*: caption).

## Transitions
Transitions happen inside the shot: the lens is a moving circular matte between two registered plates (*est.* from device). No hard cuts within an ident.

## Sound design and music
Score and sound bed by **Resonate**, designed to fit each mood and sit under the continuity announcer's voice (*source*: It's Nice That). Diegetic ambience of each location (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. One device, infinitely repeatable: same rig move, three registered realities, so the system scales to dozens of idents.
2. The matte is perfectly registered (motion control), so the reveal reads as a window, not a composite.
3. Real, specific British places and people (lido, greasy spoon, skate ramp) rather than generic stock life.
4. Restraint: the logo is small, the scene is the brand.

## What Anikit would need
- Two `video` layers of the same move, an animated circular `mask` (iris), slow `camera` rotation applied to both, `post` grade.
- GAP: Anikit cannot shoot or match motion-control plates; it would need registered source footage or `scene3d` environments rendered from the same camera path twice.

## Judge's checklist
- Is there one clear, ownable device that could generate many variants?
- Do the two worlds inside and outside the lens stay perfectly registered while the camera moves?
- Does the scene stay a single continuous move (no cut to hide the trick)?
- Is the logo small and late, with the scene doing the branding?
- Are people and places specific rather than generic?
