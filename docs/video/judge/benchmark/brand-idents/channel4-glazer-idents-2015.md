# CHANNEL 4 REBRAND — 4creative / Jonathan Glazer / DBLG (2015)

**Why it's in the set: an ident that refuses to show the logo assembled, and instead builds a whole uncanny world around its nine component blocks; the benchmark for "idea first, logo last".**

URL note: the primary URL is DBLG's own 61 s rebrand film (oEmbed: title "CHANNEL 4 REBRAND", author "DBLG_LDN"); length from the YouTube results page. Channel 4's own upload of the idents is the first alt URL (oEmbed: "New Channel 4 Brand Identity | Idents", author "Channel 4 Entertainment", 3:07 on the results page).

## The idea
4creative (Chris Bovill and John Allison) broke the Lambie-Nairn "4" into its nine constituent blocks (*source*: It's Nice That). Glazer's films present those blocks "as kryptonite-like" (*source*: It's Nice That; Wikipedia): crystalline geodes that are sought after, guarded, mined or studied under a microscope. The numeral is never formed in the idents themselves; the brand is the blocks plus the strangeness around them.

## Structure and pacing
Four films for the commercial breaks (*source*: Wikipedia, LBB). Documented scenes include: men in white suits with head torches working in a quarry; a red amethyst waterfall with rocks plunging into a pool; a shimmying blue-ponytailed creature; blocks on a factory line (*source*: The Conversation). Each ident is a slow, mostly locked-off or gently drifting observation; shot lengths are long (*est.* 3–8 s, from the calm, Under-the-Skin-like staging described and the stills). The DBLG film intercuts idents with the type and graphics system, so its cutting is quicker (*est.* median 1.5–3 s).

## Typography
Neville Brody drew two bespoke faces: **Horseferry** (display; spiky, angular, cut from the block geometry) and **Chadwick** (information text), used on stings, menus, programme titles and print (*source*: It's Nice That). Type does not appear inside the Glazer idents themselves; it lives in the surrounding presentation system.

## Colour and light
Naturalistic, desaturated live-action light (quarry greys, overcast skies) against the blocks' saturated mineral colour (amethyst red/purple, crystalline sheens) (*source* for the red amethyst waterfall: The Conversation; rest *est.* from stills). The presentation system uses flat full-bleed brand colours (red, black, yellow, blue, bright pink) behind Horseferry (*source*: It's Nice That image captions).

## Motion techniques
Live action shot by DOP Alex Barber with production design by Chris Oddy; post and VFX by One of Us (*source*: Academy Films credits). The blocks are CG composited into practical locations with matched light, so they read as physical, heavy objects. Camera moves are slow and observational; nothing is "animated" in a motion-graphics sense.

## Transitions
Within idents: hard cuts or none (single-take tableaux) (*est.*). Between idents and continuity: hard cuts to graphic stings in Horseferry (*est.* from DBLG film).

## Sound design and music
Newly commissioned music by **Mica Levi** (score of Glazer's *Under the Skin*); sound by **Johnnie Burn @ Wave** (*source*: Academy Films; It's Nice That). Expect unsettling string/drone textures rather than a jingle; the sound carries the eeriness.

## What makes it world-class
1. The logo is withheld: nine blocks are enough to identify the brand, so the frame can be about a world, not a mark.
2. CG objects are lit and grounded so convincingly in real plates that they feel found, not added.
3. A single tonal register (deadpan, eerie, patient) held across four films, scored by one composer.
4. The type system (Horseferry) is derived from the same geometry as the blocks, so the identity is consistent from ident to menu.

## What Anikit would need
- `video` layers for live plates, `camera` slow drift, `post` (`cinematic`/grain), `scene3d` for the blocks.
- GAP: photoreal CG integration into filmed plates (shadow catchers, matched lighting, refraction of crystal).
- GAP: a custom display face derived from logo geometry (Anikit can load fonts, but not design them).

## Judge's checklist
- Does the render build the brand from a fragment of the mark rather than flying in the whole logo?
- Are 3D objects lit and shadowed as if they belong to the scene (contact shadows, matching light direction)?
- Is the tone consistent and committed (no switch to cheerful motion-graphics clichés)?
- Does any shot hold for 3 s or more with nothing gratuitous moving, and still feel tense?
- Is the type, when it appears, a distinctive face rather than a default sans?
