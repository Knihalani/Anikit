# Hublot MP-10 Launch Film — Tendril (2024)

**Top-tier luxury mechanical product CG: macro, hard-light deconstruction of a dial-less watch, with "cinematic grit". This is the bar for hero product renders, the kind of shot Anikit's ray-traced `scene3d` targets most directly.**

## The idea
Director Joey Recoskie and the Tendril team "deconstruct the many striking innovations of the new MP-10, a timepiece with no dial, hands, or oscillating weight". From Tendril: "we crafted a film and campaign that merges raw energy and mystery with cinematic grit". Features shown: "the center in-line chassis, 35° tourbillon, and linear weight energy system", represented "through a unique graphic approach", and "a multi-axis compound curved glass lens" in Hublot sapphire: "No part of the glass is flat" (*source*: Stash).

## Structure and pacing
- Length 68 s (*source*: oEmbed).
- Mystery opening (fragments in darkness) → feature-by-feature deconstruction with graphic callouts → full hero reveal → logo (*est.*). Shots: about 30–40, median about 1.5 s (*est.*; to be *measured*).

## Typography
"A unique graphic approach" highlights each innovation (*source*). Expect thin technical callouts and feature names (e.g. "35°") set as engineering annotations in a geometric sans, tracked wide (*est.*).

## Colour and light
Low-key black sets with hard, narrow light strips raking across sapphire, titanium and gold surfaces. Highlights travel across bevels as the camera moves; there is little or no fill (*est.*, from Tendril's Hublot work and the "cinematic grit" brief).

## Motion techniques
- Houdini simulation by Arnis Vitols, Sylvain Gaussens and Yeseong Kim; lighting and rendering by Brad Husband, Yeseong Kim and Joey Recoskie; art direction by Daniel Lepik (*source*: Stash credits). Sims likely drive energy, particulate or material-transformation moments (*est.*).
- Macro camera moves with a very shallow focal plane, slow-to-fast speed changes on reveals, mechanical parts that assemble and explode along their axes (*est.*).

## Transitions
Light-wipe and match-on-shape transitions between components; cuts on mechanical hits (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Sound design by Cypher Audio (*source*: Stash credits); a dedicated "Sound Design Edit" exists on Behance (*source*: search result). Mechanical ticks, servo whirs and sub-bass impacts drive the edit (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. **Light as the subject.** Narrow strip highlights roll over bevels and sapphire curvature, revealing form one edge at a time.
2. **Engineering clarity.** Each innovation gets its own shot and graphic annotation; you learn the product.
3. **Material truth.** Sapphire shows refraction and internal reflections, and brushed versus polished metal is clearly distinct.
4. **Grit in luxury.** Sim FX and dark mood keep it from feeling like a sterile turntable.

## What Anikit would need
- `scene3d` chrome, metal and glass materials with true reflections and refraction, a dark `env` with strip area lights, `camera` macro moves with `aperture`/`focus` pulls, `motionBlur`, and `post` (bloom on specular hits, grain).
- `text` callouts with line leaders; `audio` hit sync.
- **GAP:** dispersion and caustics through curved sapphire; FX sims (particulate, energy) at Houdini fidelity.
- **GAP:** importing CAD-accurate product models (Anikit primitives are not enough for a watch movement).

## Judge's checklist
- Does each shot reveal form through a moving highlight rather than flat, even light?
- Are glass or crystal surfaces visibly refractive, with believable edges (no flat transparency)?
- Is there a clear one-feature-per-shot logic with legible technical callouts?
- Do mechanical motions have precise, weighty timing (no linear floaty translation)?
- Are blacks deep and clean at 100% crop (no noise or banding in falloffs)?
