# Plume — FutureDeluxe for Wacom (2017)

**Abstract, procedurally detailed CG at its most luxurious: a scanned dancer grown over with layer upon layer of feather structures. The benchmark for surface detail density and macro-scale richness.**

## The idea
"A surreal and experimental arts piece combining woman and bird" (*source*: Vimeo description). Wacom's only brief was "that it had to feature a feather in some way". The team "purposely approached this project with a very aesthetic direction with little narrative" (*source*: Stash quote). Movement artist Paleta performed "a series of dynamic poses which mimicked the movement of a bird – Growth, Display and Flight" (*source*: Stash).

## Structure and pacing
- Length 58 s (*source*: oEmbed).
- Three movements following the poses: Growth → Display → Flight (*source*: Stash). Shots: about 15–20, mixing macro detail and full-figure reveals, median about 3 s (*est.*; to be *measured*).

## Typography
None in the film beyond end branding (*est.*).

## Colour and light
"An imaginary world of intricate colour and detail" (*source*: Vimeo). Iridescent and saturated plumage colours on dark, low-key backdrops, rim-lit to pick out filament edges (*est.*, from the thumbnail and FutureDeluxe's style).

## Motion techniques
- The poses were 3D-scanned at FBFX for high-resolution models in multiple positions. "Layer after layer of detailed abstract feather structures" were added procedurally: "an extensive period of research and design followed to create a unique procedural based series of CG textures and abstract details" (*source*: Stash / FutureDeluxe).
- Character rigging and animation by Alex Bootz; additional CG and FX by Thom Haig and Simon Fiedler (*source*: Vimeo credits).
- Growth-style motion: structures sprout, unfurl and ripple across the body surface (*est.*).

## Transitions
Cuts between macro and wide, with match-on-form continuity (feather to feather) (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Audio by Resonate (*source*: Vimeo credits). Granular, textural sound that follows the growth and unfurling (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. **Detail density.** At macro distance every square centimetre has structure: filaments, scales, layered vanes. There are no flat areas.
2. **Procedural growth with a human anchor.** Real scanned poses give the abstraction a believable body and gesture.
3. **Rim and edge light** separate thousands of fine strands from a dark ground without noise or aliasing.
4. **Arc in an abstract piece.** Growth → Display → Flight gives a progression without narrative.

## What Anikit would need
- `scene3d` with instanced geometry (thousands of small textured instances), rim lighting, a dark `env`, and shallow `camera` aperture; `post` with bloom on iridescent highlights.
- **GAP:** strand or feather grooming that follows a deforming mesh; anisotropic or iridescent (thin-film) shading.
- **GAP:** importing scanned or animated mesh sequences.

## Judge's checklist
- At 100% crop on a macro shot, is there fine structure everywhere (no flat or smeared areas)?
- Are thin strands stable from frame to frame (no shimmer or crawling aliasing)?
- Does the piece progress (growth → display → release) rather than repeat one look?
- Are dark backgrounds clean (no banding) while highlights stay colourful?
