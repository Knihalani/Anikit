# Baidu Raven "H" TVC (Director Cut) — Builders Club (2017)

**"Domestic materiality" made magical: four hypnotic CG chapters (waves, connectivity, AI, colour) that morph everyday materials, unified by one lighting language. The benchmark for satisfying material transformation in product launches.**

## The idea
Baidu's Raven H AI speaker. The film "splits into four, emulating the speaker's qualities: connectivity, signal waves, AI and colours". "The overarching theme is 'domestic materiality'… We created [animated] materials associated with industrial products and home environments… then distorted and transformed them in a magical way" (Julien Simshauser, *source*: It's Nice That).

## Structure and pacing
- Length 71 s (*source*: oEmbed). Four chapters of about 15–18 s each plus the product resolve (*est.*).
- **Waves:** ripples and undulations through liquid and solid materials. **Connectivity:** solid objects in marble, wood and metal that "morph and mould together" before the speaker's layers assemble. **AI:** a botanical scene appearing from thin air, with tropical leaves, mushrooms and grass spreading across a floor, combined with "drone led lights". **Colours:** sheets and amorphous blobs of block colour gradually covering surfaces (*source*: INT).
- Shots: about 25–35, median about 2 s (*est.*; to be *measured*).

## Typography
None in the body; product and brand at the end (*est.*).

## Colour and light
"To connect the four disparate visuals in one hero film, Builder's Club created a consistent light quality throughout… we wanted to move away from the usual soft light used heavily in 3D motion graphics, and mainstream-friendly pastel colours. Instead we went for monochromatic materials combined with a few punchy accent colours" (*source*: INT). Imperfections ("scratches and wrinkles") were added "to make things feel more real" (*source*: INT).

## Motion techniques
- Solid-to-solid material morphs (marble, wood, metal) and ripple propagation through "solid" surfaces (*source*: INT).
- Procedural growth: vegetation spreading across a floor; slow-motion living plants inspired by Cyprien Gaillard's video art (*source*: INT).
- Colour flooding: block colour sheets and blobs creeping over surfaces (*source*: INT).
- References: Jeff Koons (Connectivity) and chemical reactions (Waves) (*source*: INT).

## Transitions
Each chapter likely resolves into the product assembling in layers; transitions are material-driven (one surface becomes the next) (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Music by Simon Pyke, "giving the whole film an otherworldly feel" (*source*: INT; Vimeo credits "Sound: Simon Pyke").

## What makes it world-class
1. **Lighting as the unifier.** Four different worlds share one lighting language: hard-ish, not the default soft 3D-mograph light.
2. **Monochrome plus accent.** Materials stay mostly monochrome, with a few punchy accents; it avoids pastel-by-default.
3. **Imperfection for realism.** Scratches and wrinkles break CG perfection.
4. **Impossible behaviour, real materials.** Marble that ripples and wood that melds are believable surfaces doing unbelievable things.

## What Anikit would need
- `scene3d` with textured materials (marble, wood, metal), roughness and scratch maps, consistent key light across scenes, `camera` slow moves, `motionBlur`, and a `post` punchy preset with restraint.
- `audio` for music sync.
- **GAP:** fluid and ripple deformations on surfaces, material-to-material morphs, procedural vegetation growth.

## Judge's checklist
- Do all chapters share one lighting language (same key direction and hardness), even though the worlds differ?
- Is the palette mostly monochrome with only a few deliberate accent colours (not pastel by default)?
- Do surfaces carry imperfections (scratches, roughness variation) at 100% crop?
- Does each transformation read as a real material doing something impossible, rather than a generic blob?
