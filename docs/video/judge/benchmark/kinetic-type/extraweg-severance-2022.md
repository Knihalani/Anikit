# Severance main titles — extraweg / Oliver Latta (2022)

**Why it's in the set: a one-person, Emmy-winning CG title where every shot is a single physical metaphor (soft bodies, ooze, duplicated selves), with type that deliberately stays out of the way.**

## The idea
Mark Scout's "innie" and "outie" go from home to work and back. On the way they melt through walls, are sucked up a syringe, squeeze through crowds of soft heads, and drag a balloon made of small bodies ("him dragging the past"). The brief was originally 15–20 s; the delays caused by Covid let it grow to a full narrative opener (*source*: Latta, Art of the Title). Latta's arc for the score: "slow in the beginning, then in the middle we have a high, and then in the end we're kind of falling down again" (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- Length 86 s (*source*: Vimeo oEmbed); the score is "a perfectly made one-and-a-half-minute intro title song" (*source*).
- Shot count about 18–25, median shot about 3–4 s (*est.*, from the scene list in the Art of the Title interview and the Vimeo thumbnails). Several shots are long, slow camera moves that end in a transformation instead of a cut.
- Three acts follow the score: a dreamlike walk to work, a middle section of bodily horror at the peak of the music, and a collapse as he returns home.

## Typography
- A custom typeface by Teddy Blanks: "left-aligned Helvetica with a thin horizontal line above it. Except it's not Helvetica", drawn after mid-century corporate identity manuals in the Vignelli mould (*source*).
- Credits are small, left-aligned and in sentence form, each with a thin overline rule. They sit in negative space, never on the action, and never animate expressively. The type is "minor" by design: it "had to pair well with – and ultimately take a backseat to" the animation (*source*).

## Colour and light
- Desaturated corporate palette: snow white, office beige and grey, pale fluorescent greens, with darker navy for the suits (*est.*, from stills). The skin is the warmest thing in the frame.
- Soft overcast and fluorescent lighting, with shallow depth of field on close-ups of faces. Rendered in Octane (*source*).

## Motion techniques
- Houdini soft-body, cloth and fluid simulation: bodies squash, inflate, stretch and pour like paste (*source*: Houdini for all simulations).
- Cinema 4D camera moves; 60% mocap from Latta's own Xsens suit and 40% preset and keyframed motion (*source*). The limping, head-down walk carries the mood.
- A 3D scan of Adam Scott (4–5 expressions) with groomed hair, for macro close-ups (*source*).
- Post Mango added subtle snow, a lens distortion for the camera's dive into the syringe, and the final grade (*source*).

## Transitions
Mostly in-camera: the camera pushes into an object (a head, a syringe, a doorway) and comes out in the next space. A walk through elevator doors swaps the suit for pyjamas (innie to outie) in one continuous move (*source*: still caption). Few hard cuts (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Theodore Shapiro's main-title theme (it won its own Emmy, per Latta): piano and strings with a pulse that builds to a peak in the middle and then decays. The animation was timed to the music once it arrived (*source*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Each shot is a single, readable visual metaphor (dragging the past as a balloon of bodies; being consumed by work as being sucked up a syringe). It is not "cool effects": you can name what every shot means.
2. The simulated materials have weight. Soft bodies jiggle, settle and collide believably; nothing floats or clips.
3. The camera never stops. Even long takes have a slow, purposeful push or drift that ends on a reveal.
4. Typographic restraint: a single small, left-aligned typeface with an overline rule, consistent position, no animation gimmicks, and high legibility.
5. The structure is a musical arc (slow, peak, decay), not a list of moments.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d` with PBR materials and depth of field, `camera` moves, `text` credits with a `shape` overline rule, `post` (a soft, desaturated grade) and `motionBlur`. GAPs: soft-body, cloth and liquid simulation driving deformable characters; scanned, rigged, mocap-driven digital humans; groomed hair.

## Judge's checklist
- Can you name the single idea of every shot in one sentence?
- Do deforming materials keep their volume and settle with weight, without popping or interpenetrating?
- Is every credit small, left-aligned, the same style and in clean negative space, never over the action?
- Does the camera move with intent in every shot (no static holds), and do some transitions happen through objects rather than by cutting?
- Does the edit follow a clear arc that matches the score (build, peak, release)?
