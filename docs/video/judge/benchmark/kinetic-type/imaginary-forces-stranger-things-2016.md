# Stranger Things main title — Imaginary Forces (2016)

**Why it's in the set: the benchmark for a purely typographic title. One typeface, one colour and black, and nothing else: all the mood comes from type, light and timing.**

## The idea
Pieces of the logo drift through a void and slowly assemble, in the manner of early-80s optical titles (R/Greenberg's *Altered States* and *The Dead Zone*) and Stephen King paperback covers (*source*: Art of the Title). The type is the only actor: "A typeface is like an actor" (Dougherty, *source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 53 s (*source*: Vimeo oEmbed). Art of the Title gives the aspect ratio as 2.00:1 (*source*).
- About 12–16 shots: extreme close-ups of single letter parts (a serif, a bar of the T, the curve of an S) sliding slowly past the camera, then wider shots as the pieces converge, ending on the full locked-up logotype (*est.*, from the AotT description and the studio stills).
- Median shot about 3 s (*est.*). The pace is slow and deliberate; tension comes from the music, not the cutting.
- An early "locking in" version, with snappy letters snapping into place, was rejected because snapping motion "wasn't a really familiar motion in the 1980s"; the final uses smooth, slow drifts (*source*).

## Typography
- ITC Benguiat (Ed Benguiat), chosen for its associations with Stephen King paperbacks and Choose Your Own Adventure books (*source*). The typeface was swapped late from another face, which forced the glows to be rebuilt because the serifs caught light differently (*source*: Maxon case study).
- The logotype is set in two stacked lines (STRANGER over THINGS), with enlarged outer letters and a rule (*est.*, from the studio still; check against the frames).
- The letters are hollow outlines with glowing red edges, "large, hollow type drifts through a void" (*source*). Smaller supporting credits fade in and out in the same red.

## Colour and light
- Pure black ground and saturated red emissive outlines, from bright scarlet to deep crimson (*est.*, from stills; no hex value is published), with hot near-white cores where the light is brightest.
- The glow is Red Giant Shine "doubled and sometimes tripled" for organic blooms; light rays are inspired by the practical titles of *The Thing* (*source*: Maxon).
- In the fades the type shifts colour toward red, mimicking optical printing (*source*: Dougherty).

## Motion techniques
- Mostly After Effects with a little Cinema 4D; built by "layering, masking, and stacking multiplying effects" (*source*).
- Slow linear or gently eased drifts and scale changes of glyph fragments; a slow camera dolly over the letters.
- Deliberate imperfection: light jitter (flicker), scanned 35 mm grain (Gorilla Grain), a few real optical flares (Lens Distortions), and imperfect edges studied from Kodalith test shoots (*source*).

## Transitions
Fades through black and slow dissolves between close-ups; no hard snaps, wipes or whips (*source*: the description of fade-downs; *est.* for the absence of other transitions).

## Sound design and music
Kyle Dixon and Michael Stein (S U R V I V E) synth theme; the theme won the Emmy for Outstanding Original Main Title Theme Music (*source*: Art of the Title 2017 Emmy news). An arpeggiated synth pulse with swelling pads; type movements are timed to the swells.

## What makes it world-class
1. Restraint: a single typeface, a single hue on black, no ornaments. Every frame is still a designed composition (cropped glyph details read as abstract graphic shapes).
2. The light behaves like light through film: the glow has a hot core, a soft halo and slight flicker, and the type changes colour as it fades. It is not a flat outer glow.
3. Motion is slow and continuous, without snapping or overshoot, which feels period-correct and ominous.
4. Scale contrast: extreme macro crops of serifs, then the whole word.
5. Analogue texture (grain, flicker, slight misregistration) gives the digital pieces a physical origin.

## What Anikit would need
`text` with `stroke` and `"color": "none"` for hollow outlines, `glow`, `camera` zoom and pan for the macro drifts, `post` with `bloom`, `halation` and `grain`, and fades via `crossfade`/`fadeBlack`. GAPs: splitting a glyph into its constituent strokes so that fragments move independently; optical-print artefacts (gate weave, flicker, colour shift in fades) as a post option.

## Judge's checklist
- Is the piece carried by type alone, with a single deliberate typeface and no filler graphics?
- Does the glow have a hot core and a soft, multi-radius halo (not a single uniform outer glow), and does it hold up at 100% crop without banding on black?
- Are the motions slow, smooth and free of snapping or overshoot, with each move timed to the music?
- Are there macro crops where a single glyph detail fills the frame?
- Is there visible analogue texture (grain, flicker) that is subtle and not a filter over everything?
