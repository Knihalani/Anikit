# True Detective main titles — Patrick Clair / Antibody for Elastic (2014)

**Why it's in the set: the reference for double-exposure compositing. Every frame is a portrait used as a window onto a landscape, which shows character through place.**

## The idea
"Fragmented portraits, created by using human figures as windows into partial landscapes" show "characters that are marginalised or internally divided"; the titles "reveal character through location" (*source*: Clair, Art of the Title). The sequence builds "from lightness into darkness, and then burn[s] the whole thing down" (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 94 s (*source*: Vimeo oEmbed).
- About 20–25 shots, median about 3.5–4 s (*est.*, from the music's verse structure and the storyboard of 55 images on AotT). The rhythm is slow and floaty, with a few quick optical glitches near cuts.
- Arc: pale daylight Gulf Coast landscapes inside silhouettes, then night, refineries and motels, then animated flames that consume the images at the climax (*source*).

## Typography
Credits in a light sans-serif set small and spaced, placed beside the portraits (*est.*, from stills; the typeface is not documented in the sources read). Type is supporting and static; the images carry the motion.

## Colour and light
"A really gentle undulation between pale yellows, greens, dark blues, and light flares", with light and dark dirt and marks running through the pictures (*source*). Based on Richard Misrach's Gulf Coast photographs (*source*). Late in the piece orange fire takes over the blue-black.

## Motion techniques
- Live-action footage of the cast slowed to 10–20% of its original speed with digital interpolation, so the portraits are "living photographs", neither video nor stills (*source*).
- Landscape stills projected onto low-poly 3D geometry (truck stops, refineries), with very slow virtual camera moves through them (*source*).
- Digital doubles for single-still subjects (the dancer's heels and skin built in 3D so the camera can pan) (*source*).
- Combined in one comp with "spatial animation, focus effects, and lots of texture" (*source*).

## Transitions
Optical glitches and photographic distortion stitch the cuts together "to emphasize the double exposure technique and meld the animation with the music" (*source*). Light flares and dissolves between exposures (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
"Far From Any Road" by The Handsome Family (alt-country, baritone voice, reverb guitar), chosen by the production beforehand; the structure of the piece follows the song (*source*).

## What makes it world-class
1. A single, consistent visual device (silhouette as window) is used in every shot, and its meaning (place lives inside the people) is legible.
2. The masks are crafted: figure edges are soft and organic, with texture bleeding across them, not hard cutouts.
3. Motion is almost imperceptibly slow, so the 2.5D projections feel like a living photograph, and the parallax is believable.
4. The palette moves over time, from pale yellow-green to blue-black to fire, which gives a visible arc.
5. Texture (dirt, scratches, flares) is layered with restraint and stays photographic rather than "grunge filter".

## What Anikit would need
`image`/`video` layers with a luma or alpha `matte` (portrait as window), `blend` modes (`screen`, `multiply`), `camera` with layer `depth` for parallax and `aperture` for focus, `post` (grain, halation, vignette) and `scene3d` for simple geometry. GAPs: camera projection of photographs onto geometry; optical-flow frame interpolation for extreme slow motion; procedural fire elements.

## Judge's checklist
- Is a single compositing idea used consistently, and does it mean something about the subject?
- Do matte edges look photographic (soft, textured, with plausible light wrap) rather than cut out?
- Is motion slow and continuous, with real parallax between at least two depth planes?
- Does the colour change across the piece in an arc, not stay one grade throughout?
- Is texture subtle enough that faces and credits stay clean and readable?
