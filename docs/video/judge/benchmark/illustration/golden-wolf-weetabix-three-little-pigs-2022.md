# Weetabix "Three Little Pigs" — Golden Wolf (2022)

**Why it's in the set: a 30-second spot that recreates the visual language of 1930s Disney and Fleischer cartoons so faithfully that the twist (the wolf wins) lands as a period-perfect gag.**

## The idea
The big bad wolf has had his Weetabix and finally conquers the brick house (*source*: Golden Wolf). Part of BBH's "Have you had your Weetabix?" campaign (*source*: search summary).

## Structure and pacing
- 30 s (*source*: Vimeo oEmbed).
- Classic three-act fairy tale compressed: straw and stick houses (implied or quick), the brick house, the wolf eats Weetabix, huffs, the brick house falls, end card (*est.*, from the fairy-tale structure and the description).
- About 10–14 shots, median about 2 s (*est.*).

## Typography
End card with the Weetabix logo and line (*est.*). Period-style title lettering possible; not documented.

## Colour and light
1930s cartoon palette: muted, warm, slightly desaturated colours with soft painted backgrounds, as in early Technicolor shorts (*est.*, from "visual language of the 1930s"). Film grain and softness (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
"A combination of modern techniques and traditional frame-by-frame animation" channelling early Disney and Fleischer (*source*). Expect rubber-hose limbs, squash and stretch, bouncy idle cycles, big anticipation before the huff, and smear frames on fast actions (*est.*, the period's conventions).

## Transitions
Hard cuts and iris-style period transitions (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Period-style orchestral cartoon score with mickey-mousing (music hits matching actions) (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Period accuracy: drawing style, timing, palette and texture all belong to the 1930s, so nothing breaks the illusion.
2. Classical animation principles in full: anticipation, squash and stretch, overlapping action, smears.
3. A whole story in 30 s with a twist the audience expects to go the other way.
4. Sound and action locked together (mickey-mousing) in period style.

## What Anikit would need
`image` sequences for drawn frames, painted background layers with `camera` pans, `post` grain and vignette, an end card with `text`. GAPs: frame-by-frame character animation; period film artefacts (gate weave, flicker, soft focus) as a post option.

## Judge's checklist
- Does every element (palette, line, timing, texture) belong to the same referenced era?
- Are animation principles visible (anticipation before big actions, squash and stretch, follow-through)?
- Is the story complete and readable within the length, with a clear twist or payoff?
- Do music hits land on the actions?
