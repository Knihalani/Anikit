# Enemy (Arcane) — Fortiche / Riot Games (2021)

**Why it's in the set: the reference for the painterly 2D/3D hybrid (Arcane). 3D characters and sets carry hand-painted textures, 2D effects and illustrative lighting, so each frame reads as a painting in motion.**

## The idea
Animated versions of Imagine Dragons and JID move through the world of Arcane, intercut with scenes from the younger days of Powder (Jinx) and Vi (*source*: Wikipedia via search). Released on 28 October 2021, ahead of the Netflix series (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 214 s (*source*: YouTube listing, "3 minutes, 34 seconds").
- Performance by the band and JID in Arcane's world, intercut with a story montage of the sisters (*source*). Cuts on the beat and on vocal accents, about 1–2 s per shot (*est.*, from the track's tempo and the genre).

## Typography
None in the body (*est.*); a Fortiche credit appears at the start (*source*: esports.gg via search).

## Colour and light
Arcane's palette: warm, golden Piltover against the toxic greens, magentas and deep shadows of Zaun (*est.*, from the series). Strong painted rim light and coloured fog (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
- 2D/3D animation by Fortiche (*source*: Wikipedia); Riot describes the Arcane look as a "hand-painted art style and nuanced character animation" made with Fortiche (*source*: ComicBook.com quoting Riot; this refers to the series in general).
- Painted textures on 3D models; 2D hand-drawn effects (smoke, sparks, graffiti) composited over 3D (*est.*, from the series' well-documented look).
- Character animation often on twos (stepped) inside smooth camera moves (*est.*).

## Transitions
Hard cuts and match cuts between the band's performance and the story (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
"Enemy" by Imagine Dragons with JID: a stomping, anthemic hook with a fast rap verse. The edit follows the drums (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Every surface looks painted (brush texture in skin, clothes and backgrounds) while perspective and camera are fully 3D.
2. 2D effects (smoke, sparks, graffiti, smears) are drawn in the same hand as the textures, so there's no "CG FX" look.
3. Lighting is designed like illustration: strong rim and colour separation, warm against toxic cool.
4. Character performance is specific and expressive, with stepped timing that reads as drawn.
5. The edit is locked to the beat while still telling a story.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d` with custom `shader` for painterly or toon shading, `camera` and `depth`, `post` grade and bloom, `motionBlur` off for stepped timing. GAPs: hand-painted texturing of 3D assets; drawn 2D FX layers over 3D; stepped character animation inside a smooth camera.

## Judge's checklist
- Does every surface (characters, sets) carry visible painted texture, not clean CG shading?
- Are FX drawn in the same style as the rest, rather than particle-system defaults?
- Is the lighting designed (rim light, colour separation) and readable at a glance?
- Is character timing deliberately stylised (stepped poses) while the camera stays smooth?
- Do cuts lock to the beat while keeping the story clear?
