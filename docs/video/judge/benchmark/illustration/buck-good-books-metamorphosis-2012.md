# Metamorphosis — BUCK for Good Books (2012)

**Why it's in the set (classic): the reference for VO-driven illustrated animation in which every line of a rant becomes a visual transformation, without a single static shot.**

## The idea
A Hunter S. Thompson-style rant: Dr. Gonzo struggles to buy Kafka's *Metamorphosis* "without paying tribute to our gluttonous, corporate overlords" and finds Good Books, an online bookseller that gives all profits to Oxfam (*source*: BUCK). "A graphic equivalent of a drug fueled rant" (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 162 s (*source*: Vimeo oEmbed).
- Follows the VO rant line by line; ends with the book steadying a wobbly desk leg, Enter pressed, tea spilled, and a beetle that sipped the tea convulsing and dying (*source*: Motionographer).
- Each VO phrase gets its own visual, typically 1–3 s, chained by transformations (*est.*, from "pioneering piece of transitional animation" and the VO pace).

## Typography
None in the body (*est.*); the end card names Good Books.

## Colour and light
"Somewhat dark but arty" with paint texture (*source*); saturated, psychedelic accents (*est.*, from the thumbnails).

## Motion techniques
- Mostly pencil-tested and finished as cel animation in Flash, Photoshop and After Effects (*source*); "we did a shit ton of cel animation on this one" (*source*: BUCK).
- 3D used as reference for cel (sunglasses, hat); hair dynamics as the base for loose squiggly and dotted lines; one 3D morph (book to flowers); CG hero beetles and goat with illustrative textures, drawn over (*source*).
- Lens-distorted human bodies, re-animated many times (*source*).
- About four months of production, about 40 credited artists (*source*).

## Transitions
"Transitional animation": one image turns into the next (book into flowers, and others), so the film is close to one continuous metamorphosis (*source* for the concept and the book-to-flowers morph; *est.* for frequency).

## Sound design and music
VO by Thor Erickson (*source*), music and sound design by Antfood, whose score won Best of Show at the AMP Awards (*source*). The VO drives the pace.

## What makes it world-class
1. The image changes on every phrase of the VO, and changes by transformation, not by cut: motion is the edit.
2. Hand-drawn cel animation with real drawing skill (squash, lens distortion, line boil) is used where a cheaper technique would be easier.
3. 3D is used invisibly as reference and drawn over, so the whole film keeps one hand-made line.
4. The idea (a book called *Metamorphosis*, a beetle dying at the end) is carried by the technique (constant transformation).
5. Sound and VO timing are as crafted as the picture.

## What Anikit would need
`image` sequences for drawn frames, `shape` morphs, `camera`, `audio` VO with `captions` timing for sync. GAPs: frame-by-frame cel animation with painted textures; drawn character performance with lens distortion. Anikit's role here is the transition timing to VO, not the drawing.

## Judge's checklist
- Does the picture change meaningfully on every VO phrase?
- Are transitions transformations of one image into the next rather than cuts or dissolves?
- Does the line quality stay consistent and hand-made, even where 3D is involved?
- Is there a payoff at the end that ties back to the idea?
- Is the VO-to-picture sync tight (actions land on stressed words)?
