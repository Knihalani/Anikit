# The Bear & The Hare — Elliot Dear and Yves Geleyn / Blinkink (2013)

**Why it's in the set (classic): hand-drawn 2D characters printed and animated in camera on built miniature sets. Illustration gets real light, real lenses and real depth, which no flat 2D pipeline matches.**

## The idea
The hare wants his friend the bear, who always hibernates, to experience Christmas for the first time (*source*: bestmediainfo). The payoff is the bear waking to a Christmas tree in the forest, set to a single tender song (*est.*, from the story).

## Structure and pacing
- Full advert about 2 minutes (*source*: Wikipedia-style listing via search; length_s left null because no official page states it).
- A slow seasonal arc: autumn, the bear going to sleep, snow and the other animals' Christmas, the hare's gift, the bear waking to the tree (*est.*, from the story).
- Long, gentle shots of 3–6 s (*est.*).

## Typography
Only the end card (John Lewis logo and line) (*est.*).

## Colour and light
Cool winter blues and whites in the forest, with warm practical-light glows (fairy lights, the tree) at the climax (*est.*, from stills). Lit like live action: the sets were physical, so the light, shadow and lens depth of field are real (*source*: "almost everything in camera").

## Motion techniques
- Nearly 4,000 hand-drawn 2D frames laser-cut, printed and then animated stop-motion on the set (*source*: No Film School).
- A physically built, scaled-down winter world; a 3D previs animatic at Blinkink planned sets and characters to scale before building (*source*).
- About 6 weeks of animation; about £1m for the animation (*source*).

## Transitions
Gentle cuts and camera moves through the forest; seasonal change in place (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Lily Allen's cover of Keane's "Somewhere Only We Know" (*source*), which reached UK number one (*source*). Piano and voice; the edit breathes with the song (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Drawn characters exist in real space: real light falloff, real shadows and real lens depth of field on illustration.
2. The illustration line keeps its hand-made boil while the world around it is solid and photographed.
3. A simple, emotional story told without dialogue, readable by children.
4. Warm practical light is saved for the emotional climax; the palette has a clear arc from cool to warm.
5. Music and picture pace are matched: slow, tender and patient.

## What Anikit would need
`image` sequences for drawn characters, `camera` with layer `depth` and `aperture` for real-looking DOF, `post` (bloom on fairy lights, grain), `audio`. GAPs: placing drawn cards in a lit 3D miniature world with real shadows and lens behaviour (could be approached with `scene3d` and textured cards); stop-motion stepped timing.

## Judge's checklist
- Do illustrated elements receive light, shadow and depth of field consistent with their environment?
- Does the drawn line keep a hand-made quality while the space is solid?
- Is the story readable without words?
- Does the palette move from cool to warm (or another deliberate arc) toward the emotional peak?
- Is the pace patient and matched to the music, without dead holds?
