# Honda "Grrr" — Smith & Foulkes / Nexus (2004)

**Why it's in the set (classic): the most-awarded illustrated ad of its decade. A whistled song and a candy-coloured storybook world deliver an engineering message (a quieter diesel engine) with no product shot until the end.**

## The idea
"Hate something, change something": the idea came from Honda engineer Kenichi Nagahiro's dislike of noisy diesel engines (*source*: Nexus via search). Nexus were asked "to imagine and create a landscape of positive hate" (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 90 s (*source*: Wikipedia).
- Scene order (*source*: Wikipedia): an idyllic landscape of grassy hills and blue lakes with topiary spelling "HATE"; Keillor's intro; a noisy, primitive diesel engine flies in (flowers cough and close, a frog leaves its lily pad, chickens squawk); a flock of diesel engines is picked off by cartoon animals and scenery; a short psychedelic pattern section ("Hate something, change something"); the last noisy engine destroyed; a double line of flamingos introduces the new quiet engine; songbirds escort the shining engine; it flies over a flower bed spelling "Diesel i-CTDi"; Honda logo and "The Power of Dreams" on white.
- About 20–25 shots, median about 3–4 s (*est.*, from the scene list and the length).

## Typography
Type is grown in the world: topiary "HATE" and a flower-bed "Diesel i-CTDi" (*source*). The end card is a clean Honda logo and tagline on white (*source*).

## Colour and light
Saturated, sunny storybook palette: bright greens, sky blues, flamingo pinks, flower reds and yellows, soft and even daylight (*est.*, from stills). The noisy engines are dark, grey and smoky against it; the new engine is shiny chrome (*source*: "shining engine").

## Motion techniques
Illustrative CG and 2D: rounded, simplified creatures and landscape with soft shading; the engines are mechanical CG; a 6-month production with a £600,000 budget for the TV spot (*source*: Wikipedia). Cartoon physics for the attacks on the engines (bounces, pops) (*est.*).

## Transitions
Continuous camera moves through the landscape; a hard switch into the psychedelic pattern section and back (*source* for the pattern section; *est.* for the method).

## Sound design and music
"Can Hate Be Good?", sung by Garrison Keillor with guitar and a group whistling the tune; written by W+K's Michael Russoff, Sean Thompson and Richard Russell (*source*). Sound effects of coughing flowers and squawking chickens play against the gentle song (*source*).

## What makes it world-class
1. The tone is the strategy: a gentle, whistled, sing-along song about "hate" makes a technical message charming.
2. The world is coherent: every creature, plant and prop shares one rounded, toy-like design language.
3. Character animation is full of specific, funny reactions (flowers coughing, a frog leaving its pad), each readable in a second.
4. The type is integrated into the landscape (topiary, flower beds), never laid over it.
5. Clear story structure: problem, fight, transformation, resolution, logo.

## What Anikit would need
`shape` and `image` for illustrated assets, `camera` with layer `depth` for parallax across the landscape, `text` (or shapes) arranged as topiary or flowers, `repeat` for flocks, `audio` for the song. GAPs: toon-shaded illustrative 3D with paper or painted textures; character animation for many small creatures.

## Judge's checklist
- Does every element share one design language (shapes, palette, line quality)?
- Is there a specific, readable character reaction in most shots, not just things moving?
- Is any text integrated into the world rather than overlaid?
- Does the story have a clear problem, change and resolution within the length?
- Does the tone of the music and the visuals work together toward a single idea?
