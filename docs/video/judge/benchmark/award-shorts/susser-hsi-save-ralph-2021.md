# Save Ralph — Spencer Susser / Arch Model Studio (2021)

**A campaign film that works as cinema: a mock-documentary interview with a lab rabbit, made by the puppet team behind Wes Anderson's stop-motion films.**

## The idea
Ralph, a rabbit "tester", is interviewed for a documentary as he goes through his daily routine in a cosmetics lab; the deadpan format makes the horror land (*source*: Partizan). Part of HSI's campaign to end cosmetic animal testing. Voiced by Taika Waititi (*source*: oEmbed title; search summaries). Puppets and sets by Andy Gent's Arch Model Studio, shot in East London during lockdown (*source*: search summaries of AWN / Cartoon Brew).

## Structure and pacing
- 4:22 (*source*: Partizan). Steady documentary rhythm: interview framing, cutaways to routine, *est.* 40–60 shots, median *est.* 4–6 s.
- Longer than 90 s: analyse the 60 s around the lab interview (hq2 still) as representative.

## Typography
Minimal: documentary-style captions/end card only (*est.*).

## Colour and light
*observed* hq2: clinical cool lab light (cyan-white fluorescents, ~#BFD6E6), white cabinets with blue accents, shallow depth of field so the background shelves blur; Ralph in white felt/fur with a red, irritated eye and bandaged ear, restrained in a clear acrylic stand. Production designers used a "convincingly dated set" for his imagined 70s home, referencing Elvis's kitchen, as warm contrast to the lab (*source*: D&AD).

## Motion techniques
Stop-motion puppet animation; much of the emotion is carried by the eyes ("they can tell the story on their own", Andy Gent — *source*: D&AD). Documentary camera grammar (static interview shots, observational cutaways) on miniature sets (*est.*).

## Transitions
Hard documentary cuts (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
VO-led: Waititi's light, matter-of-fact delivery as the counterpoint to the images; room tone and lab sounds; little music (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Format as idea: a mock-doc interview gives a puppet credibility and irony.
2. Material truth: real fur, felt, glass, acrylic and practical light — at 100% crop everything has texture.
3. Lens behaviour: shallow DOF and motivated fluorescents sell miniature sets as real spaces.
4. Performance in the eyes: tiny eye changes carry the emotion.
5. Tonal contrast: a warm, dated home set against a cold lab.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d` with physically plausible materials, `camera.aperture` DOF and macro framing, `post` cinematic grade, `audio` VO. GAP: fur/felt materials, puppet facial animation, practical-set lighting realism.

## Judge's checklist
- Does the camera language (here, documentary) serve the idea?
- At 100% crop, do surfaces have believable material texture?
- Is the light motivated by a visible source in the set?
- Does a small performance detail (eyes, timing) carry emotion?
- Is there a deliberate palette contrast between two worlds?
