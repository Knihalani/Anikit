# WWF - Up in Smoke — NOMINT (2023)

**The most awarded animation craft piece of its year: stop-motion with real smoke, shot entirely in camera, scored by a choir.**

## The idea
Released for COP28, the film shows the effects of fossil-fuel emissions on people and the planet: a girl in a dark void, her home dissolving, chased by a plume of smoke that consumes animals and landscape (*source*: NOMINT Vimeo description; Colossal). Third in NOMINT's WWF series after a film made of melting ice ("Can't Negotiate the Melting Point of Ice") and one of live fire ("A Flammable Planet") — each film's material *is* the threat (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 102 s (*source*: oEmbed). Steady pace, *est.* 20–30 shots, median *est.* 3–5 s; the plume's continuous motion keeps holds alive.
- Arc (*source*: Colossal): girl on a melting ice floe → home disintegrating → smoke chases her → animals unable to escape → aftermath under a spotlight.

## Typography
None in the film itself (*observed* in stills); end card with WWF branding (*est.*).

## Colour and light
*observed* (Vimeo thumbnail): near-monochrome cool greys (~#9AA5A8 haze to ~#1E2224 shadow), a falling figure in a pale dress half-dissolved in smoke, lit by a single soft key from top left that the smoke scatters into a glowing veil. Colossal stills describe a "spotlight" in a dark space. Colour is almost removed so the smoke's tonal range carries everything.

## Motion techniques
- Over 700 unique full-colour 3D-printed sculptures of the girl for different poses (replacement stop-motion), and real smoke, all captured in camera over a month-long shoot within a year-long project (*source*: Vimeo description).
- Smoke is real fluid motion, so every frame has organic, unrepeatable detail; the figure moves in stop-motion steps against it (*est.*: the contrast of stepped character vs continuous smoke is the signature).

## Transitions
Smoke wipes: the plume engulfs the frame and reveals the next shot (*est.*); dissolves through haze.

## Sound design and music
A choral reinterpretation of Billie Eilish and Finneas's "When the Party's Over" by The Social Singing Choir (Margate), arranged by Hughie Gavin; sound design/mix by Rabbeats Music (*source*). Music-led, sparse, human voices only.

## What makes it world-class
1. Material = message: the film is literally made of smoke.
2. Physical light: a single key scattered through real smoke gives depth, glow and falloff that no flat composite has.
3. Tonal restraint: near-monochrome palette with a human figure as the only warm/pale element.
4. Score choice is specific (amateur choir, a known pop song slowed) and emotionally exact.
5. Craft density: 700+ printed poses, one month of shooting.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d` figure with pose replacement, a volumetric smoke `shader` (buffers for advection) lit by one key, `camera` with `aperture` DOF, `post` grain/vignette, `audio` choir track. GAP: true volumetric lighting through a fluid sim; stop-motion pose stepping (on twos) on a 3D character.

## Judge's checklist
- Is there one motivated key light, with visible scattering/falloff in the atmosphere?
- Does the medium itself carry the idea (not just illustrate it)?
- Is the palette restrained so tonal values, not hue, tell the story?
- Does the soundtrack use specific, non-generic instrumentation (here, human voices)?
- Do the hazy, soft frames still keep a clear subject silhouette?
