# Metamorphosis — BUCK for Good Books (2012) — classic

**The benchmark for fluid, ink-loose 2D in advertising: a Hunter S. Thompson rant animated as one continuous hallucination.**

## The idea
An imagined Hunter S. Thompson rants about needing to buy Kafka's *Metamorphosis* (which he despises) and concludes he must buy it from Good Books, whose profits go to Oxfam projects (*source*: Vimeo description; Open Culture). Concept by String Theory (NZ); BUCK directed (*source*: Motionographer).

Slightly older than 2015: included as the category's all-time classic of commissioned 2D.

## Structure and pacing
- 162 s (*source*: oEmbed). Fast; the image never stops transforming: *est.* few hard cuts, many metamorphoses, a new idea every 1–2 s, paced to the VO.
- Production: late June to November 2011 (*source*: Motionographer).

## Typography
None in frame (*observed* thumbnail); end card with Good Books URL (*est.*).

## Colour and light
*observed* (Vimeo thumbnail): black ink swirls on a mint/sea-green (~#7FD0B0) field, the character in a red floral shirt on an inner tube, lost in a whirlpool; small floating debris (bottle, fish, book). Limited palette: black, mint, cream skin, red/pink accents, a hint of violet. Ink shapes have brush texture and paint grain.

## Motion techniques
(*source*: Motionographer interview) Mixed 2D and 3D: only the book-to-flowers transition was a 3D morph; most shots were pencil-tested and finished in Flash, Photoshop and After Effects; 3D elements (Hunter's sunglasses, hat) served as references for cel animation; hair dynamics gave a base for loose squiggly and dotted lines; the beetle and goat scene used CG hero elements with illustrative textures drawn over and composited with paint texture. Method chosen shot by shot.

## Transitions
Continuous metamorphosis: forms flow into one another (liquid ink, the book becoming flowers) instead of cuts — the title is the technique (*source* + *est.*).

## Sound design and music
Music and sound design by Antfood; VO by Thor Erickson (*source*). The VO's manic cadence sets the edit.

## What makes it world-class
1. The technique enacts the title: everything metamorphoses, nothing simply cuts.
2. Hybrid pipeline chosen per shot (cel, Flash, CG under paint) so each beat gets the right tool, but the surface stays uniformly hand-made.
3. A limited ink palette with one strong accent per shot.
4. Animation fluidity matched to a voice performance.

## What Anikit would need
`shape` paths with animated morphs, `image` paint textures with `matte`, `post` grain, `camera`, `audio` VO. GAP: cel-animated characters; shape-to-shape morphs of complex illustrated forms; ink/paint texture over CG renders.

## Judge's checklist
- Do transitions happen *inside* the image (morph, liquid flow) rather than by cut or crossfade?
- Is the surface consistently hand-made (ink, brush, grain) even where CG is underneath?
- Does motion fluidity match the voice's energy?
- Is the palette limited with a clear accent per shot?
