# Benchmark set: the work Anikit is judged against

The user asked for "the best of the best videos out there to compare and compete against … a proper set of them, not just 1 or 2". This is that set: short-form motion work from the top studios, brands and artists, in 14 categories. Every piece has its own file with a precise analysis (idea, structure and pacing, type, colour and light, motion, transitions, sound, what makes it world-class, what Anikit would need, and a judge's checklist).

- **Patterns across the set, with numbers:** [WHAT-WORLD-CLASS-LOOKS-LIKE.md](WHAT-WORLD-CLASS-LOOKS-LIKE.md)
- **Pick the 3 closest references for one of our videos:** `python3 docs/video/judge/benchmark/tools/bench.py match --spec our.json [--analysis our.analysis.json]` (see [README.md](README.md))
- **Machine-readable:** [pieces.json](pieces.json) (generated by `bench.py index` from the front matter), [measured.json](measured.json) (stats from `anikit-video analyze` on legitimately obtained clips)
- **Tag words:** [VOCAB.md](VOCAB.md); file format: [_TEMPLATE.md](_TEMPLATE.md)

## How to use it when judging

1. Run `bench.py match` on the spec (and the analysis of the render). Read the 3 pieces it picks, in full.
2. Run `bench.py fetch <slug>` to cache their official stills (outside git), and put our frames next to theirs at the same size.
3. Answer each piece's **Judge's checklist** for our render. A "no" is a concrete gap; quote it.
4. Numbers marked *measured* come from `anikit-video analyze`; *source* numbers cite a link; *est.* numbers are estimates. Weigh them accordingly.

Copyright: only URLs, metadata and our own analysis are in git. Stills and clips are cached in `.cache/benchmark/` (gitignored) for evaluation only.
