# That's Framer — Framer (2025)

**Why it's in the set:** a 38 s product ad (≈ 33 M views on YouTube at time of research, *source*: YouTube listing) that explains a design tool by *doing* it: selection handles, layout boxes and a cursor-driven Publish click, cut fast on pure black. The reference for cursor and UI-zoom grammar.

## The idea
"How professional designers can turn their ideas into live, responsive websites without writing code… frames, text, and stacks… make everything responsive, then hit publish" (*source*: YouTube description). The film is the editor's own visual language (selection boxes, bounding handles, layer panel) blown up to full-frame.

## Structure and pacing
- 38 s (*source*: YouTube listing); published 14 Apr 2025 (*source*: YouTube).
- 25 % (hq1): full-frame canvas detail: concentric white rings and a blue→red gradient pill on black over a blurred blue ribbon, the pill wrapped in a selection box with 4 corner handles and a centre point (i.e. we are "inside" the canvas while an object is being transformed).
- 50 % (hq2): pure black frame with a hairline blue layout box on the left and a right-hand text stack built from separately boxed elements: headline "Imagine it & publish it" (light grotesk), subhead "The easiest way to turn your ideas into a live website", two buttons "Get started" (outline) and "Learn More" (white fill). Each element has its own 1 px blue bounding box: layout is shown as structure.
- 75 % (hq3): pure black, a single bright blue (#0099FF *est.*) rounded "Publish" button centred, white bold label, cursor arrow pressing it.
- Thumbnail (custom frame): editor UI (layers panel left, properties right, blue Publish button top right) around a site "The Internet Canvas".
- *est.* 20–28 shots, median ≈ 1–1.5 s, cut to music.

## Typography
- Display: a tight neo-grotesk (Inter Display–like *est.*) in white, large sizes, light to regular weight for headlines ("The Internet Canvas", "Imagine it & publish it"), tight leading.
- UI labels at product size (properties panel: "Breakpoint", "Width 1400 Fixed").
- The type is always UI content (headlines on a site being built), never an external caption.

## Colour and light
Pure black (#000) ground; white type; one product blue for selection/primary action (Framer Publish blue, #0099FF *est.*); content colour from the demo site (electric blue 3D ribbon, blue→red gradient pill). No lighting; flat screen graphics with high contrast.

## Motion techniques
- UI-zoom: the camera is inside the canvas at extreme scale (objects fill the frame), then pulls out to the full editor.
- Cursor as protagonist: one arrow cursor performs the decisive action (click Publish at ~75 %).
- Selection handles, bounding boxes and layout boxes animate on to explain structure.
- Scale contrast between macro UI details and full-editor shots.

## Transitions
Hard cuts on beat between black-ground shots (*est.*: black backgrounds in all three frames make every cut seamless).

## Sound design and music
Not documented; *est.* upbeat electronic track with click sfx on cursor actions.

## What makes it world-class
1. The product's own UI language (handles, boxes, Publish button) is the graphic language; no invented decorative motion.
2. Black ground in every shot: cuts are invisible and focus is total.
3. One cursor, one decisive action, framed alone at the centre (Publish).
4. Real content (a designed site with copy and imagery), not placeholder cards.
5. Fast cut rate with each shot carrying exactly one idea (transform → layout → publish).

## What Anikit would need
- `composition` scenes with UI built from `box`/`text`/`shape`; `component` "cursor" with click; `camera` zoom/pan for UI-zoom; hairline `shape` rect outlines with corner handles; `transitions` `cut` on `@beat` markers; `audio` with `click` sfx.
- No major GAP; the risk is falling into placeholder UI (BAR.md #3).

## Judge's checklist
- Does the UI shown use real product affordances (selection handles, layout boxes, properties) with real copy?
- Is there one clear cursor action per beat, with the cursor large and readable?
- Do UI-zoom moves go from macro (an element fills the frame) to context (whole editor) or vice versa?
- Is the ground one flat colour across shots so hard cuts feel seamless?
- Is the median shot ≤ 1.5 s while every headline still holds long enough to read?
