# Introducing: Material 3 Expressive — Google Design (2025)

**Why it's in the set:** a design-system launch where the motion *is* the spec: springy component morphs, shape changes and colour themes cut in a rapid grid rhythm. The reference for flat-UI motion that still feels physical (springs, squash) without 3D.

## The idea
"The latest update to Google's design system—Material Design—adds vibrant colors, intuitive motion, adaptive components, flexible typography, and contrasting shapes" (*source*: YouTube description, published 13 May 2025). Material 3 Expressive introduced spring-based motion ("spatial springs mirror the physics of how objects actually move"; "effects springs" for colour and opacity), 35 new shapes with built-in shape morphing, emphasised type styles and new components (button groups, FAB menu, loading indicators, split button, toolbars) (*source*: 9to5Google).

## Structure and pacing
- 39 s (*source*: YouTube listing).
- 25 % (hq1): bento grid of nine dark-plum (#2B0F33 *est.*) tiles on a deep purple ground, each tile demonstrating one component: FAB menu (Select / Add photos / Share album / Search with a close FAB), a floating toolbar, a segmented "Going / Not Going / Maybe" group, wavy and straight progress bars, the new scalloped loading indicator, an editing toolbar with a coral selected button, a navigation rail with "Add friend".
- 50 % (hq2): off-white ground with a loose cluster of cyan/lilac buttons at very different sizes and shapes (circle, pill, squircle): "Play" large in the centre, icon buttons around; scale contrast as composition.
- 75 % (hq3): grid of photographic portraits alternating with colour-scheme swatch strips (coral/pink/lilac, peach/blue, charcoal/mint, yellow, orange…): dynamic colour derived from people's photos.
- Thumbnail: "Material 3 Expressive" in white rounded sans over a lilac/coral radial gradient.
- *est.* 25–35 shots, median ≈ 1 s.

## Typography
Rounded-terminal geometric sans (Google Sans Flex with rounded setting *est.* from the rounded stroke ends in the thumbnail), heavy weight, white; UI labels in the same family at component size.

## Colour and light
Per-section palettes: deep purple/plum dark theme with lilac and coral accents; light off-white with cyan and lilac; multi-scheme swatches. Flat colour, no lighting; colour itself is the subject (dynamic theming).

## Motion techniques
- Spring physics on every component (overshoot and settle), shape morphing (e.g. scalloped loader, button shape changes) (*source*: system features; *est.* that the film demonstrates them).
- Bento/grid layouts where many components animate simultaneously; scale contrast clusters.

## Transitions
*est.* hard cuts on the beat between grid compositions; colour swaps between themes.

## Sound design and music
Not documented; *est.* upbeat track.

## What makes it world-class
1. Motion demonstrates the system's real physics (springs, morphs), so the film doubles as the spec.
2. Grid/bento compositions fill the frame with many simultaneous, real components (no empty frame, no placeholder bars).
3. Scale contrast in button clusters creates hierarchy without type.
4. Colour is the content: palettes derived from photos.
5. Very short shots, but each reads instantly because every tile contains one component.

## What Anikit would need
- `composition` grids of `box` components; `shape` with radius animation; `mograph` repeaters for grids; `transitions` `cut`; `animate` with spring-like ease (`outBack`/`spring` if available); `audio`.
- GAP: Material-accurate spring presets; arbitrary rounded-polygon shape morphing.

## Judge's checklist
- Does each animated UI element settle with a physical spring (visible overshoot and decay) rather than a linear/ease tween?
- Are frames filled with many real components (grid/bento), with no empty space or placeholder bars?
- Is scale contrast used to create hierarchy?
- Does each section have a deliberate palette (theme) and switch on a cut?
- Are shapes morphing smoothly between rounded forms without kinks?
