# TED-ED | How does artificial intelligence learn? — Champ Panupong Techawongthawon (2021)

**TED-Ed at its best: an abstract topic (machine learning) turned into a graphic system of orbits, balls and gradient tubes, by one director who art-directed, designed and animated it.**

## The idea
Explain supervised, unsupervised and reinforcement learning to a general audience (script by educator Briana Brownell, narrated by Safia Elhillo — *source*: Vimeo credits). Champ's approach: data as small coloured spheres moving on concentric tracks; learning is shown as order emerging from motion (*est.* from the Vimeo thumbnail: concentric rings with marbles at different phases). He writes he "art directed, designed and animated" it himself and that Ambrose Yu's music "drives this animation" (*source*).

Note: 278 s, longer than 90 s; analyse a representative 60 s around the thumbnail frame (YouTube/Vimeo poster), *est.* 1:30–2:30.

## Colour and light
Thumbnail (*observed*): near-black ground (~#05090B) with concentric rings alternating black and colour; rings carry long gradients (ultramarine ~#2F5BE0 → periwinkle; tangerine ~#F7A23A → cream; coral ~#F2503A; leaf green ~#2E7D4F; pink ~#F3B8D8). Balls are soft-shaded spheres with a white specular and some half-black/half-white "binary" balls. Gradients are airbrushed with soft inner glow — vector shapes with a light source, not flat.

## Motion techniques
- Orbit/loop animation: marbles travelling around rings at different speeds (*est.* from layout); concentric repetition (repeater) with phase offsets.
- Gradient tubes likely draw on along their arcs (trim paths) and colour shifts mark classification (*est.*).
- Rhythmic sync to Ambrose Yu's score — the description says the music "drives this animation" (*source*).

## Transitions
Graphic matches: circles scale or collapse into the next diagram (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Composer and sound designer Ambrose Yu (*source*). Rhythmic, percussive electronic-acoustic score with SFX ticks on ball movement (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. A single invented visual system (balls on tracks) explains three different algorithms, instead of stock AI imagery (no robots, no glowing brains).
2. Dark ground + saturated airbrushed gradients with specular balls = depth and light in pure 2D.
3. Strong radial symmetry and concentric repetition make the frame dense edge to edge.
4. Motion is choreographed to the score.

## What Anikit would need
`shape` circles/arcs with gradient strokes, `mograph.repeat` for concentric rings, `path` + `progress` for balls on orbits, `glow`/`post` bloom for soft highlights, `audio` beat sync (`iBeat`) for timing. GAP: gradient *along* a stroke (conic/arc-length gradient) with soft edge glow.
