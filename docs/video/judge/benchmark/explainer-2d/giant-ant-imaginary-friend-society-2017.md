# The Imaginary Friend Society // Finding Out You Have Cancer — Giant Ant (2017)

**The benchmark for warmth in an explainer: a hard medical subject told to children through a crayon-textured, character-led world.**

## The idea
One of 20 shorts (each by a different studio, pro bono) for the Pediatric Brain Tumor Foundation, conceived by RPA, in which imaginary friends explain cancer care to kids (*source*: WePresent, Cartoon Brew). Giant Ant's episode covers the moment of diagnosis. Jay Grandin aimed for "a balance between being optimistic, without it seeming forced", using a saturated palette and loose, child-like brushwork as "an on-ramp" for kids to picture themselves in it (*source*: WePresent).

## Structure and pacing
- 119 s (*source*: oEmbed).
- Still file names on Giant Ant's page run s03 SLIDE, s05 BOOKS, s09 CAULDRON, s14 TUG, s15 DOCTORS/HOVERTANK, s17 EUGENE, s19 TOP FOREST → at least 19 numbered shots (*source*: file names) → median shot *est.* ~5–6 s. Steady, VO-paced, each shot a self-contained illustrated tableau with character acting.
- Arc (*est.* from shot names): the friend (a pink, wizard-hatted character) appears → explains (books, cauldron/potion = treatment) → the fight (tug-of-war against a grey spiky monster, with tanks and UFOs on the kids' side = doctors and medicine) → resolution in the forest.

## Typography
None in the stills; the narration carries everything (*observed*). Series title card by Elastic (*source*: Motionographer), not part of Giant Ant's frames.

## Colour and light
- **Cauldron (s09):** deep violet ground (~#2A1F4A *est.*) with a yellow light cone (~#F7EE9A) from top centre pouring onto the cauldron; trees as flat triangles in teal, cobalt, mint, orange; fire character in orange-red (~#F2683A). Light is *motivated*: the cone is the focal device, the edges are vignetted with near-black purple foreground silhouettes (depth by overlapping planes).
- **Tug-of-war (s15):** white ground, shapes floating in space; the cast in pink (~#F0607A), mint, mustard, purple; the monster in faceted blue-greys with dark purple limbs. Faceted shapes (planes of two tones) fake volume without 3D.
- Every fill carries visible crayon/coloured-pencil strokes and grain; outlines are absent — the shape edges are the drawing.

## Motion techniques
- Character animation (credits list 7 character animators, incl. Henrique Barone, Alex Grigg, Allen Laseter) on top of 2D compositing — *source*: credits. Likely frame-by-frame acting for faces and hands over rigged/flat-shape bodies (*est.*).
- Texture boil on fills (*est.*: hand-textured style typically animated with a looping texture shift).
- Layered foreground silhouettes imply parallax camera moves (*est.* from s09 composition).

## Transitions
Mostly hard cuts between tableaux on VO phrase ends (*est.*); some scene-to-scene continuity by keeping the pink friend in frame.

## Sound design and music
Original music and sound design by Antfood (*source*). VO-led; soft, playful score; character Foley (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Tone control: frightening subject, but the palette (saturated, warm, light cone) and characters keep it safe — the visual answers the brief "optimistic, not forced".
2. Texture is total: crayon strokes in every fill; no flat default vector anywhere.
3. Volume without 3D: two-tone facets on the monster and tanks; depth by layered planes and a motivated light cone.
4. Each shot is a complete, readable illustration (one subject, clear silhouette) that holds as a poster.
5. Metaphor carries the medicine (potion = treatment, tanks = doctors), so no diagrams are needed.

## What Anikit would need
Illustrated plates as `image` layers in `group`s with `depth` for parallax; `shape` paths for cast; `post` grain; `camera` push-ins; `audio` VO + music. GAP: expressive frame-by-frame character acting; procedural crayon/pencil fill texture (could be a `shader` over shape mattes); texture boil (animated noise offset at 8–12 fps).

## Judge's checklist
- Does every shot have one clear subject with a readable silhouette at thumbnail size?
- Is there a motivated light source (cone, glow) rather than flat even fill?
- Do all fills carry hand texture so no area looks like a default vector flat?
- Does the palette set an emotional tone that matches the narration (warm reassurance here)?
- Are characters *acting* (faces, hands, timing), not just sliding?
