# School of Motion: Manifesto — Ordinary Folk (2019)

**An explainer whose visual complexity *is* the argument: it grows from single line work to many styles in three dimensions, mirroring a student's growth.**

## The idea
School of Motion commissioned Ordinary Folk to unveil its rebrand and mission ("a compelling alternative to traditional four-year art schools", *source*: SOM blog). Ordinary Folk's concept (*source*, Vimeo description): "the visuals would begin as simple shapes + line work, and gradually increase in complexity to incorporate multiple styles of design in all three dimensions working together — to illustrate how School of Motion's education philosophy can help take almost anybody from novice to superstar." Tagline: "Join the Movement."

## Structure and pacing
- 110 s (*source*: oEmbed; studio page says 1:50).
- Shot/scene numbering on styleframes runs to at least S20 and SH26 (*source*: file names on ordinaryfolk.co) → ≥26 shots → median shot *est.* ≤3.5 s; many shots transform in place, so visual change is roughly every 1–2 s (*est.*).
- Three acts *est.* from the concept and stills: (1) monoline geometry on paper white; (2) flat-colour characters and shapes; (3) multi-style finale with 3D spheres, gradients, motion streaks, DOF.

## Typography
VO-led (Megan Hensley, *source*); on-screen type minimal. Characters are built from letterforms (an "n" as a body/legs, an "e" as a torso in still S19) — type as anatomy rather than captions (*observed*).

## Colour and light
- **Act 1 (still 9):** off-white paper (~#F4F2EE *est.*) with a single deep aubergine line (~#2B1B3D), 3–4 px at 1080p *est.*, slight print speckle — compass geometry (square, quatrefoil, circles, crossing diagonals) symmetrical about centre.
- **Act 2 (still S19, design Nuria Boj):** same warm off-white ground; limited palette of ultramarine (~#3B4CC0), vermilion (~#F2483A), mustard yellow (~#F5C842), lilac shadow pads (~#8F8FE0), blush (~#E7C9C3); small soft gradients and glassy highlights on spheres/torus — "flat with a dash of material".
- **Act 3 (still S20):** saturated teal/green gradient (~#3BD5C8 → #4CD47F) speed tunnel, chunky purple 3D-ish character with iridescent rim colours, white-hot flash bloom at left, chrome spheres with motion blur, streak lines, foreground out-of-focus ribbons (DOF) and fine grain. Light is directional and hot.

## Motion techniques
- Line draw-on and geometric construction in act 1 (*est.* from monoline still; the "pencil drawing a line" still S24 shows a hand-drawn pencil entering frame).
- Character animation from many hands (8 animators + 4 alumni, *source*), each shot handled in a different style → a style relay.
- Act 3 uses speed lines, motion blur and DOF on foreground elements (*observed* in S20).

## Transitions
In-camera transitions: shapes from one shot become the next (geometry → character), and the camera dives through elements (*est.* from Act 3 tunnel and concept of continuous growth).

## Sound design and music
Music & SFX by Ambrose Yu (*source*); the Motionographer article names Lawrence's "Casualty" as the mood reference (*source*). Upbeat, live-feel groove under VO; SFX accents on shape builds (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. A structural idea you can see: complexity, palette and dimensionality rise monotonically across the piece.
2. One ground colour (warm off-white) unifies acts 1–2, so the jump to the saturated act 3 lands as a payoff.
3. Characters built from geometric primitives and letterforms, each with a grounding shadow pad — consistent design language.
4. Act 3 frames have real lens behaviour (bloom from a hot source, DOF foreground, motion streaks) — the opposite of flat.
5. Style diversity without chaos: palette rules persist while the rendering style changes.

## What Anikit would need
`shape` paths with `trimEnd` draw-on for act 1; `shape` + gradient fills, `shadow` for act 2; `scene3d`/`camera.aperture` DOF, `motionBlur`, `post` bloom/grain for act 3; `echo` for speed streaks. GAP: smooth morphs between rendering styles (line → flat → 3D) of the same character.

## Judge's checklist
- Does visual complexity escalate deliberately across the piece (not uniform from frame 1)?
- Is there a consistent ground/palette rule that makes the final escalation feel earned?
- Do act-3-style frames show real lens cues (bloom from a hot source, DOF, motion blur) that read as intended, not as defects?
- Are characters built from one geometric language and grounded (shadow pads)?
- Does any change happen at least every ~2 s?
