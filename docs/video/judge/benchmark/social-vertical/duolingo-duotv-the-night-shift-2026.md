# DuoTV Ep 6 "The Night Shift" — Duolingo in-house (2026)

**Why it's in the set:** a vertical-first 2D animated series made entirely in-house by a brand design team (Duolingo absorbed animation studios Gunner and Animade and motion team Hobbes); the reference for character-led 2D animation composed for 9:16, Saturday-morning-cartoon style.

## The idea
DuoTV: six short episodes in which Duo, Lily, her friend Zari and Falstaff the bear work at a mysterious supermarket, "DuoMart"; each episode stands alone but feeds a wider arc; styled after classic Saturday-morning cartoons; released on Duolingo's YouTube and Instagram; the brand's first animated series made fully in-house (*source*: Fast Company Brasil). Scripts were A/B tested like the brand's other content (*source*). This is the finale (*source*: title), published Oct 2026 (*source*: Shorts listing "Oct 16, 2026").

## Structure and pacing
- 9:16 confirmed (*source*: oEmbed 113×200). Length not verified (null).
- *est.* 1–2 min, cut on dialogue beats every 1–2 s (cartoon pacing).

## Typography
Type appears only as in-world signage, hand-lettered: "STAFF ONLY" sign in condensed, slightly irregular red-brown caps with an outline (oardefault still). No overlay titles in the frame seen.

## Colour and light
Vertical still: a dim back-of-store corridor in olive/ochre (#6B5A2A *est.*) with a dark teal swing door; Duo (saturated Duolingo green, #58CC02-ish *est.*, with a white shirt collar and rainbow stripe, orange feet) stands in a lit doorway, back-lit from a pale cyan room; a hard light spill fans across a brown/cream checkerboard floor as projected light shapes; a glowing cyan keypad on the wall is the only other emissive. Flat cel colour, black/dark-brown outlines, light done as flat shapes (spill, glow), not gradients.

## Motion techniques
- 2D character animation (cel-style), acting through expressions (Duo's half-lidded side-eye).
- Lighting as graphic shapes: doorway spill polygons and soft glow on the keypad.
- Vertical staging: doorway as a frame-within-frame centred low; character in the lower-middle third; sign in the upper third.

## Transitions
Hard cuts (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Not documented; *est.* dialogue, cartoon sfx, music cues.

## What makes it world-class
1. Composition is built for 9:16 (tall doorway, vertical light spill, sign above), not cropped from 16:9.
2. Light and mood are designed with flat shapes, so a 2D frame has atmosphere and a clear focal point.
3. Character acting carries the joke; the brand appears as characters, not logos.
4. Series structure (standalone + arc) for social return viewing.
5. A consistent, ownable palette (Duo green against warm desaturated environments).

## What Anikit would need
- `shape`/`image`/`group` scenes with flat colour and outlines; light spills as `shape` polygons with `blend: "add"`/`screen` and `glow`; `camera` subtle push; `captions`; `audio`.
- GAP: character animation (rigs, lip sync, acting).

## Judge's checklist
- Is the composition designed for 9:16 (vertical lines, frame-within-frame), not a crop of a wide frame?
- Is light expressed graphically (spill shapes, glows) to create a single focal point?
- Is the main character in the lower-middle or centre, clear of platform UI?
- Is the palette ownable, with the brand colour reserved for the hero?
- Do cuts land on story/joke beats?
