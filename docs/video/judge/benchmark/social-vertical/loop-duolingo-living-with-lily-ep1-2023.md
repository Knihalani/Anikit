# Living with Lily, Ep 1 "The One With Deep Fried Duo" — Loop for Duolingo (2023)

**Why it's in the set:** the benchmark for *native* vertical brand storytelling: a ten-episode, one-minute sitcom that puts Duolingo's app characters (as giant costume heads) into a '90s multicam sitcom set, built for YouTube Shorts and TikTok. 8.6 M views on this episode on YouTube alone at time of research (*source*: YouTube listing). Not motion design; included as the social-format reference for hook, framing and comedic timing in 9:16.

## The idea
Lily (Duolingo's deadpan purple-haired teen character) "lives" in a sitcom; across ten one-minute episodes she has an existential crisis and discovers she is trapped in a simulation; the show leans on cheesy '90s sitcom tropes, from laugh track to theme music (*source*: MediaPost via search summary). Episode 1's caption is a "recipe" for a "perfect green chicken" (i.e. deep-frying Duo the owl), a dark joke in the brand's established social voice (*source*: YouTube description).

## Structure and pacing
- 9:16 confirmed (*source*: oEmbed 113×200). Episode length ≈ 1 min (*source*: MediaPost via search summary); exact seconds not verified (length_s null).
- Published 7 Nov 2023 (*source*: YouTube).
- *est.* sitcom coverage: wide master + singles, cuts every 2–3 s on dialogue and laugh beats.

## Typography
No designed type in the frame seen; platform captions/titles carry text. Brand typography is absent by design: it reads as a TV show, not an ad.

## Colour and light
Vertical frame (oardefault still): Lily in a full costume with an oversized foam head (lavender bob hair, heavy purple eye make-up, black headphones with purple D-pad earcups), purple velour tracksuit, seated at a round table with a eucalyptus-print tablecloth in a warm, flatly lit suburban kitchen set (cream cabinets, brick backsplash, net curtains); a co-star in a blue apron leans in from frame right. Lighting is high-key, multicam-sitcom flat with warm practicals: deliberately "TV".

## Motion techniques
Live action. Comedy is in performance and costume; Lily's foam head is centred in the upper third, where a phone viewer's eye lands, with the body and props filling the vertical frame.

## Transitions
Hard cuts (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
'90s-style sitcom theme music and laugh track (*source*: MediaPost via search summary).

## What makes it world-class
1. Format borrowed wholesale from a known genre (multicam sitcom) and re-framed for 9:16, so it reads as entertainment, not advertising.
2. The brand's own characters carry it; the product is never pitched.
3. The face/head is placed in the upper-middle third of the vertical frame, safe from platform UI at top and bottom.
4. Episodic structure (ten episodes) drives return viewing.
5. Tone is consistent with the brand's established social voice (deadpan, dark jokes).

## What Anikit would need
- Only the finishing layer: `captions` (word-synced, safe-area aware), title/end cards, `audio` (laugh track, theme sting). The live-action production itself is a GAP outside Anikit's scope.

## Judge's checklist
- Is the key subject (face, product, word) inside the 9:16 safe area (clear of the top 15 % and bottom 20 % platform UI)?
- Does the first second establish character and situation without needing sound?
- Does it read as native platform content rather than a resized 16:9 ad?
- Is there a recurring character/brand device instead of a product pitch?
- Is comic or rhythmic timing tight (a beat or cut every 2–3 s)?
