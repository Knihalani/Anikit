# A peek at some handmade magic (#MacBookNeo) — Apple (2026)

**Why it's in the set:** Apple's vertical behind-the-scenes cut of its "Hello, MacBook Neo" launch film, showing that the "flashy" effects were made in camera with stop-motion and practical props. Reference for how a premium brand uses 9:16 to show craft, with stacked split-screens and overhead tabletop framing.

## The idea
Apple called it "a peek at some handmade magic" (*source*: title). The launch ad relied on physical craft rather than full CGI: stop-motion animation, LED strip lights, miniature confetti cannons and a simulated trackpad, captured in camera and combined with digital effects in post; a stop-motion fireworks sequence is one example (*source*: PetaPixel, 24 Apr 2026, via search summary). Published 24 Apr 2026 (*source*: YouTube).

## What makes it world-class
1. 9:16 is used as a stack: three horizontal panels tell cause → process → effect in one frame.
2. The craft is the message: visible rigs and hands make the effect credible and charming.
3. Light is practical and coloured by real materials (gels, LEDs), so glows have real falloff and texture.
4. Clean overhead framing with the product centred and rig elements placed symmetrically at the edges.
5. Pastel product colour against white keeps it on brand.
