# Syria's war: Who is fighting and why — Vox (2017 update)

**The Vox map explainer at its peak: a moving map, a scrubbing timeline and cut-out actors, so a viewer can follow a multi-sided war in minutes.**

## The idea
Explain the Syrian civil war's sides and foreign backers ("divided between four sides, each side with foreign backers", *source*: Vox description in search results) on a single map, with a running timeline of events. The 2015 original, "Syria's war: a 5 minute history" by Max Fisher and Johnny Harris, won the Information is Beautiful Awards 2015 bronze (Motion Infographic, *source*). This YouTube upload's timeline runs to "Trump elected", "Assad uses WMD", "USA attacks" (*observed* hq3), so it is the updated 2017 cut (year *est.* from those events); the award belongs to the 2015 version, so `awards` is empty here.

Length: ~7 min (*source*: search-result summaries); `length_s` null. Analyse the 60 s around the 2012 timeline position (hq1), where actors are introduced on the map.

## Structure and pacing
- One persistent map plane; the camera pans/zooms between Syria and the wider world (USA in hq3); actors pop on as cut-outs; a timeline strip at the bottom scrolls, its current chip enlarged (*observed* hq1, hq3).
- *est.* median "shot" 3–5 s; in practice continuous camera moves with new elements every 1–3 s.

## Typography
- Actor labels: heavy condensed sans, white, reversed out of black boxes ("ASSAD", "KURDS", "USA" — *observed*), sitting under the cut-out photo.
- Timeline chips: italic serif (Vox-style editorial serif) in black on cream cards ("Kurds secede", "Trump elected", "Fall of Aleppo"), the current chip larger with a red triangle marker above and year beneath (*observed*).
- Big faint map labels ("TURKEY", "SYRIA") in a light grey all-caps grotesk.

## Colour and light
*observed*: pale map paper ground (~#E3E6DC, sea slightly greener), thin dark border lines, territory fills in muted blue (~#9FB8D8 for USA). Cut-out photos desaturated/duotone (Assad in pinkish duotone on a pink label, Kurdish fighter in sepia). Mild paper texture. Colour is reserved to mark factions.

## Motion techniques
Camera pans and zooms across the map; cut-out photo pop-ons; territory fills appearing; timeline strip sliding horizontally to the current event (*est.* from layout).

## Transitions
Camera moves on the same map plane (no cuts) between regions; occasional cut to archival footage (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
VO-led (Vox narrator), light rhythmic bed and UI ticks on timeline steps (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. A persistent spatial frame (the map) plus a persistent temporal frame (the timeline strip): the viewer always knows *where* and *when*.
2. Faction identity carried by consistent label style and colour; photos are cut-outs, so actors are characters on the map.
3. Editorial typography: two families (condensed sans for actors, italic serif for events) with clear roles.
4. Restrained palette lets the faction colours carry meaning.

## What Anikit would need
`image` base map and cut-out photo layers; `text` labels on black `box`es; a `box` row for the timeline with an animated highlight; `camera` pan/zoom; `post` grain/paper texture; `audio` VO. GAP: a map component with country outlines and animated territory fills from data.

## Judge's checklist
- Is there a persistent spatial and/or temporal anchor that orients the viewer throughout?
- Do type styles have distinct roles (actor vs event vs place) applied consistently?
- Is colour reserved for meaning (factions/territory), with a neutral ground?
- Are photos treated (cut-out, duotone) to fit one visual system?
