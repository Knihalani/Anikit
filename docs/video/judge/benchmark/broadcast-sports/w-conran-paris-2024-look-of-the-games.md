# #Paris2024 - Look of the Games — W Conran Design (2023)

**Why it's in the set: the Olympic graphic system as motion; a modular grid derived from Paris cobblestones that animates into every venue, screen and broadcast surface.**

oEmbed: author "Paris 2024"; 2:01 on the YouTube results page (length_s 121). The pictograms film (1:01, "Paris 2024") is the first alt. Year: the look was revealed in February 2023 (*source*: IOC news title "Paris 2024 reveals look of the upcoming Games and 62 new sports pictograms"; date *est.* from upload age).

## The idea
"Based on cobblestone pavement as an iconic feature of Parisian streets"; the cobblestones are "modular and infinitely adaptable", playful through a "bold colour palette" (*source*: Red Dot). It "refused to fall into postcard clichés" (*source*: D&AD). Square blocks are the unit of the design grid; each arrondissement and venue can personalise it (*source*: search summary). Art Deco from the 1924 Paris Games is a named inspiration (*source*: theolympicdesign.com).

## Structure and pacing
A system film: pavé grid fills and rebuilds, applied across venues, uniforms, wayfinding and broadcast; longer explanatory beats than a trailer (*est.* median 2–3 s from a 2 min reveal film).

## Typography
A bespoke Art-Deco-inflected display face for Paris 2024 (*est.*; name not confirmed in sources read) set large within grid cells; 62 new pictograms (*source*: IOC news title).

## Colour and light
Bold blue, red, green and violet (*source*: search summary of Paris 2024 colour announcement), flat, no lighting.

## Motion techniques
Grid-based modular animation: cells flip colour, tiles slide, patterns propagate across the pavé grid; logos and pictograms snap into cells (*est.* from the modular system).

## Transitions
Grid wipes and colour-swap transitions where tiles change state in sequence (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Music-led reveal film (*est.*; no credit found).

## What makes it world-class
1. One unit (the pavé square) generates the whole visual world, so any frame is on-system.
2. Bold, limited palette applied flat with complete confidence.
3. Scales from a pictogram to a stadium without losing identity.
4. Locally adaptable, globally consistent.

## What Anikit would need
- `mograph` grid repeat of `shape` tiles with staggered `animate`, `mask` wipes, `text` in cells, `box` layout.
- GAP: a pattern-system generator (rules for tile states across arbitrary layouts) rather than hand-placed tiles.

## Judge's checklist
- Is every element snapped to one visible unit grid?
- Does the palette stay within 4–5 named colours, used flat?
- Do transitions propagate through the grid in an ordered sequence (not random flicker)?
- Is the system shown working across several real applications, not one abstract canvas?
- Is there a specific local reference (here, the cobblestones) rather than generic sport graphics?
