# Let's Make It Iconic — Blinkink / Nicos Livesey (2026)

**Why it's in the set: a current, top-tier broadcast sports trailer that animates "on everything" (knitwear, badges, nails, bobbleheads), mixing documentary live action with stop-motion; the reference for tactile texture and object-to-object transitions.**

oEmbed: author "BBC Trailers"; 0:52 on the YouTube results page. The BBC's "Making Of Titles" (2:00, oEmbed author "BBC") is the first alt and documents process.

## The idea
Football belongs to its fans: the build-up told through the physical culture of fandom, turning everyday objects into storytelling devices (*source*: Stash).

## Structure and pacing
Documentary-style live action of fans intercut with animated objects; each object hands off to the next in "complex transitions" (*source*: Stash). Documented frames: cartoon players in orange No. 8 shirts on a brick wall going from despair to celebration; a woven textile player in blue/black stripes; red-white-blue nail art with a kicking player; an enamel pin portrait on denim by a brass zip; a "YAMAL" bobblehead among bucket hats (*source*: Stash image captions). Median shot *est.* 1–1.5 s.

## Typography
Supporting; tournament and BBC end frame alongside the FIFA World Cup 2026 logo (*source*: search summary of press). Type otherwise appears on objects (names on shirts, bobblehead label).

## Colour and light
Saturated kit colours (orange, blue, black, yellow; red/white/blue nails) on real-world textures (brick, denim) (*source*: Stash captions). Grade by Black Kite (Rich Fearon) (*source*).

## Motion techniques
Stop-motion (three animators, DOP Malcolm Hadley), 2D animation (lead Reg Issac), illustration by Dan Evans, traditional fabrication: knitting, woven textiles, enamel badges, nail art (*source*: Stash). Live action "intimate and unpolished", camera moving "with immediacy and intent" (*source*). Director: "The imperfections, the fingerprints, the texture are what make it feel alive" (*source*).

## Transitions
Object-to-object: an animated figure on one material continues its action on the next material (*est.* from "each object behaving differently in complex transitions").

## Sound design and music
Track: **Tomora, "Somewhere Else"**; sound design/mix by Factory (Mark Hills) (*source*: Stash).

## What makes it world-class
1. Every surface is a real, textured material, so the animation has fingerprints, not vector smoothness.
2. The same character action continues across different media (knit to enamel to nail), stitching the edit.
3. Fan culture specifics (bucket hats, bobbleheads, pins) make it feel true to football.
4. Live action and animation share one handheld, documentary energy.

## What Anikit would need
- `video`/`image` layers of real textures, `mask` reveals, cut `transitions` on action, `camera` shake for handheld, `post` grain, `audio` beat markers.
- GAP: stop-motion and craft fabrication; Anikit could only approximate with textured `image` sequences and `boil`-style jitter.

## Judge's checklist
- Do surfaces show real texture at 100% crop (fibres, grain, scratches), not flat fills?
- Does a motion continue across a cut between two different materials or media?
- Is there a specific cultural detail in most shots, not generic "sport" imagery?
- Is camera energy deliberate (handheld immediacy) rather than floaty?
- Does the end frame resolve cleanly to broadcaster + tournament branding?
