# BBC Tokyo 2020 ("Let's Go There") — Factory Fifteen / Nexus Studios (2021)

**Why it's in the set: a sports trailer built as one continuous camera move through an over-designed, real-feeling city, with every frame packed with authored detail; the reference for density and the "camera as character".**

oEmbed: "BBC Tokyo 2020", Factory Fifteen, 60 s. The BBC's own upload is the first alt (oEmbed author "BBC"; 1:07 on the results page). BTS film on Factory Fifteen's Vimeo (166 s).

## The idea
Reimagine Tokyo "with Olympic athletes and events embedded into its fabric and culture" (*source*: Factory Fifteen). Athletes never appear in person: Jason and Laura Kenny as manga posters, Katarina Johnson-Thompson as gashapon toys, Sky Brown as tourist merch, Dina Asher-Smith and Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce as Street-Fighter-style arcade characters, a J-pop dancer doing Simone Biles' mic drop (*source*: Dezeen).

## Structure and pacing
Six interconnected scenes in 60 s: street kiosk, J-pop music video, video arcade, gashapon shop, teenager's bedroom, rooftops ending on neon signage (*source*: Dezeen) = ~10 s per environment (*est.* from 60/6). "Shot to feel like a single continuous camera move" (*source*: Factory Fifteen), so there are no visible cuts; pace comes from camera speed and detail density. Over 50 planted easter eggs (*source*: Factory Fifteen).

## Typography
Diegetic Japanese/English signage, packaging and arcade UI designed with Japanese artist **Fantasista Utamaro** (*source*: Factory Fifteen). Type is part of the set dressing rather than overlaid; the BBC Sport end frame closes it (*est.*).

## Colour and light
Night-time neon (magenta, cyan, warm sodium), saturated pop-culture palettes in the arcade and bedroom (*est.* from Dezeen stills and the neon-rooftop ending, *source* for neon ending).

## Motion techniques
Remote filming in Japan and London plus built sets (cycling bedroom set, J-pop set) (*source*: Factory Fifteen), stitched with CG and VFX (VFX and colour by The Mill, *source*: search summary of Shots credits) into one camera path. Parallax through foreground props, whip-like accelerations between rooms (*est.*).

## Transitions
All in camera: the camera passes through screens, windows and doorways to reach the next environment (*est.* from "single continuous camera move").

## Sound design and music
Score by anime composer **Kenji Kawai** mixing traditional Japanese instruments with min'yō-style vocals (*source*: Dezeen); sound design Mark Hills (*source*: search summary).

## What makes it world-class
1. One unbroken camera journey through six worlds; the transitions are architecture, not effects.
2. Information density: every surface carries authored, culturally specific artwork (50+ easter eggs).
3. Athletes appear as culture (toys, posters, game characters), which is the idea, not decoration.
4. Live action, sets and CG are matched so seamlessly that the city feels real.

## What Anikit would need
- `camera` path with `depth` parallax, `scene3d`, `image`/`video` plates, `glow` on neon, `motionBlur`, `post`.
- GAP: a continuous 3D camera move through several authored environments with seamless joins; Anikit's scene model cuts between scenes, so it would need one long `scene3d` world or a camera-through-portal transition.
- GAP: dense bespoke illustration assets (Anikit can place them, not author them).

## Judge's checklist
- Does the piece hold one continuous camera move for most of its length, with transitions motivated by objects in the scene?
- At any paused frame, are there at least 3 distinct authored details (signage, props, characters)?
- Is the subject (athletes/sport) embedded in the world rather than pasted on top?
- Is neon/light glow motivated by actual light sources in the scene?
- Does the camera speed vary (accelerations, settles) rather than gliding at constant speed?
