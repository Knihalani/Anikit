# Welcome to the City of Love — Nexus Studios / Fx Goby (2024)

**Why it's in the set: 58 shots and 36 athletes in 60 seconds of outline-free 2D animation, cut to Piaf; the reference for editorial rhythm and hand-made craft in a sports promo.**

oEmbed: author "Nexus Studios"; 1:00 from the YouTube results page and "60 seconds" on Nexus' page (*source*). BBC's own upload is the first alt.

## The idea
"Paris has never seen a love like this": a parallel between romantic love and athletes' passion for their sport; "snapshots of love interrupted" by athletes training (*source*: Nexus Studios; Stash).

## Structure and pacing
58 shots in 60 s (*source*: Nexus Studios) = mean shot ~1.03 s (*est.* computed). Expect slower romantic shots early, accelerating to sub-second sport cuts at the musical climax (*est.*, standard arc for the rearranged song).

## Typography
Minimal: BBC Sport / Olympics end card (*est.*).

## Colour and light
"Light and shadow to suggest volume", no outlines (*source*: Fx Goby, Nexus). Warm Parisian dusk tones and restrained palettes per shot (*est.* from press still).

## Motion techniques
Hand-drawn 2D, "recreating each illustration 25 times per second" (25 fps on ones, *source*). Hundreds of hours "refining or removing anything unnecessary in compositing, frame by frame" (*source*). Sport-specific body types, posture and kit researched per athlete (*source*).

## Transitions
Love-to-sport match cuts on pose and gesture (an embrace to a grapple, a dip to a dive) are the device (*est.* from "love interrupted" concept); hard cuts on musical accents.

## Sound design and music
Edith Piaf's **"Hymne à l'amour"**, "rearranged for the story arc" (*source*: Nexus); music supervision SIREN; sound design Factory Studios (James Utting) (*source*: Stash credits).

## What makes it world-class
1. Each frame is an "iconic" illustration (Goby's brief): composition holds as a poster.
2. 58 cuts in 60 s yet always legible, because each shot has one clear action and silhouette.
3. Volume through light and shadow only, no outlines: premium, painterly 2D.
4. Edit and music arc together: the rearranged song drives the acceleration.

## What Anikit would need
- `image` sequences for drawn frames, `mask`, hard-cut `transitions`, `audio` with beat markers, `post` grain.
- GAP: drawing the animation itself (frame-by-frame character animation is outside a spec-driven engine).

## Judge's checklist
- Does every shot read in under a second (single silhouette, single action)?
- Are cuts placed on musical phrases/accents rather than at arbitrary times?
- Do transitions rhyme in shape or gesture between consecutive shots?
- Does a paused frame look like a finished illustration (deliberate composition, lighting)?
- Is there a pace arc (slower to faster) across the minute?
