# Nothing :: 2a Film (Nothing Phone (2a) Plus) — Post Office Studios (2024)

**Why it's in the set:** the phone's own surface becomes a monochrome architectural landscape explored by tiny human figures: a scale-contrast device that turns spec features (camera, chip, materials) into places. Strong reference for "product as world" and for Nothing's austere black-and-white brand language.

## The idea
"Transform the surface of the Nothing Phone (2a) Plus into a surreal, otherworldly landscape where the product's key features are discovered and explored," emphasising premium materials, the front camera, and the chipset (*source*: director Prasad Kelkar via Stash). Stash summarises it as "dramatic scale and austere monochrome compositions."

## Structure and pacing
- 65 s (*source*: oEmbed). Feature-by-feature exploration of the device-landscape (*source*).
- *est.* 20–28 shots, median ≈ 2–2.5 s (from length and a feature-tour structure); slow, monumental camera moves rather than snap cuts.

## Typography
Not described in sources. 2D designers and 2D motion designers are credited (*source*), so there are graphic overlays; Nothing's brand system is built on its dot-matrix display face (NDot), so feature callouts are *est.* in a dot-matrix/monospaced face, white on black. Verify when stills are available.

## Colour and light
Thumbnail: strict black/white/grey. The rear camera module rises like a planet/dome over a horizon of machined radial grooves (vinyl-record-like concentric ridges); a single curved white light bar (the phone's Glyph LED) glows upper left with bloom; two silhouetted people stand on a ring-shaped plateau for scale; strong one-point perspective lines of brushed-metal channels run into the frame centre. Light comes from the Glyph itself and a top rim; everything else falls to black.

## Motion techniques
- Architectural-scale camera moves across product geometry (product-as-landscape).
- Human character animation for scale (two 3D character animators credited; *source*).
- Emissive Glyph light used as a motivated key light with bloom.
- Render on Fox Render Farm; VFX/compositing by Stocknock Studios (*source*).

## Transitions
Not documented; *est.* camera travel and cuts between "regions" of the device.

## Sound design and music
Music composition & mix by Dale Curtis (*source*). Character not documented.

## What makes it world-class
1. Scale contrast: tiny humans make a camera module read as a monument; the product is the environment.
2. Monochrome discipline consistent with the brand; the only "colour" is white light.
3. The product's own light (Glyph) is the motivated key light, so light is part of the product story.
4. Strong one-point perspective compositions using the product's real machined details (radial grooves, channels).
5. Each feature gets a "place" in the world rather than a callout over a turntable.

## What Anikit would need
- `scene3d` with emissive materials driving light, `glow`/bloom from real highlights, long lens and wide-angle `camera` moves, `motionBlur`, `post` mono grade.
- `text` callouts in a dot-matrix face (font asset) if used.
- GAP: product-as-landscape hard-surface modelling at architectural scale (procedural radial grooves, brushed metal).
- GAP: animated human figures for scale.

## Judge's checklist
- Is there a clear scale cue (humans, known objects) that turns the product into a place?
- Is the palette strictly monochrome with light (not hue) carrying emphasis?
- Is the main light source motivated by the product itself (an emissive part)?
- Do compositions use strong perspective lines built from real product geometry?
- Does each feature get its own distinct, legible composition?
