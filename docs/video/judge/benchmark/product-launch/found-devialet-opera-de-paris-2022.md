# Devialet, Opéra De Paris — Found (2022)

**Why it's in the set:** a fast, choreographed three-product reveal where a clockwork music box carries each speaker through a set built from the Paris Opera's palette (red velvet, gold, ivory); benchmark for luxury product choreography and set design that comes from the brand's story.

## The idea
Devialet's Opéra de Paris speaker range (gold-finished Phantom speakers). Found "showcased their distinctive Opéra de Paris speaker range in a stylised, mechanical space inspired by the opera hall's iconic colour scheme"; as a nod to orchestral roots the speaker is revealed from a custom music box activated by a bespoke wind-up key and transported through the environment by a clockwork system; the box carries glimpses of the Devialet brand (*source*: Found via Stash). "The range's three speakers are revealed through a choreography of rotations that gradually introduce each speaker and its key features" (*source*: Vimeo description).

## Structure and pacing
- 30 s (*source*: oEmbed); the studio calls its films "fast-paced" and made them for outdoor through digital placements (*source*).
- Wind-up key → music box opens → clockwork transport → three speakers introduced one at a time by rotations → group hero (thumbnail shows the three on plinths) (*source* + thumbnail).
- *est.* 14–18 shots, median ≈ 1.5–2 s.

## Typography
The thumbnail carries the studio's own FOUND card (a high-contrast stencil-like didone, white, wide-tracked) over the hero; this is a studio title, not the film's. In-film brand type is described only as "glimpses of the Devialet brand" on the music box (*source*). Treat type as minimal.

## Colour and light
Thumbnail (hero frame): three white Phantom speakers with gold side panels on ivory/beige textured plinths with gold trim and arched cut-outs; behind, a deep opera red (≈ #8E0A0A *est.*) wall and an arched screen of thin vertical gold-and-red rods, an ivory disc like a halo behind the centre speaker. Soft, warm key from front-left, gold specular highlights, gentle shadow under each speaker; palette locked to red/gold/ivory.

## Motion techniques
- Mechanical choreography: wind-up key, clockwork transport, rotations that present each speaker's features (*source*).
- Rigid, precise motion with eased stops (luxury-product "presentation" timing) (*est.* from "choreography of rotations").

## Transitions
Not documented; *est.* match moves on rotations and cuts on mechanical beats.

## Sound design and music
Audio by Louis Lacoste (*source*). Not described further.

## What makes it world-class
1. The set and palette come from the product's story (Opera Garnier red/gold), not a generic studio cyc.
2. A mechanical device (music box, key, clockwork) motivates every movement of the product.
3. Each of three products gets a feature moment, then a group hero composed with a clear centre of interest (taller centre plinth + halo disc).
4. Materials read as luxury at full size: gold anodised metal, textured ivory, red velvet-like walls.
5. Fast but legible pacing suited to short placements.

## What Anikit would need
- `scene3d` with PBR gold/metal and textured cloth/stone materials, warm key light, shallow `camera` DOF, `motionBlur`; `post` warm grade; `audio` with mechanical sfx (`tick`, `click`) synced to rotations.
- GAP: mechanical rigs (gear trains, key-driven constraints) to drive product motion procedurally.

## Judge's checklist
- Does the set palette derive from the brand/product story rather than a default backdrop?
- Is every product movement motivated by a visible mechanism or physical cause?
- Does each product get its own feature beat before a composed group hero?
- Do metal finishes show real speculars and reflections at 100% crop?
- Are rotations eased to precise, weighty stops (no float or overshoot wobble)?
