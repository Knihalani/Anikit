# OPPO | Reno6 Jin Series — Tendril (2021)

**Why it's in the set:** a monochrome, macro-scale phone launch where the phone's finish is explained by a natural phenomenon (snow and crystals), with a near-empty dark environment that keeps all attention on material and light. Companion film (GenZ series, aurora concept) at https://vimeo.com/647785908.

## The idea
"For the Jin series, we looked to nature for inspiration. The idea of snowflakes, crystals, and snow forming the phone's surface was the key concept for our visuals. The environment was kept minimal to maintain the focus on the premium phones." (*source*: Tendril via Stash and the director's portfolio). The GenZ film's base design idea is an aurora: a multi-layered surface reflecting many colours from one light source (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 31 s (*source*: oEmbed). Upload frame ≈ 1.89:1 (426×226 oEmbed, 1280×675 thumbnail; *source*), i.e. a wide cinema crop, not 16:9.
- *est.* 10–14 shots, median ≈ 2–2.5 s (from length and the macro, contemplative stills): steady, not frantic.
- Arc *est.* from stills: macro crystal/snow details → phone silhouette forming in snow → product hero.

## Typography
Not documented. Treat any feature callouts/end card as minimal, small, white sans; type is not part of the concept.

## Colour and light
- Jin: pure monochrome. Thumbnail: a single dendritic snowflake (fern-like six-arm crystal) in sharp focus against near-black, with a soft grey dune-like surface across the bottom and out-of-focus specks floating in the air.
- Portfolio still: a phone outline embossed into a field of black, glittering granular snow, lit by a low raking key from upper left so each grain sparkles; the phone reads only through its edge relief.
- Lighting is single-source, raking, high-contrast; blacks are deep but carry texture.
- A second portfolio still (likely GenZ) shows layered translucent crystal "cells" with cyan/teal cores and a prismatic light streak across a grey background: the aurora idea expressed as iridescence from one light.

## Motion techniques
- Macro camera with very shallow depth of field; drifting airborne particles for scale.
- Particle/granular simulation (FX artists credited) forming the phone's surface (*source*: concept + still).
- Toolkit: Cinema 4D, Redshift, Houdini (*source*: Stash).

## Transitions
Not documented; *est.* slow cuts and focus pulls between macro details.

## Sound design and music
Music and sound design by Echoic Audio (*source*). No further detail published.

## What makes it world-class
1. A natural-phenomenon metaphor that maps one-to-one onto the product's finish (snow/crystal texture → frosted phone back).
2. Single raking key light; texture is revealed by light direction, not by colour.
3. Strict monochrome palette; no accent colour is needed to read as premium.
4. Product revealed through relief/silhouette in a material (phone outline in snow) before the clean hero.
5. Macro scale with shallow DOF and airborne specks gives believable physical scale.

## What Anikit would need
- `scene3d` with a single area/spot light at a grazing angle, shallow `camera` aperture, `motionBlur`, `post` mono grade with lifted-texture blacks; `component` "snow" for airborne specks.
- GAP: procedural dendritic crystal growth.
- GAP: granular particles settling on and outlining a product mesh.

## Judge's checklist
- Is there one light, at a grazing angle, that reveals surface texture?
- Is the palette strictly limited (mono, or one accent) and deliberate?
- Does the metaphor (snow/crystal) explain a real property of the product?
- Do macro shots show physical scale cues (DOF falloff, floating specks, micro-sparkle)?
- Are blacks deep but textured (not crushed to flat #000)?
