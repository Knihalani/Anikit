# Last I Heard (…He Was Circling The Drain) — Saad Moosajee / Art Camp (2019)

**Why it's in the set: 3D crowd simulation and camera work given an entirely hand-made, charcoal-and-powder surface, frame by frame. It is the bar for "CG that doesn't look like CG".**

## The idea
A lone figure (Thom Yorke) moves through a "hazy dystopian world" of anonymous crowds, from fragments of Yorke's imagination (*source*: Colossal; D&AD).

## Structure and pacing
- 308 s (*source*: YouTube listing, "5 minutes, 8 seconds").
- More than 3,000 individual hand-treated frames (*source*: Colossal; D&AD), i.e. most of the film at about 10–12 unique frames per second (*est.*: 3,000 / 308 s ≈ 9.7 drawn frames per second, so animated on twos or threes).
- Sections move between crowd scenes, city spaces and fire (*source*: techniques list); shots of 3–8 s with continuous camera moves (*est.*).

## Typography
None.

## Colour and light
Black and white (*source*: D&AD), with charcoal washes, grain and powder textures; deep blacks and smoky mid-greys; the light seems to come from within the haze (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
- 3D animation, crowd simulation, fire simulation (*source*), with cel animation for characters and stop-motion photography of powder for texture (*source*: Cartoon Brew credits; Colossal).
- Every frame is "hand-treated" with charcoal washing (*source*), so the surface boils.
- A 16 mm film consultant is credited (*source*), suggesting a film-grain and print look.

## Transitions
Cuts and dissolves through smoke, charcoal and crowds; masses of figures fill the frame and part to reveal the next space (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Thom Yorke's "Last I Heard" from *Anima*: a pulsing electronic bass figure under falsetto vocals, with a nervous, rising tension. The crowd motion is timed to the pulse (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Every frame has a physical, hand-made surface (charcoal, powder, grain) over a mathematically correct 3D structure: the camera and perspective are precise, the texture is not.
2. Monochrome discipline: only tone, contrast and texture; no colour to fall back on.
3. Crowds of thousands with real flocking behaviour make the theme (isolation inside masses) literal.
4. The surface "boils" frame to frame at a hand-made rate (about 10 drawn frames per second), not at a digital 24/30 fps.
5. Haze and fire simulation give depth and atmosphere in a grayscale world.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d` with many instanced figures, `camera` moves, a `shader` pass for charcoal and powder textures with per-frame variation, `post` (grain, contrast, halation). GAPs: crowd simulation (agents, flocking); hand-made per-frame texture treatment of 3D renders; fire and smoke simulation; "on twos" frame-holding for the whole render.

## Judge's checklist
- Is the image monochrome with deep, clean blacks and no banding in the smoky gradients?
- Does the surface texture change every drawn frame (boil), while the geometry and camera stay stable?
- Do crowds move as believable groups (no clones in lockstep, no intersections)?
- Is there atmospheric depth (haze, layered planes) in every shot?
- Is the effective drawing rate deliberately lower than the video frame rate?
