# Cash In Cash Out — François Rousselet / Division / ETC (2022)

**Why it's in the set: a fully CG music video that convincingly pretends to be a physical zoetrope, so a single conceit (a spinning carousel of miniatures) carries four minutes.**

## The idea
Pharrell, 21 Savage and Tyler, the Creator perform as miniature figures on "a gigantic digital zoetrope" (*source*: shots.net via search; Its Nice That). The intent was for the CG to be photographic, so that "the viewer question[s] if this was stop-motion or CGI" (*source*: Annecy listing via search). It took about two years from concept (*source*: Promonews; iHeart says "a year and some change").

## Structure and pacing
- 236 s (*source*: YouTube page `lengthSeconds`).
- Set on a looping carousel with rotating cash floors, model train tracks, and a face-swap scene with a hula-hooping Tyler (*source*: Its Nice That). Verses change the scene on the zoetrope per artist (*est.*).
- Visible rhythm comes from the rotation and the strobing of the poses rather than from cuts; the camera cuts between macro angles on the rotating platter about every 1–2 s (*est.*, from the hq stills and the genre).

## Typography
None in the body of the video (*est.*).

## Colour and light
Dramatic blue and red lighting, plus green, neon and orange-teal tones (*source*: Its Nice That captions). Miniature-photography look: shallow depth of field, tiny dust in the air (*source*).

## Motion techniques
- Every character and object is a CG model built at miniature scale (*source*).
- Motion references the roughly 12 fps "stop-motion" feel of real zoetropes, with an added organic jitter and staccato poses (*source*).
- Materials: plastic, like 3D-printed figures, with low-resolution detail at close range; clay-like clothing; fingerprints; scuffs on the zoetrope; Hot Wheels-style diecast cars (*source*).
- Lip sync copies each performer's mouth movements and mannerisms, scaled to miniature (*source*).

## Transitions
Mostly hard cuts between macro camera angles, plus continuous rotation that carries new figures into frame (the zoetrope is its own transition) (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Pharrell's sparse, bass-heavy beat; verses by 21 Savage and Tyler. The figures' poses step on the rhythm (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. A single physical conceit (the zoetrope) explains every motion artefact: the stepped poses, jitter and strobing are motivated, not stylistic noise.
2. Material honesty at macro scale: layer lines, fingerprints, scuffs and dust sell the miniature; nothing looks like default CG plastic.
3. The lens behaves like a miniature camera: shallow depth of field and close focus, with highlights that bloom from real light sources.
4. Each artist gets a recognisable, caricatured likeness and performance that still reads at toy scale.
5. Coloured stage lighting (red/blue, neon) changes per section, which gives the loop progression.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d` with PBR materials, `camera` with `depth`/`aperture` for macro DOF, `post` (bloom, grain), `motionBlur` off for the strobe look, and `repeat` for instanced figures around a ring. GAPs: zoetrope sampling (poses that advance in steps tied to rotation, with a shutter); miniature material library (3D-print striations, fingerprints, clay cloth); lip-sync and likeness for characters.

## Judge's checklist
- Is there a single physical conceit that explains how everything moves?
- Do materials hold up at macro distance with real surface detail (scratches, prints, dust), not clean default shaders?
- Is the stepped or jittered motion consistent and motivated, rather than accidental judder?
- Does the depth of field and light falloff convincingly read as a tiny scale?
- Does lighting or colour progress between sections so a loop doesn't feel static?
