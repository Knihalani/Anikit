# Sign O' The Times — Bill Konersman for Prince (1987)

**Why it's in the set (classic): it is widely cited as the first lyric video. The artist never appears; the words, a few symbols and colour carry the whole song. It is a benchmark for the idea and the timing, not for polish.**

## The idea
Prince chose to show only the lyrics. Director Bill Konersman: it "was just an aspect of his creativity" (*source*: Grammy.com). Grammy.com calls it "widely considered the first-ever lyric video"; Prince does not appear (*source*). Bob Dylan's 1965 "Subterranean Homesick Blues" cue cards were a more limited precedent (*source*).

## Structure and pacing
- 224 s (*source*: YouTube listing, "3 minutes, 44 seconds").
- Every lyric line appears on screen in sync with the vocal; phrases change on the vocal line, roughly every 2–4 s (*est.*, from the song's phrasing).
- Instrumental breaks are filled with graphic symbols rather than text (*est.*; PrinceVault describes "colored graphics that present the song's words", via search summary).

## Typography
- "Times Roman was the only font available": the team traced a blown-up Photostat from a type book with a mouse (*source*: Grammy.com, Konersman).
- Konersman, a Macintosh-literate graphic designer, calls the graphics "lamentably crude", made "before true 3-D graphics" (*source*).
- Key words are swapped for symbols (the "O" of the title, hearts, peace-sign-like marks) in Prince's rebus style (*est.*, from Prince's period lettering; check against frames).

## Colour and light
Saturated flat colour fields behind and in the type (red, blue, yellow, pink on black, *est.*), changing per section.

## Motion techniques
Words pop, slide and scale on in 1987 video-graphics style; simple 2D moves timed to the vocal (*est.*).

## Transitions
Hard cuts and colour swaps between lines (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Prince's sparse drum-machine (LinnDrum) and bass track with a socially conscious lyric (*est.*, well-documented production). The video's only content is the lyric, so sync is everything.

## What makes it world-class
1. A conceptual bet: the words are the whole picture, which makes a protest song's lyric impossible to miss.
2. Lyric-to-vocal sync: each line is on screen when it is sung, so a viewer can read along.
3. Rebus substitutions (symbols for words) add personality inside a strict text-only format.
4. As a benchmark, it marks the floor: a modern lyric video must keep this clarity and sync while adding typographic craft the 1987 tools couldn't.

## What Anikit would need
`text` with `kinetic` presets and per-word timing, `captions` timing from the vocal, `shape` symbols, `transitions`, `audio`. No GAP for the basic form; Anikit should exceed it on typesetting and motion.

## Judge's checklist
- Is every lyric line on screen while it is sung (no early or late lines)?
- Is each line held long enough to read?
- Does the type treatment add meaning (emphasis, substitution) rather than decoration?
- Is the typesetting better than the 1987 baseline (real typeface choice, spacing, hierarchy)?
