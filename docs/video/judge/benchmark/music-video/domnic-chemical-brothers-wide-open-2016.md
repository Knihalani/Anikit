# Wide Open — Dom&Nic / The Mill (2016)

**Why it's in the set: a single, unbroken take in which a dancer is slowly replaced, limb by limb, by a 3D-printed-looking lattice of herself. The VFX is invisible as craft and devastating as an idea.**

## The idea
Dancer Sonoya Mizuno performs Wayne McGregor's choreography in an empty space; "Mizuno gradually transforms limb by limb as she moves through the space" until she is an open mesh figure with a visible beating heart (*source*: Wayne McGregor production page; Station Film).

## Structure and pacing
- 331 s (*source*: YouTube page `lengthSeconds`); "5 minutes" (*source*: McGregor page).
- One continuous shot (*source*). Pace comes from the slow spread of the transformation over the whole song, not from cuts: about one new body part transformed every 15–30 s (*est.*, from the length and the number of limbs).
- In the final section, "Sonoya and her clothing are entirely rendered in 3D" (*source*: The Mill, via search summary).

## Typography
None.

## Colour and light
Soft, flat daylight from large windows in a former cab workshop in Bethnal Green, London (*source*: location); a pale, whitewashed, desaturated room with grey concrete tones (*est.*, from the stills). The mesh body is white or grey like a 3D print (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
- Lidar scan of the entire set for accurate camera tracking (*source*).
- A full CG model of the dancer with 107 anatomic rigs and bespoke tools for a seamless transformation (*source*: The Mill press release via search).
- McGregor gave animation notes on "the weight of the head, foot planting, and even the subtle shapes of her fingers" (*source*), so the CG matches the dancer's weight exactly.
- Slow, gliding handheld or Steadicam camera circling the dancer (*est.*).

## Transitions
None: it is one take; the transformation itself is the only transition.

## Sound design and music
The Chemical Brothers with Beck: a mid-tempo, melancholic track with a long build. Movement phrases follow the song's swells (*est.*).

## What makes it world-class
1. A single take with no cuts to hide in: tracking, match-move and the replacement must be perfect for five minutes, and they are.
2. The CG body has exactly the dancer's weight; feet plant, the head carries mass, fingers hold their shape.
3. The material idea (an open lattice that shows the space through the body, and a heart beating inside) is legible and emotional.
4. The escalation is paced to the whole song, which makes the slow spread of the effect the structure of the film.
5. Restraint in the setting: a plain room and flat light so all attention goes to the body.

## What Anikit would need
`video` plate, `scene3d` body with a lattice material, `matte` to hand off from live to CG, `camera` match to the plate, `post`. GAPs: match-moved digital doubles replacing live action; lattice or Voronoi shell material; lidar and camera solve. Practically out of scope for Anikit as a renderer; it is in the set as a bar for invisible VFX and long-take structure.

## Judge's checklist
- Does a single transformation idea structure the whole piece rather than repeating?
- Is CG motion indistinguishable in weight and contact from the live performance (no sliding feet, no floaty heads)?
- Does the camera stay continuous without cheating cuts where the effect is hard?
- Does the setting stay simple so the subject reads at all times?
- Does the effect escalate in step with the music?
