# feelslikeimfallinginlove — Raman Djafari / Blinkink (2024)

**Why it's in the set: 15 animation styles in a single unbroken journey. Each hand-off is a designed transition, so a relay of very different techniques reads as one film.**

## The idea
"Two friends, lovers, soulmates" travel between worlds and change form; Djafari "wanted this journey to feel like a dream", surreal in image but familiar in emotion (*source*: Stash).

## Structure and pacing
- 261 s (*source*: YouTube listing, "4 minutes, 21 seconds").
- Three chapters: Longing, Searching, Finding (*source*). Fifteen styles by 15 animators, in order of appearance: Tokay, Dante Zaballa, Martin Robic, KINEMUS, Jack Zhang, Niki Lindroth von Bahr, Moera.cf, Gaia Esther Maria, Aaron Fisher, Karlotta Freier, Raman Djafari, Isabel Garrett, Hannah van der Weide, Kohana Wilson, Ram Han (*source*).
- About 15 segments of about 15–18 s each (*est.*: 261 s / 15).
- "Every transition was to bring us to a new world"; each transition carries the emotion of the previous sequence forward (*source*).

## Typography
None.

## Colour and light
Varies per animator; graded together by colourist Andi Chu (*source*) so that the warm, saturated and pastel palettes flow (*est.*). Each world has its own palette; the two characters stay identifiable throughout (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
Stop-motion, 2D, 3D, oil pastels, watercolour and felt (*source*). Isabel Garrett's segment is stop-motion shot in Dragonframe (*source*: Dragonframe blog title). The hand-drawn segments boil; the stop-motion ones have real material texture (*est.*).

## Transitions
Every scene is "linked to the next by an intricately animated transition" (*source*): forms morph from one medium into the next, the characters pass through a portal or object into a new world (*est.* for the specific devices).

## Sound design and music
Coldplay's track (pop, rising chorus); additional sound design by Ultraherz (Christoffer Jarlbak Overgaard) (*source*).

## What makes it world-class
1. Radical variety held together: very different media, but one pair of characters, one emotional arc and continuous transitions.
2. The transitions are designed moments, not crossfades; each one changes medium in motion.
3. Craft in each segment is festival-level (several of the animators are known auteurs, e.g. Niki Lindroth von Bahr).
4. A three-act emotional structure (Longing, Searching, Finding) maps onto the song.
5. A unified grade keeps different media from feeling like a compilation.

## What Anikit would need
`image`/`video` layers for pre-made segments, `matte` and `shape` morph transitions, `camera` moves through portals, `post` grade. GAPs: frame-by-frame drawn animation in hand media; medium-to-medium morph transitions (e.g., watercolour to felt) are beyond current transitions.

## Judge's checklist
- Do the characters stay recognisable across every change of style?
- Is every change of world a designed transition in motion, not a cut or a crossfade?
- Is there a clear emotional arc (setup, search, resolution) across sections?
- Do mixed media feel unified by grade and rhythm?
- Is each segment's craft complete (no placeholder-looking sections)?
