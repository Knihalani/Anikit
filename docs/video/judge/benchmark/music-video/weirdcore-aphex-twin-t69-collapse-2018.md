# T69 Collapse — Weirdcore (2018)

**Why it's in the set: the reference for generative, beat-locked motion design. Photogrammetry scans of real streets fold, melt and explode in time with an irregular, tempo-shifting track.**

## The idea
Inspired by "how an AI might see the world on psychedelic drugs" and "how a simulation could break and go to next level" (*source*: press release quoted by Dazed).

## Structure and pacing
- 319 s (*source*: YouTube listing, "5 minutes, 19 seconds").
- Four parts (*source*: Dazed): about 25 s of a nearly illegible text dialogue; a glitchy high street rising into a mountainous plain; a wormhole transition at about 3:15 into Gwennap Pit (Redruth, Cornwall); an escalating CG sequence ending in a "neon assault".
- Changes happen several times a second in the dense parts, with edits and deformations synced to individual drum hits (*est.*).
- It failed the Harding photosensitivity test and was dropped from its Adult Swim TV slot (*source*).

## Typography
ASCII and monospaced terminal text, mostly drawn from the opening dialogue, mapped onto surfaces and floating through the scene (*source*). Text is textural rather than for reading.

## Colour and light
Photographic textures of grey Cornish streets corrupted into neon magentas, greens and cyans; flashing full-frame colour swaps in the climax (*source*: "flashing colours", "neon assault"; *est.* for hues).

## Motion techniques
- Photogrammetry scans of Cornish locations (Redruth viaduct, Falmouth Road, Penryn High Street) combined into one impossible street (*source*).
- Point clouds and text textured with After Effects UV and position passes (*source*: Geo Week via search).
- Neural style transfer composites (*source*: Weirdcore quoted in search results; not in the Dazed article).
- Geometry that stretches, extrudes and folds on the beat; a camera that flies through and around scans (*est.*).

## Transitions
In-camera: the scan itself tears, folds or tunnels (the "wormhole") into the next location (*source* for the wormhole). Flash frames in the climax (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Aphex Twin's "T69 Collapse": broken beats with frequent tempo changes ("the bpm … changes about 150 times" in the second collapse, *source*: a student analysis via search, weak).

## What makes it world-class
1. Hit-by-hit sync to a highly irregular rhythm: deformations land on individual percussion events, not on a regular grid.
2. Real-world capture as raw material: scanned streets give photographic detail that the deformation then breaks.
3. A single continuous camera journey through transforming spaces rather than cuts.
4. Escalation: from legible street to total abstraction over the length of the track.
5. Text used as a texture of the world, not as captions.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d`, `cloud` and `splat` for point-cloud rendering, `shader` for deformation and colour corruption, `camera` fly-throughs, `audio` beat detection to drive parameters, `text` for ASCII textures, `post`. GAPs: importing photogrammetry or point-cloud captures; a style-transfer pass. Note the photosensitivity issue: Anikit output should pass a flash check.

## Judge's checklist
- Do visual events land on individual sound events (including off-grid ones), not just on bar lines?
- Does the piece escalate from legible to abstract with a clear arc?
- Does the camera travel continuously through transforming space?
- Is the raw material (scans, photos) rich enough that the deformation reveals detail rather than noise?
- Does it stay below photosensitivity flash limits (an issue the original failed)?
