# Burn The Witch — Chris Hopewell (2016)

**Why it's in the set: a cosy 1960s children's-TV style (Trumptonshire) turned into folk horror. The gap between the look and what happens is the whole idea, and the pastiche is exact.**

## The idea
An inspector with a clipboard visits a toy village that looks "cute and innocent" but hides a sadistic side (*source*: It's Nice That), retelling *The Wicker Man* (1973) in the style of the Trumptonshire trilogy (*Camberwick Green*, *Trumpton*) (*source*: Time Out; Promonews via search).

## Structure and pacing
- 240 s (*source*: YouTube listing, "4 minutes").
- A linear tour: arrival, greeting by the mayor-like leader, a series of sinister vignettes (a red cross painted on a door, a girl on a ducking stool, a girl tied to a tree under six masked swordsmen, a bleeding carcass outside a bakery, a noose on the maypole, empty apple crates), then the wicker man (*source*: Time Out).
- Shots about 3–5 s, mostly locked-off or gentle pans, like the original children's TV (*est.*).

## Typography
None in the body.

## Colour and light
Bright, saturated toy-town primaries and soft, even studio light, as in 1960s British children's TV (*est.*, from stills). The cheerfulness of the light never changes, even at the burning (*est.*).

## Motion techniques
Stop-motion puppets with simple, limited articulation imitating the Gordon Murray originals (*source*: homage); the puppets and props were reportedly made quickly (production in about two weeks reported in coverage, *source* uncertain, not confirmed in the pages read).

## Transitions
Plain hard cuts in the manner of the original series (*est.*).

## Sound design and music
Radiohead's "Burn the Witch": col legno strings (struck with the wood of the bow) by the London Contemporary Orchestra, building to a dissonant climax (*est.*, from the record's well-known arrangement). The calm puppet pace runs against the urgent strings.

## What makes it world-class
1. Concept through pastiche: the medium (beloved, innocent children's TV) is what makes the content (mob violence, scapegoating) disturbing.
2. The pastiche is exact: proportions, limited articulation, set dressing and framing faithfully match the source.
3. Every vignette is a specific, readable sign of menace, so the dread accumulates.
4. Tonal contrast with the music: the gentle pace against driving strings.

## What Anikit would need
`scene3d` with toy-like materials, locked-off `camera` framing, stepped motion. GAPs: a stop-motion puppet look (stepped poses on twos, felt, wood and paint materials, miniature lighting).

## Judge's checklist
- Does the chosen style carry meaning (here the contrast between innocent form and dark content)?
- Is the pastiche faithful in detail (proportions, motion limits, framing) rather than generic "cute"?
- Is every scene a readable story beat that escalates?
- Does the visual pacing deliberately play against the music?
