# Finding the film's idea

A launch film fails when it looks like the last one, or like any other product's. The idea has to come from
*this* product. This page is a method, not a menu.

## Method

1. **Nouns and verbs.** What physical thing is the product most like? What does it do to the user's problem?
   (sorts, shields, compresses, connects, reveals, accelerates, remembers …)
2. **Before → after.** The problem as an image, and the same image resolved. This is usually the turn.
3. **One motif.** A single object, shape, colour, light or move that appears throughout the film and resolves at the
   end (often into the logo or its underline).
4. **The world.** Where does this happen? Build it from the brief's own nouns, using whatever Anikit can render
   (ray-traced sets, simulation, particles, shader worlds, type as architecture, the product's UI as material).
5. **Palette and type.** Use the brand's own if given. Otherwise derive them from the world, with one accent kept
   for the moments that matter.
6. **Record it** in `concept.txt` (noun, verb, before → after, motif, world, tempo and why), so the next video can
   be different.

Test: if you swap in another product's name and the idea still works, it is too generic. Push further.

## Examples (to show the range, not options to choose from)

- *An email triage app* (`docs/video/skills/launch-examples/sift`): letters as physical tiles in a storm; the turn
  is the storm frozen, then everything in one sorted line. A single red tile is the motif.
- *An offline trail-map app* (`launch-examples/fernway`): the terrain *is* the map; an amber trail of light draws
  itself through fog and becomes the path to the name.
- *A password manager* (illustrative): one continuous dolly through a vault made of the user's own forgotten
  passwords, each one dissolving as it is stored, until only a single key remains.
- *A video editor* (illustrative): a reverse reveal that starts on the finished film's last frame and rewinds
  through the timeline that made it.
- *A database* (illustrative): no words at all. A million particles stream into ordered structures at the beat,
  then form the name.
