# Built to be shared (steps 1, 5 and 9)

Nothing guarantees virality: seeding, timing, endorsers and the product itself are outside the film. What a film *can* control is measurable, and we hold every launch film to it. Evidence and sources: `docs/video/craft/launch-viral.md` (viral launches across 20 years, platform data) and `launch-ai-viral.md` (40+ AI and software launches; a 194-video study of startup launches on X: hook, differentiation and pace predicted views; polish didn't; title-card openings did worst).

## In `1-promise.md`

1. **The shareable moment:** one event in ≤ 15 words where something visibly *happens*, not an adjective. For example, "a phone falls through a glass tower and lands unscathed", or "one line of text becomes a finished film". Test: a stranger shown only those 2 s would write that sentence back.
2. **Proof inside the spectacle:** the moment demonstrates a true product property, such as precision, speed, durability, scale, or what it produces. Delete the product and the moment stops making sense.
3. **Target emotion:** awe (impossible scale, precision, transformation) or amusement. Never sadness, and never contempt for something the audience loves (Apple "Crush").
4. **Send test:** name who would DM this, to whom, and why it makes the sender look good (first to know, insider, funny). If you can't, it optimises likes, not sends.
5. **Hostile retell:** write the cruelest one-line description a critic could post ("a CG marching band", "a person in a VR headset", "a car ad with no car"). If it's damning, change the film.
6. **Remixable unit (optional):** one format line, sound or visual gag others could redo with their own subject.

## In `4-beats.md`

7. **Frame 0 is a poster:** the product, the output or the anomaly, already moving by frame 2. No black, fade-in, logo sting or title card. At 25% size it still intrigues.
8. **Timing:** in cuts ≤ 15 s the moment or the product is on screen by 3 s. In longer cuts a first version lands by 3 s and the full payoff by 40% of the runtime.
9. **"How did they do that?":** one shot that is plausible in look and impossible in event, and that's earned by the product's truth. Never fake reality to get it; a designed impossibility (type, diagram, product object) works better.
10. **Mute test:** with the sound off, a viewer can state the moment and the product name. Key text is up by 2 s, ≥ 4% of frame height, inside the safe area.
11. **Sound earns the unmute:** the moment has a synced sound event (impact, drop, silence then hit). Energetic cuts run ≥ 120 BPM. No speech is needed to understand it.
12. **Loop** (vertical cuts): the last 0.5 s matches the first frame's composition, motion and sound, so a replay is seamless. The end card is ≤ 1.5 s or overlaid on the payoff.

## In `6-ship.md`

13. **Native cuts:** 9:16, 1:1 and 16:9 composed separately (`modes.md` table).
14. **Post copy:** a first line that states the outcome or the moment (no "Introducing", no "We're excited to announce"); one explicit ask (try it, link, waitlist); the real URL or handle. No "comment YES" or "tag a friend" bait.
15. **Honesty floor:** nothing shown as the product doing what it can't do today. Staged or designed events are impossible on their face, or captioned.
