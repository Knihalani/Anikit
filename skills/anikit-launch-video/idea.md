# Finding the idea (steps 1–2)

The idea is where launch films are won or lost. In blind tests, rebuilding the idea and structure moved scores by +1 to +3; polish, fonts and fidelity moved them by about 0. Our best launch film so far, judged professional, was built this way.

## 1. The promise

- **The unownable truth:** find the one thing this product does that its competitors *can't* honestly claim. Not "fast", "simple" or "powerful". For example, "the same spec gives the same film, byte for byte".
- **The promise:** write the benefit the audience should believe afterwards, as ≤ 15 words without the product's name.
- **Proof or teaser?**
  - **Proof:** show the real product working early (by 5 s), as the cause of a change. It suits tools, apps and anything new to the viewer.
  - **Teaser:** withhold the product and show its effect; the reveal is the payoff. It suits hardware, brands and sequels.
- **Not in this film:** the brief's other facts. Listing them as beats turns the film into an effect demo; the jury's words for one such film were "unrelated effect demos".

## 2. Ideas: find the analogous craft

The strongest method we've measured: **find a human practice, outside tech, that already does what the product does.** Then set the film inside that practice's world.

- **A worked example, and its warning:** for Anikit (deterministic films from one page), an agent found the marching band's drill chart. The drill writer's sets are keyframes, the counts are frames, and 144 players hit every dot every night. The concept was strong and the blind judge passed it. But it was rendered as a CG simulation of a real band on a real field, and the user rejected it as "horrid, so artificial and weird". **Carry the analogy as design, not as a fake photograph:** the drill chart itself, type, diagram, UI, print or graphic abstraction.
- **The questions to ask:**
  - Who else turns a plan into a performance?
  - Who else makes the same thing perfectly every time?
  - Who else carries something from A to B instantly?
  - Who else listens to soil, or keeps an object for a hundred years?

  Look for crafts, rituals, trades, sports, ceremonies, natural phenomena and games.
- **Mine the craft for insider detail** (from `refs/world.md`): its tools, jargon, rituals and the moments only insiders know ("8 to 5", spray dots on the practice field, the silence after the hit). Those details make the film specific and shareable.

Write ≥ 4 ideas, each from a **different family** (a different craft or world, not variations), one deliberately out there. Other good sources:

- **What visibly changes because the product exists?** Mess → order, heavy → weightless, wait → instant, noise → silence. The product is the *cause*, not a turntable object.
- **A structural device:** one continuous move, a reverse reveal, one object transforming, a loop that returns changed, a scale journey, type as the set.

## The gates (fail = discard the idea)

- **Literal test:** describe the idea with the product's name and its feature mechanism deleted.
  - If nothing is left, or what's left is the product's name drawn as a picture or its mechanism diagrammed, it's literal.
  - Real failures: Railyard drawn as rails; a split-flap board for a payments API ("a literal pun"); a film showing its own JSON; a waveform turned into mountains.
- **Swap test:** put a competitor's name on it. If the film still works, it's generic.
- **Renderable test:**
  - **Fail:** a world that only works as photoreal close-ups of organic or complex things (faces, food, animals, products in macro). Our 3D reads as "placeholder modelling" there.
  - **Fail:** any simulation of a real-world scene with people, crowds, places, nature or food. Even top-down and stylised, it reads as artificial and game-like (BAR.md, fifth rejection).
  - **Pass:**
    - graphic, print and illustrated worlds;
    - diagrams, maps and charts (drawn, not simulated);
    - the real product: its UI, its object, its own surfaces;
    - type as material;
    - abstract shader and simulation worlds that never pretend to be a real place.
- **Fresh test:** check the last films in `deliverables/launch*/`, `deliverables/demo/` and `docs/video/quality/BAR.md`'s rejected looks. If this world, palette or medium matches one, choose another family.
- **Viral test** (see `viral.md`): can you describe the one shareable moment in a sentence a viewer would repeat to a friend?

## Pick

Choose the idea whose device can **escalate**, so each beat is bigger, stranger or more consequential, and that ends in the name. Write in `2-ideas.md`:
- the device in one sentence;
- the insider details it uses;
- the shareable moment;
- how it escalates across 4–6 beats;
- how it becomes the ending;
- why it beat the others.

Keep the rejects for the next film.
