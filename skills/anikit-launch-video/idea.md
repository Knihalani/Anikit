# Finding the idea (steps 1–2)

The idea is where launch films are won or lost. In our blind tests, rebuilding the idea and structure moved scores by +1 to +2; polish, fonts and fidelity moved them by about 0.

## 1. The promise

- **One sentence, ≤ 15 words, no product name:** the one thing the audience should believe afterwards. For example, "Your money arrives the moment you send it." It isn't "fast, secure, global payments API with webhooks".
- **Proof or teaser?**
  - **Proof:** show the real product working early (by 5 s), as the cause of a change. It suits tools, apps and anything new to the viewer.
  - **Teaser:** withhold the product and show its effect; the reveal is the payoff. It suits hardware, brands and sequels.
- **Not in this film:** the brief's other facts. Listing them as beats turns the film into an effect demo; the jury's words for one such film were "unrelated effect demos".

## 2. Four ideas

Write at least four, each built on a different device. Make one deliberately out there. Sources:

- **What visibly changes because the product exists?** Mess → order, heavy → weightless, wait → instant, small → vast, noise → silence. The product is the *cause*, not a turntable object.
- **The subject's world, not the product's mechanism:** the audience's day, place, material or ritual. A payments film about rails, wires or an API diagram is literal. A film about the moment money lands, in a world built from that moment, isn't.
- **A physical metaphor you can render:** a material that behaves like the promise (ice that holds, paper that unfolds, light that arrives before its sound…).
- **A structural device:** one continuous move, a reverse reveal, one object transforming, a loop that returns changed, a scale journey, type as the set.

## The two gates (fail = discard the idea)

- **Literal test:** describe the idea with the product's name and its feature mechanism deleted. If nothing is left, or what's left is "the product's name drawn as a picture", it's literal. Real examples that failed: "Railyard" drawn as rails, a split-flap board for a payments API ("a literal pun"), a film showing its own JSON.
- **Swap test:** put a competitor's name on it. If the film still works, it's generic.

## Pick

Choose the idea whose device can **escalate**, so each beat shows it bigger, stranger or more consequential, and that ends in the name. Write in `2-ideas.md`:
- the device in one sentence;
- how it escalates across 4–6 beats;
- how it becomes the ending;
- why it beat the others.

Keep the rejects: the next film should use one of them, or none.
